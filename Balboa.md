## Definition and Character

Balboa is the maritime principality holding the tip of the Ertrese Peninsula, the Cape, and through it the door between the Western and Eastern Gyres. Its core landholding has never been large -- a cliffside city, a modest hinterland, a scatter of baronies -- but its reach has always outrun its acreage, first by conquest, then by marriage and the shrewd management of a wartime crisis, until a principality that could be walked end to end in a few days found itself, by the close of the Great War, the effective master of most of western Ertra. Balboans are proud of this disproportion rather than affect embarrassment, and prouder still of the age behind it: the city stood since before Reme fell, watched the crusaders' fleet pass them by a trick of current, and has been answering to no one but itself, or to masters of its own careful choosing, ever since. Where Lavion counts its history in the line of kings and Vel Kiriya in the wealth gained from its satrapies, Balboa counts it in centuries of a mercantile-mercenary reputation built as much on knowing when not to fight as on any battle won outright.

## Geography

Balboa occupies the seaward tip of the Ertrese Peninsula, a cliff-bound cape where the Rush brushes the coast before shearing away into the Gyres to either side. The city itself rises from a harbor at the cliff's foot to a fortified height above, with no gentle approach from the water in either direction; what flat, arable ground the principality commands lies inland and behind it, in the hinterland baronies of Caldasci and Maristagna and the lesser city of Rivazziano, absorbed into the settled core in the centuries after the Fall and administered since as the principality's agricultural and manpower base.

The city itself sits in a natural amphitheater of steep coast opening north to the sea, its harbor -- called simply, in everyday Balboan usage, the Outer Basin for the commercial waters and the Inner Basin for the walled cothon beside it -- and its Arsenal rising directly from the water's edge much as the Old Town does above them. Within that bowl, the Palazza Comittata and its gardens occupy their own segment on the eastern side, while the whole western arc is given over to the packed insulae and windcatchers of the wider Old Town, which from the 12th century onward merges seamlessly with the Water Quarter projecting out into the bay.

Beyond this core, Balboa's writ runs south and east through a chain of territories held not as provinces but as personal unions under the Principe: the archduchy of Gaviatolis immediately to the south, the march of Fiurisci guarding the Lupine passes into Lu-An to the far south, and, since the close of the Great War, the archduchy of Nikapolante at the peninsula's western foot. None of these answers to Balboa in quite the same way twice, each having come into the union by a different road, and a map of the principality's actual reach looks far less tidy than a map of its formal borders would suggest. Add to this commingled affair the guardianship of the Grand Academy of the Broken Isles that are situated in the Broken Drift off the eastern tip of the peninsula, and you begin to understand the headaches felt by many a chronicler that would much rather have written down a clean and singular line of fealty. For a Balboan, such mixed and manifold loyalties are simply the way things are.

Off the Cape's own east coast, where the Broken Drift turns as a circular countercurrent against the wider Eastern Gyre, lies the archipelago of the Broken Isles, a scattered island world of its own, though none of its isles amount to more than regional influence in their own right, and most accept Balboa's sovereignty in fact if not in any formal name. The Grand Academy of the Broken Isles takes its seat among them, an isle of the learned that began as a specialized naval yard and grew to become the Circulean's oldest and most prestigious center of learning. Through patronage and protection the House di Balboa became its de-facto guarantor, a further thread binding the archipelago to the Principality.

The city's position astride the Rush -- the swift current that has always governed passage between the Western and Eastern Gyres -- is the single fact on which Balboa's relationship with the rest of the Circle Sea has turned, and the reason no power in the Circulean basin has ever found it expedient to treat the principality as merely a small coastal state.

## The City of Tiers

To anyone who walks the city of Balboa with an eye for masonry, its architecture and expansion reads as a record of successive answers to the same problem: a place and population the sea left with nowhere to go but up, until they found a way to project the city out into the sea itself. Four rough tiers result, each dated to when the city first needed it and none ever abandoned once built.

### The Citadel and the Via Salita (7th century BR onward)

The oldest standing structure in the city is the fortified height above the harbor, raised by the Remean garrison that first staged fleet operations from the Cape and never abandoned since, whatever hands have held it in the centuries after. What began as a single fort has grown, addition by addition across the centuries, into the Palazza Comittata, the "Princely Palace" proper, set squarely on the height where Remean legionaries once stood watch over the bay and still commanding the city from it. The Palazza's hilltop front faces the Piazza dei Principi, a broad, level parade square paved in patterned stone for reviews, proclamations, and civic musters, where the Via Salita ends between ranks of the mercenary companies' own standards and the more modern lamp-pylons that light the square by night. Where once wooden palisades and legion tents stood, now marble stairs climb from the piazza to a triple-arched portico and a first inner courtyard planted in citrus and laurel around a long, shallow reflecting pool, where the Palazza's two main wings diverge into the many further quarters a palace of this size and age invariably accumulates. From this hilltop core the Palazza's rear descends by tiered, cascading gardens eastward down the sea -- pergolas, stair-canals, loggias, and belvederes threading downward level by level -- all the way to a fortified sea gate where the princely barges and, in the later ages, the Principe's own aeronaves berth in all weathers.

The Piazza dei Principi remains the city's ceremonial and civic heart in its own right, where oaths are sworn, proclamations read, and every Principe's investiture is completed. The Via Salita itself, the switchbacked road the Remean garrison first cut to move men and matériel between ship and citadel at speed, was widened over later centuries into the grand promenade it is today; it is still the processional route by which a new Principe descends from the piazza to be acclaimed at the water or ascends from it to take up the Palazza's authority, depending on which telling of the ceremony a given generation prefers.

### The Old Town and the Windcatchers (7th century BR to 8th century AR)

Between citadel and shore, the old town occupies the whole western arc of the city's amphitheater, in the same packed, vertical, insular style Balboa inherited directly from Remean building practice, unbroken by any intervening ruin the way the mother city's own remains are. A handful of public baths from this earliest period remain in ordinary use today, regarded by Balboans as simply Balboan rather than as relics of anything older. Rising above this dense fabric are the windcatchers, tall shafts open at the top to catch the sea breeze and drive it down into the close-built blocks beneath -- a product not of the city's earliest centuries but of the wealth and density that came with the Five Generations of Expansion, when a booming, crowded hillside city first had both the means and the need to solve its own heat the way it had already solved its own defense.

### The Harbors and the Arsenal (2nd to 8th century AR)

Below the old town, cut into the shoreline and walled apart from the natural Outer Basin, lies the military harbor proper, the Inner Basin in everyday speech. Fashioned in the way of a cothon, Balboa's dedicated naval basin is home to a fleet that predates every foreign claim ever laid upon it. Built and expanded across the Dark Ages to support first the consolidation of the Cape and then the push of the Five Generations, the cothon and its adjoining state Arsenal were redirected after the expansion's collapse at Gaviatolis, away from a war machine built to conquer toward one built to be hired out, the seed of the mercenary fleets and companies that would go on to fill the city's coffers for the centuries following. The Arsenal itself has grown by phases across that long span much as any great state shipyard does, its oldest slips and forges standing alongside additions from every subsequent age of Balboa's naval history, including, since the Aetheric Age's wondrous leap into the air, hangar bays where aeronaves are built and maintained.

### The Water Quarter (11th century AR onward)

Once the cliffs and the packed hillside had no more room to give, Balboa did what its geography eventually demanded and projected outward into the sea itself, in the boom years that followed the rise of Circulean corsairy. Where the Old Town solved its want of ground by building tightly and ever upward, the Water Quarter solves the same problem by building outward on piling and reclaimed shoal, so that the two districts that read, side by side, as opposites -- one cramped and vertical, climbing the cliff in switchbacks and stacked insulae, the other flat, spread wide across the shallows of the lagoon, and threaded through with canals rather than stairs.

The Water Quarter proper is by far the largest and most varied of Balboa's districts, a patchwork of reclaimed islets linked by countless footbridges and worked by a whole trade of skiffmen and ferrymen who move goods and passengers alike down its canals. Merchant houses front directly onto the water here, their ground floors given over to loading quays and warehouse arches, their upper stories opening onto galleries and loggias the factors and proprietors use to watch their fortunes come and go. Broad open campi break the district at intervals, wide enough for a guild muster or a public market that the canals themselves cannot serve, and it is around these squares rather than along any single great thoroughfare that the Water Quarter's grandest guildhalls and trading-house palazzi cluster. The mercenary companies keep their own barracks, muster-grounds, and contracting-houses along the district's outer edge nearest open water, where a company can embark or make landfall without threading the canals proper, while the merchant families and the guilds hold the inner islets closer to the Old Town's own western arc, into which the Water Quarter merges without any clean seam between the two.

## Government

Balboa is ruled by a Principe of the House di Balboa, a dynasty founded in the 2nd century AR by Cassan Balbo, grandson of a Balboan master of the harbor, who turned personal wealth and connection into a lasting line and was the first to bear the name "di Balboa." The title of Principe itself, and the House's formal enthronement as the principality's ruling dynasty, came later still, in the late 3rd century AR, in the settlement remembered as the First Princely Compact -- a full century after Cassan's own death, and the point at which what had been merely the city's foremost family became, in law and title both, its royal house. The principality's formal relationship to Lavion has been, for most of its history, a matter of careful and profitable ambiguity: the old Crown Vassal oath sworn in the Second Princely Compact that ended the War of the Three Kings (1117-1121) bound Balboa's fleet and officers to the Crown's ongoing use and left its mercenary companies free to contract as they always had, without ever quite settling what the Principality owed Lavion should the two obligations come into conflict. That ambiguity held for four centuries and was only finally resolved, in Balboa's own favor, by the Great War and the return to independence that followed the Peace of Balboa in 1574.

The territories held in personal union -- Gaviatolis, Fiurisci, and, since 1574, Nikapolante -- are governed as distinct holdings under the Principe's person rather than folded into a single administration, each retaining its own baronial and civic structure from before the union was made.

## Heraldry

Balboa displays three distinct devices, each proper to its own register rather than interchangeable with the others: the dynastic arms of the House di Balboa, the state arms of the Principality, and the civic arms of the City. A ship flies the Principality's colors, a city gate or guildhall bears the City's, and only the Principe's own household and personal writ carry the House's -- a visitor who learns to read the difference can tell at a glance whether they are dealing with the dynasty, the state, or the town.

The City of Balboa has the oldest arms of the three. Its blazon -- _azure two fishtail griffins argent armed and beaked or combattant, on an enty argent an anchor of the field_ -- is the oldest heraldic reference to the giant fishtail griffin, Gran Pescoda in the local tongue, that has become emblematic of Balboa everywhere around the Circle Sea. Its achievements are a crest of _a capital's mural crown proper_ and _a mantling of erminois and pean_, befitting a proud city that rules its surrounding lands and itself, however much the administration is beholden to the wishes of the principate. The blue anchor on a white enty (a bowed triangle at the base of the shield) is a plain reference to the site of its fabled natural harbour in the bay under the white cliffs of the Cape, and the silver griffins "locked in battle" speak of the fact that the rocks out in the approach to the bay have ever been a nesting ground for the giant fishtail, whose dominance displays have fascinated people since the earliest days of settlement.

The arms of House di Balboa are blazoned _per pale azure and argent, a long axe sable bendwise_ for a black axe laid diagonally across a field split blue and white. The full achievement adds _a royal helm sable crested with a griffin's head of the same beaked or and langued gules, shewn with a cloak pavilion royal ermine and gules, with a princely crown proper, and supported by two fishtail griffins sable, armed and beaked or_ -- the Pescoda flanking the shield as a deliberate echo of the city's heraldic beast, while the pavilion and crown of estate carry forward, in gold and ermine and crimson, the trappings in blazon of a sovereignty the House has never once set down, whatever accommodations history otherwise forced on it. The house motto is _"Cutting Both Ways"_ -- equally a description of the axe on the shield and as a plain admission of temperament: a dynasty that has spent its whole history balancing two edges at once, home against liege, trade against arms, independence against the Crown Vassal oath, and has generally preferred to let both edges do their work rather than dull either one. By folk tradition, the axe of the arms is taken to be "Treebiter", legendarily wielded by Cassan di Balboa, though possibly an even older reference given the evidence uncovered of Remean legionaries once stationed here bearing an axe-like emblem on their banners.

The Principality's blazon then brings both to the fullest radiance. It reads _per pale argent and azure two fishtail griffins combattant counterchanged within a double Tressure Flory counter flory counterchanged_, repeating the bifurcated blazon of the princely House but showing on it the battling pescodas of the city, here counterchanged into the colors of the opposing field. A similarly counterchanged double-line with floral accents elevates the blazon into a sophisticated register; no mere battlefield banner, this. Its achievement are the _royal helmet sable_ of the House, but given a crest of _a Naval Crown proper_ (a golden crown of ships and rope) to signify the maritime and naval character of the Principality. Its stately mantle is a _cloak pavilion proper_ sans the princely crown that applies only to the House. Whereas the city had no supporters, the Principality takes _to the dexter a Fishtail griffin sable_ as per the House, but grants it the _Princely Crown proper_ seen on the mantling of the House blazon. This is for the support given by the House to the Principality it rules. On the other side the supporter is _to the sinister a mermaid in her glory argent_ (ie, a silver mermaid crowned with the golden dolphin crown that heraldically signifies the charge is standing in for the Noble Element) _bearing a long axe sable_, putting the fate of the House symbolically into the hands of the element, just as the motto itself exemplifies: _"At Her Mercy We Prosper"_ is the plain admission that Balboa's prosperity, however carefully managed, ultimately answers to a hand no mortal may command.

## Notable Principes

**Cassan Balbo**, also called Cassan di Balboa (c. 137-188 AR), founded the dynasty from the wealth and standing of his grandfather, a master of the harbor rather than any prior noble house, and was the first to bear the name that would define the line thereafter.

**Giancarle di Balboa** (1088-1159 AR; r. 1109-1158 AR) is the Principe popularly meant whenever Circuleans call someone "crafty as a Balboan." The last Principe to rule an independent Balboa, he negotiated the settlement that ended the War of the Three Kings (1117-1121) on terms so favorable to the city that the loss of formal sovereignty it entailed was never felt, by his own subjects or by history since, as any real defeat.

**Vittorio II di Balboa** (1357-1401 AR; r. 1380-1401 AR) was a conservative, competent ruler who expanded Balboa's influence through learned patronage rather than arms, investing heavily in the Grand Academy of the Broken Isles. A voice of moderation during Lavion's succession crisis of 1401, he died in the same year under circumstances that have never been satisfactorily explained.

**Lauralotta Victualia Emmanuela Augusta Terisea di Balboa** (1389-1460 AR; r. 1405-1457 AR), remembered as La Principessa Serenissima, is the most famous of the principality's few female rulers, monumental in stature both physical, standing some seven feet tall, and political: Privy Councilor to Queen Lurie-Mavrelle of Lavion, Magistra Plenipotentiary of the Grand Academy of the Broken Isles, and Principessa in her own right besides. Known for her fashion dominance and her aromantic aloofness as much as for her patronage of learning and progressive reform, she stood as a known and important ally of Lurie-Mavrelle through the unsettled later years of the Aetheric Age.

## Economy

Balboa's wealth rests on three legs that answer to its three harbors. The cothon and Arsenal represent the oldest of these: a home fleet, older than any foreign claim on it, whose maintenance under the Crown Vassal settlement is an addition to Balboa's own naval establishment rather than its reason for existing. The commercial harbor and water quarter represent the entrepôt trade proper, built on Balboa's position astride the Rush and on the pan-Circulean Guilder standard, alongside Beniotti one of the two fixed points of Circulean commerce since the trade's rise. The mercenary companies represent the third and most distinctly Balboan leg: born of the Five Generations' collapse at Gaviatolis and the turn inward that followed, they have contracted out across the Circle Sea for the centuries since, an income stream entirely separate from the state's naval obligations, which is exactly what made their wartime recall in the 1560s so consequential -- the Principe was not redeploying a single fleet but withdrawing an entire second arm of the city's income back to its own defense.

## Faith

Balboa falls under the Autocephalous Church of Ertra, seated in the city itself and governing the whole of the Ertrese Peninsula from the Cape to the march of Fiurisci. Ertra's autocephaly was granted in recognition of the peninsula's long habit of governing itself, and its clergy carry an academical reputation to match, expected to command letters and numbers as readily as creed. The city's Lurism itself has old and pragmatic roots: the faith arrived by accommodation rather than conquest, welcomed into the city around 55 AR to keep peace with the pressing north rather than out of any crisis of the older reverence paid to Maregina, and the two claims on Balboan devotion appear to have coexisted for some time before whatever settlement, doctrinal or simply habitual, left the Church of Ertra as sole steward of the city's faith today.

Balboa is understood as the birthplace of Harmonism, the Orthodox school that reads the Light's order as continuous with the lawful structure of the world itself, prizing meticulous craftsmanship and scholarly rigor as virtues in their own right -- a theology that could scarcely have taken root anywhere but a city that had spent a millennium and a half running an entrepôt economy on contracts, ledgers, and Remean-descended hydraulic and naval engineering. That same instinct produced the Similitudean current within Ertrese clergy, common among the jurists, engineers, and academy-trained priests who hold that to order the self rightly is to harmonize the community, and it is this current, more than any other, that gives Harmonism its particular Ertrese and Balboan flavor against the wider version of the school that later took equally firm root among the academies of eastern Lavion. The Grand Academy of the Broken Isles stands as the tradition's great intellectual seat, its scholars active in aetheric and pneumatic learning as much as in doctrine, and it was Balboan patronage -- Vittorio II's investment foremost among it, and Lauralotta di Balboa's own tenure as its Magistra Plenipotentiary after him -- that carried the Academy to the prominence Lavienese queens would later pattern their own royal colleges after. Harmonism is not without its critics even within Orthodoxy, praised for the heights of its rhetoric as readily as it is chided for the ivory tower from which that rhetoric is so often preached, a charge Balboans tend to receive as more compliment than rebuke.

## Historical Sketch

Balboa's founding is attested with more precision than most Remean-era history allows. In 687 BR the empire raised a naval staging ground on the tip of Ertra, serving at once as the first western port for the barge-borne tribute of the Merhal and as a forward anchorage for fleet operations into the Western Gyre. A hilltop fort raised in 650 BR to protect that anchorage is sometimes mistaken, in looser tellings, for the settlement's true founding, but the two events are distinct: the port came first, the fort second, once the port had proven worth defending. Together the installations were known in surviving Remean usage as the _statia vala buia_, "the stations between the currents," and it is from Valabuium, the proper name the settlement itself took from this designation, that "Balboa" descends -- a gradual mangling of the same kind that turned Remea into Reme in Lurist mouths, though gentler, since no _damnatio memoriae_ ever set out to erase Valabuium's name the way Remea's was suppressed.

From 680 BR to the Fall, Valabuium thrived as a semi-autonomous crossroads of Remean trade, governed by appointed Capitolinae in the manner of any directly governed periphery polity, and it was here, at the empire's furthest western edge, that Remean culture and commerce flourished at some remove from Remea's own direct hand.

When Remea fell in 0 AR, the same catastrophe that buried the mother city scrambled the Western Gyre's currents badly enough to sweep the Eternal Dominion's advancing war-fleet far south of its target. Some Balboans still call this upheaval the grief of Maregina, rather than naming Phlogistophor and the Collegium's failed working outright, and both accounts are told in the city to this day without either one displacing the other. Whichever a household prefers, the practical result was the same: Valabuium's colonial government collapsed with the rest of the empire's, the Eternal Dominion's warbands and missionaries pressing down from the north found no fleet waiting to meet them at the city's own door, and local families were left to scramble for power in a vacuum no Capitolina now claimed to fill.

By around 35 AR, a council of local patricians had emerged to govern the city in the empire's continued absence, and as the Gyres settled back into their old, reliable character after years of unreliability, trade resumed and the old Remean forms -- civic, linguistic, cultic -- began the slow drift into distinctly local variants that would in time produce something recognizably Balboan rather than merely provincial-Remean. That drift met its first real outside pressure around 55 AR, as Lurist missionaries and warbands continued their crusade against the Moonkin of the southern Wolves' Teeth. Balboa, already pragmatic and already mercantile even this early in its independent history, opened its walls to the worship of Lur rather than risk the north's displeasure, and the faith spread through the city gradually and without confrontation, arriving alongside rather than in place of the older reverence paid to Maregina. It is this same instinct -- to accommodate rather than resist, and to let two claims on the city's loyalty stand side by side rather than force a choice between them -- that the patrician council of this era, and in time the House di Balboa itself, would each make their own.

In the century or so following, as the patrician council gave way in turn to more durable rule, Balboa consolidated the hinterland immediately behind it, bringing the baronies of Caldasci and Maristagna and the city of Rivazziano into a settled core. It was in this same period, in the 2nd century AR, that Cassan Balbo, grandson of a Balboan master of the harbor, built his own wealth and connections into the lasting dynasty that would rule the principality thereafter. Between roughly 650 and 800 AR, the principality pushed south beyond that core in what is remembered as the Five Generations of Expansion, taking the rival power of Gaviatolis and reaching, at the push's furthest extent, the country of Nikapolante. The expansion ended in collapse around 800 AR, when a Gaviatolian warlord expelled Balboa's governors and delivered the Principality a legendary defeat in the field. Balboa turned inward in the defeat's aftermath, and around 830 AR the standing regiments that had carried the Five Generations -- themselves patterned after the original Remean legions once garrisoned at the port -- were released from state service as free mercenary companies, granted wide autonomy and soon famous the Circle Sea over for their discipline, their flair, and their brutality alike.

Turned away from open conquest but hardly away from the sword, Balboa spent the centuries from roughly 900 to 1300 AR as one of the great powers of the Age of Corsairs, waging private wars against pirate princes and rogue states across the Circle Sea and beyond, its own merchant-marine warships and flamboyant captains blurring the line between commerce and piracy so thoroughly that Circulean memory has never fully separated the two. It was in the midst of this era, around 1000 AR, that Genovrast de Lavion's newly forged kingdom first laid claim to sovereignty over the whole Ertrese Peninsula, Balboa included -- a claim never pressed in Genovrast's own lifetime, nor in his heirs', but just as pointedly never abandoned. For more than a century Balboa, mighty in its corsair prime and in no mood to be told it answered to an upstart river-kingdom, treated the claim as beneath serious notice, a scoffing dismissal history did not entirely vindicate.

That old claim was finally pressed in 1117 AR, when the death of King Oswine de Lavion and the succession of his uncle, Roburic "Ironfist" -- the same king whose name and epithet a much later Roburic II would deliberately pattern his own reign after -- gave Cavrian de Nevron, ruler of Cendrelac and self-styled King in the South, the opening he judged the moment demanded. Cavrian pressed his own claim in open rebellion, drawing Mirevigne and other southern powers into coalition against Lavion and throwing the realm into the War of the Three Kings. Balboa, initially allied to the King in the South, found itself formally at odds with Lavion from the war's first days, though its own fragmented condottieri companies, true to the independence the Azure Codex would later have to reckon with, ended up fighting on every side of the conflict rather than as any single disciplined arm of it.

The reckoning fell on the city itself in 1119 AR, when Lavienese and allied forces settled into what became the Siege of Balboa, a fierce, grinding investment that held for more than two years against defenses that gave up ground only slowly. Blockade runners and smugglers kept the populace fed throughout, but no amount of smuggled grain could spare the garrison the attrition of a siege that long, and by 1121 AR the city had no real choice left but surrender.

What followed surrender was, by the standard of any conquered city, remarkable. Principe Giancarle di Balboa's diplomacy secured terms generous enough that Balboan memory has never quite treated 1121 as a defeat: the House retained the city and its title outright; the Principality became a privileged Crown Vassal owing fealty directly to the King of Lavion rather than being subsumed under any local duke; Balboa kept the right to mint its own coin, to regulate its own commerce, and to appoint its own officials; and in exchange for elite mercenary service and naval force projection at the Crown's call, the Principality was exempted from most royal levies outright. This settlement, the Second Princely Compact, is the origin of the Crown Vassal fealty that would hold, deliberately ambiguous, for the four centuries following. Balboa's own right to mint coin, granted in the same settlement, is what lets the principality circulate both the Lavienese Livre and its own Guilder-pegged coinage side by side -- struck to the Commonwealth's standard closely enough to pass interchangeably in most transactions, though a precise accounting would call it the Balboan Guilder rather than the Commonwealth's own.

The following year, 1122 AR, saw the Azure Codex established -- named for the House's own azure and argent, and drafted to regulate the rights and duties of the mercenary companies under the new framework of Balboan autonomy within Lavienese sovereignty. From the Codex forward, every company swore never to take contract against Balboa itself, and every new captain required license from the reigning Principe or Principessa before raising a company of their own.

Nikapolante, lost in the collapse of the Five Generations, passed in time into Lavion's own direct vassalage during the 13th century, an acquisition made around Balboa's old sphere rather than through it. Gaviatolis, by contrast, returned to Balboa peacefully in 1244, gained by marriage rather than reconquest and held ever after in personal union. Fiurisci followed in 1319 on the same terms. Through the same 12th century that brought the Second Princely Compact, the Guilder standard took hold across the wider Circle Sea, and Balboa rose alongside Beniotti as one of the basin's fixed entrepôts.

That ambiguity held for four centuries, tested only once, decisively, by the Great War (1561-1574). As Vel Kiriya's push toward the Ertran coast threatened to expose the true weight Balboan hulls carried within Lavion's own navy, the Principe drew the fleet inward to secure the Rush, then recalled every company and mercenary asset under contract abroad, declaring that Balboa would raise arms only in its own defense until the threat had passed. The Peace of Balboa, signed in the city itself in 1574, rewrote the old Crown Vassal fealty into a genuine, formally recognized autonomy -- and in the same settlement, Nikapolante, severed from Balboa for nearly eight centuries, declared for the Principality and seceded with it, closing a loop opened by the Five Generations' collapse and giving Balboa, for the first time, a quiet but real reach across the whole of Ertra.
