### Definition and scope
Scalarism is a quantitative account of material composition in Arlos that represents any substance as a six‑vector composition of shares. The six shares are non-negative and normalized to a fixed whole, recorded as shares of this whole in fractional form. Informed by a very mechanically minded school of aetheric thought, Scalarism refuses to use the traditional names of the elements, and prefers a "cleaner" (in their eyes) reference point of using their supposed relational functions, thus listing *Excitance, Quiescence, Diffusance, Persistence, Fluence*, and *Consistance* in place of the traditional hexad.
### Historical overview
Scalarism emerged during the late 13th to early 14th centuries AR in the progressive alchemical workshops of the Eastern Circulean, where closeness to the Academy of the Broken Isles lead to a steady influx of scholastically trained apprentices that favoured fine scales, regularized bookkeeping, and the reformed Academic style of dozenal accounting. These new generations of alchemical engineers established specifications that could travel between provinces without reliance on a particular master alchemist's assay, which tended to be highly ideosyncratic in practice and did not readily translate between practicioners. By mid‑15th century AR, major guilds used per-total composition declarations as provable marks of quality for their traded materials.
### Relationship to Fractalism
Fractalism, being the older school by several centuries, holds much sway in scholastic circles, and its vocabulary is widespread even among the common folk, if imprecise. Scalarism does not dispute the Fractal observations, but it treats its roles as emergent orderings of the same numbers. Fractalism's Body, Veins, and Humours are, in Scalarist terms, merely the largest shares at their "layer" of composition. In contemporary workshops both idioms are used side by side: Fractalist vocabulary is quick to convey the coarse understanding (and its language is closer to the vernacular), while Scalar vectors provide the fine detail to certify lots and predict performance.
### Notation and measurement
Scalarism, much like Fractalism, arose from earlier alchemical practices. Thus by historical convention, Scalar usage expresses its measurements as six fractions of the holy number 360, traditionally the unit used to express totality and represented with the old form ⨀. Thus, an Fire share of sixty parts of the total would be written down as "ποʃ⨀" in clean and precise Academic dozenal notation, and expressed as "a Excitance share of sixty degrees of the whole".

In modern usage, the symbol "⨕" has arisen as a shorthand, being a ligature of the dozenal fraction marker "ʃ" and the mathematical symbol for 360. Thus the above would be shortened to "πο⨕", a notation style that has gained widespread scholastic usage wherever it is necessary to identify relative parts of a greater whole. Similarly, the long form "degrees of the whole" is often shortened to "per whole" outside scholarly contexts, or clipped further to just "degrees". In the sexagesimal and duodecimal systems prevalent in the Circulean, degrees have thus taken the place filled by percentages in a decimal context.

The six do not always sum neatly to 360, as Circulean instruments are nowhere near fine enough to provide the necessary granularity to peer into the finest components of material reality -- a fact that both the Scalarist and the Fractalist account agree on. Given the admitted limits of observation, whole-number parts per whole are preferred to forestall the impression of total accuracy.
### Method and aims
Scalarism treats bulk behavior as a function of the six shares and seeks regular correlations between composition and observable properties. Mixtures that do not undergo internal reorganization are modeled by weighted sums of their vectors. Repeated processes are specified by the observed changes in the targeted elemental vectors, expressed in relative terms per vector. When necessary to account for granular differences in composition, a Resolution is declared to indicate the volumetric scale over which the shares were established.
### Limitations and declarations
Elemental shares depend on resolution. A granite block at a scale of 1 pole cubed will differ from a flake taken from a mica seam within the same block. Scalar assays therefore pair the resultant vectors with the resolution used, and often include notes on any process history likely to have impacted the composition. Within these stated limits, Scalarism provides a neutral, reproducible language for describing material composition across works, guilds, and provinces.
