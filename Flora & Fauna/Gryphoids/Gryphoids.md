## Premise

Arlos has no birds. Their ecological work -- pollination, aerial insect control, scavenging, wading, apex predation, seed dispersal -- is performed by **gryphs**, a single but diverse clade of endothermic, oviparous hexapods. Typical anatomy blends a feathered beaked forebody with manipulative scaled **foretalons** and furred digitigrade **hindlegs**. Most lineages are strong fliers; more specialized niches exist of flightless runners, divers, or burrowers. Calls are varied and often diagnostic of guild and habitat.

---

## Core body plan and behavior

- **Six-limbed plan:** Beaked head; a dedicated wing pair; grasping foretalons that do not bear weight in most volant taxa; furred hindlegs that do. This separation of flight and manipulation lets gryphs handle food, brace, and tool around while still specialized for flight.
- **Natural modulation:** Specialized nodules and bone microstructures introduce marginal biases in Attraction and Rigidity, just enough so that the larger breeds can launch, carry riders, and stay aloft in workable weather.
- **Thermal ecology:** Endothermy and plumage patterns parallel Earth birds in function. Furred hindquarters give added insulation in alpine and polar edges (e.g., **Frostmane**).
- **Sociality:** From tight urban kin bands (**Gutterjack**, **Quay Skulk**) to colonial cliff nesters (**Cliffscream**) to pair-bonded apexes (**Royal Griffin**). Many species show strong site fidelity and seasonal route memory.
- **Acoustic landscape:** Urban bands trade harsh barks and rattles; shorelines ring with skrees and honks; uplands carry trumpeted kraauns. Calls coordinate foraging, warn of predators, and maintain territory.

## Habitats and flagship species

### Urban

Cities host a near-complete miniature ecosystem. Species mentioned here are widespread and emblematic of their niche; local variants exist all across Arlos, adapted to local environs.

- **Omnivorous problem-solvers:** **Gutterjack** or the smaller **Nick o' Silver** patrols markets and middens, learning latches and routes. **Tower Crook** syndicates belfries, trading perch safety for scraps and serving as early-warning against large predators. **Market Tumbler** flocks gather on statues and cornices, commuting to squares for grain and crumbs with excellent homing sense.
- **Garden insect control and pollination:** **Courtyard Sparr** and **Sweep** suppress aphids and midges by day; **Humsip** and **Red-Throat** keep trellised herbs and citrus pollinated. **Lamp-Mothlet** takes night swarms at lanterns -- together these species buffer insect booms across the diel cycle.
- **Wood and structure specialists:** **Chiselbark** and **Saptap** partition trunks by height and season, prying bark seams and tapping sap; their presence tracks street-tree health.
- **Waterfront cleaners:** **Quay Skulk** dominates fish districts, flipping lids and following carts; **Canalet** spears minnows in rills and fountains; **Paddletail** sifts ponds and canals.
- **Domestic lines:** **Yardheeler** (eggs, pests), **Tallowback** (meat, eggs, alarm), **Shepherd's Kite** (aerial herding), **Millpick** (granary vermin patrol), **Ringneck** (boat fishing partner), **Sun-Pennant** (display), **Painted Cantor** (caged songster).
- **Nocturnal predators:** **Duskmantle** glides hedge lines to take rodents, complementing the lamp-mothlet's insect niche.

**Net effect:** Cities that provide perch-gantries, twine curtains, and secure bin latches channel gryph activity into sanitation, pollination, and pest control while reducing conflict.

### Marine and shore

Gryphs dominate the air-sea skin by specializing above and below the surface.

- **Air-sea apex:** **Giant Fishtail** (Ertrese Peninsula) -- immense fishtail griffin that breaches bait schools and rides channel winds to hunt for seal and tuna; few peers at the surface.
- **Pelagic soarers:** **Spraywing** ranges far offshore on long scimitar wings; **Gyre Runner** skims blue-water gyres and occasionally pirates lighter soarers.
- **Coastal hunters:** **Shoalharrier** threads mangroves and coral alleys with agile fluke turns; **Reef Pennant** works breaker lines and harbor edges, timing raids with human schedules; **Mudmantle** rides turbidity fronts in estuaries.
- **True diver:** **Fourwing** has forelimbs and wings fused into flippers and wins chases once prey sinks beneath the bright skin.
- **Colony marker:** **Cliffscream** paints navigation clues below its ledges with guano, a reliable signal of fishy water for small craft.

**Partitioning:** Fishtails rule the surface strike; spraywings the long fetch; fourwings the subsurface chase; reef and delta specialists fill the margins where structure and turbidity create prey traps.

### Cliffs and open-air apex

- **Royal Griffin (Lu-An):** Pair-hunting escarpment ruler whose coordinated stoops pin and hammer medium game. Its patrols reshape herd movement on adjoining plains.
- **Bonehook:** Thermal rider and obligate scavenger that cleans carcasses quickly and reduces disease pressure; yields cliffs to royals but owns flatland thermals.

### Alpine and high country

- **Frostmane:** Grizzly-scale omnivore and carrion opener of the Wolves' Teeth and Svenu highlands. Mixes rock-flipping, short rush ambush, and winter cleanup. Holds ridge ledges against mid-sized runners and deters storm royals with bluff postures.

### Open steppe and scrub

- **Demigryph:** Flightless quad-sprinter that ends chases in seconds by ankle-hooking and shearing the throat. Can be trained, but resists domestication.
- **Utar:** Wolf-scale bipedal pack grappler; off-ground grasping foretalons rotate large prey down. Dominant where ground is broken or scrubby.
- **Windrunner:** Banner-pennoned cursorial grazer that concentrates at water points and frames predator ranges.
- **Theronga:** Tall steppe browser that marries giraffe-like reach with a powerful spearing neck, taking small prey between feeds; capable of flight but seldom airborne.

**Predator partitioning:** Demigryphs excel at short open bursts; utars control rough mosaics with teamwork and grappling; royals pressure both from the air. Mixed mammal--gryph predator fields are common, with local outcomes set by terrain.

### Wetlands and rivers

- **Reed-Stalker:** Tripod stance in reeds -- two hands and one foot brace while the spear beak strikes fish and frogs.
- **Tidepicker:** Pebble-mottled probe of strandlines that advances in loose bands as the tide drops, competing with small beach mammals.

## Food webs and ecosystem services

- **Insect regulation:** Sweeps and mothlets reduce biting insects; chiselbarks and saptaps suppress wood borers; millpicks clear stored-grain pests.
- **Pollination and seedwork:** Humsips and painted cantors pollinate courtyard flora; garden sparrs disperse seeds.
- **Sanitation:** Quay skulks, bonehooks, and gutterjacks clear carrion and refuse quickly; cliffscreams and mudmantles funnel marine nutrients ashore via guano.
- **Top-down control:** Royals, demigryphs, and utars check mid-sized herbivores, shaping grazing mosaics and plant succession.

---

## Human interfaces

- **Urban infrastructure:** Gryph-latches on bins and shutters; twine curtains over fish racks; carved **griffin shelves** in seawalls to concentrate coastal roosts.
- **Husbandry:** Domestic lines emphasize low reactivity, social tolerance, and route memory. Training relies on whistle codes and hand flags; some work with collars or rings to limit feeding on the job.
- **Cultural presence:** Balboa's native **Giant Fishtail** and the universally venerated **Royal Griffin** anchor civic identity and heraldry. Painted cantors and sun-pennants shape sound and color in homes and processions.

---

## Reproduction and life history

- **Clutches:** Generally small for large taxa; many urban species manage multiple clutches per year if food is steady. Domesticated breeds such as the **yardheeler** are selected for reliable daily eggs and quick growth.
- **Site fidelity:** Cliff and tower species reuse ledges and platforms for years. Urban gardeners cultivate colonies of sweeps and humsips with predictable returns.
- **Ontogeny:** Strong imprinting in several domesticated lines aids training; eyass-style hand-rearing of royals or demigryphs for scouting or hunting is rare and tightly regulated.
