**Also called:** Longneck; Strider

**Morphology:** Plumage tawny to sand with pale chevrons along the long neck; fur sandy over the loins and thighs; wings short, banner‑like pennons used for display and balance; beak deep and hatchet‑shearing; foretalons small but dexterous for stripping twigs; legs tall and springy with two forward toes and a stabilizing dewclaw; tail long and weighty for counterbalance.

**Size:** Body 2.8-3.2 m; tail 0.9-1.1 m extra; pennon span 1.4-1.8 m.

**Call:** A booming "oom‑oom" punctuated by display bill‑clacks "klock‑klock" and a rough alarm "krorr!"

**Notes:** A tall cursorial browser that marries giraffe‑like reach with the ground speed of emus and terrorbirds, the theronga raids thorny shrubs and scattered steppe trees other grazers cannot easily use. Mixed herds often trail it to catch shaken pods and trimmed shoots, and the theronga’s high head serves as an early‑warning sentinel for windrunners. Utar packs target juveniles, but adults form outward‑facing rings and strike with devastating forward kicks; demigryphs avoid the feet and rarely press an attack. Seasonal movements link distant water points, and along these routes they open browse corridors that shape shrub regeneration.
