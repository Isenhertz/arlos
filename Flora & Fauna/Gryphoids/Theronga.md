**Also called:** Longneck; Strider

**Morphology:** Plumage tawny to sand with pale chevrons along the long neck; fur sandy over the loins and thighs; wings short for its bulk, flared like banners for display and balance, yet carried aloft by the aetheric lattice of its bones in short, laboured flights over a ridge or a river; beak deep and hatchet‑shearing; foretalons small but dexterous for stripping twigs; legs tall and springy with two forward toes and a stabilizing dewclaw; tail long and weighty for counterbalance.

**Size:** Body 2.8-3.2 m; tail 0.9-1.1 m extra; wingspan 1.4-1.8 m.

**Call:** A booming "oom‑oom" punctuated by display bill‑clacks "klock‑klock" and a rough alarm "krorr!"

**Notes:** A tall cursorial browser that marries giraffe‑like reach with the ground speed of emus and terrorbirds, the theronga raids thorny shrubs and scattered steppe trees other grazers cannot easily use. It is no pure browser: between feeds it stalks small and middling prey across open ground with a slow, deliberate gait, and the hatchet beak drops in a single darting strike. Mixed herds often trail it to catch shaken pods and trimmed shoots, and the theronga’s high head serves as an early‑warning sentinel for windrunners. Utar packs target juveniles, but adults form outward‑facing rings and strike with devastating forward kicks; demigryphs avoid the feet and rarely press an attack. Seasonal movements link distant water points, and along these routes they open browse corridors that shape shrub regeneration.
