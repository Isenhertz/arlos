## Split by the Teeth of Wolves

The southern coasts of the Circle Sea belong to the continent of Rimec-Suur. Today's cartographers divide the continent into several regions, such as [[Stranu]], [[Svenu]], [[Ertra]], and [[Lu-An]], all of which are described best on their own; what follows is the description of what might most accurately be termed "central Rimec-Suur", the heartland of the continent that lies to either side of the vast [[Wolves' Teeth Mountains]].

Both sides of the mountains are defined by the rivers that gather the water spilled against those granite titans: the Varsa in the north, the Swicca in the south.

### Hydrology of Northern Rimec-Suur
The North's waterways form a system of arteries that define its politics, economy, and settlement. Flowing down from the Wolves' Teeth Mountains, rivers carry snowmelt and rainfall toward the [[Circle Sea]], carving fertile plains and sustaining the great cities. None is more important than the Varsa, a river vast but not oceanic, navigable for sea-going ships as far inland as [[Virell]].

The Varsa begins high in the western Wolves' Teeth, where glacial melt and mountain tributaries combine into a torrent. It flows southeasterly for a thousand leagues through cataracts and foothill valleys, before sweeping to the north-east in a crescent towards Virell where it breaks in the magnificent White Falls, and then eastward for another 340 leagues of deep, slow flow to the Circle Sea. At the base of the White Falls the river is at its widest, about a league across, its breadth broken by numerous islets and backwaters formed around the White Falls' descent. The White Falls themselves are the largest waterfalls in all of the Circulean by flow volume and width, a spectacular curtain-like cascade where the river plunges in broad cataracts, making direct passage impossible. Only a series of engineered river-locks bypassing the falls allow navigation. At the delta the river spreads to an estuary of about three leagues from shore to shore as it spills into the Circle Sea. The Varsa is deep enough at Virell to host sea-going vessels, and serves as the trade spine for the Lavienese heartland. Its delta is a hub of shipbuilding, commerce, and fishing. Among its tributaries, the Ardelve is famed as a cold mountain stream rich in trout, while the Mirevant runs slower and more fertile, its banks lined with rich abbeys and bustling market towns.

In the northwest lies Rasselune, the duchy at the foot of the Commonwealth. This region lacks a single dominant river; instead, short and steep coastal streams drain the Paltese highlands into Cavrel Sound, which forms a natural harbor. The City of Paltos thrives as a maritime gateway rather than a riverine hub. Its economy rests on fishing fleets, trade with the [[Beniotti|Keystone Isles]], and naval expeditions, while irrigation remains minimal compared to the river-fed heartlands.

The east is defined by [[Principality of Balboa|Balboa]] and the Ertrese Peninsula, a land of steep, storm-fed valleys with shorter rivers. The Ertra itself is a narrow, quick river that runs through the hillsides before emptying into the great natural harbor that is the jewel of the Principality, while the Gavine cuts across Gaviatolis, watering vineyards and olive groves. These rivers with their many cataracts and shallow depth are not suited for vessels larger than a rowboat, but they rush down to the sea with force, powering mills and workshops on the way, and some of the ancients' aqueducts still funnel drinking water into the cities.

Southward, the Wolves' Teeth send their waters into narrower valleys and alpine basins. Glacial and snowmelt-fed rivers descend into the marches, including the Fiure, which winds through Fiurisci before joining the Gavine, and the Galas, the principal river of Engalas, which forms a narrow but fertile floodplain at the foot of the mountains. These rivers sustain irrigation for hardy crops and pastures, but they also define the strategic geography of the Marches, for their valleys are the only practical invasion routes from Svenu and Lu-An. Many fortresses stand at the confluences, guarding crossings and mountain passes.

There are also important lakes and wetlands. Lake Trastel, fed by the Mirevant, serves as an inland shipping hub and is increasingly used as a landing site for airships, while Lake Cyrvene is a high alpine tarn that supplies water for the fortresses that guard key southern passes. The Varsa Delta marshes form broad saltmarshes and reedbeds, vital for fishing, reeds, and waterfowl.

Climate shapes the flow of the North's waters. By the coastline, patterns of rainfall and summer droughts dictate the rhythm of agriculture. In the center, temperate rains keep the rivers flowing steadily, though spring floods are common. In the south, snowmelt defines river life, and the timing of thaws can mean either feast or famine. Together, these waters form the natural structure of life, binding the regions as surely as the oaths of crown and vassal.

## Hydrology of the Southlands

South of the Wolves' Teeth lie the Southlands -- a foreland belt of glacial valleys, lake basins, and a broad piedmont plain that trends east. The Teeth separate it from Lavion to the north; to the west a deep, cold gulf opens to the Open Ocean and cleanly parts the Southlands from Svenu, preserving Svenu's maritime, fjorded character. Eastward the plain lowers and dries until it meets the fringe of Lu-An.

### The Swicca

Like the Varsa that dominates the northern half of Rimec-Suur, the Swicca is the Southland's master river, a long continental trunk that keeps close to the mountain's toe. Rising from multiple glacial headwaters along the inner wall, it gathers the outflow of the lake belt and most piedmont streams, then runs broad and deliberate toward the east. In its upper reaches, still tight to the range, it braids green with silt; a few dozen leagues on it binds into a single deep channel that meanders behind high natural levees. Its regime is snow and ice fed -- a firm spring rise as firnfields unlock, a second, slower crest as the deep lakes release their storage, and low, steady summer flow modulated by karstic springs. In hard winters the Swicca ices over in long sheets upriver and in pans downriver; breakup comes from the head, stacking floes against bends and logjams and carving fresh side channels.

### The Deserted Sea

Where Southland meets Lu-An, the Swicca does not find the sea. It empties into a vast endorheic basin called the "Deserted Sea" -- a shallow inland sea cupped by old desert pediments and low salt scarps. In wet decades it is a single, steel-blue expanse with a crenellated shoreline of reed-beds and wandering dunes. In dry cycles it retreats into multiple lobes, leaving behind salt flats, stranded spits, and crescent marshes stitched together by distributary cuts. The Swicca's delta is a labyrinth of levee-top ridges, blind channels, and seasonal lakes; avulsions come in human lifetimes, not millennia, and ports must follow or die. Salinity varies by lobe and season, highest in the eastern pans where the water thins to a glare and the wind writes ripples across bare crust.

### Lake belt and tributaries

At the foot of the Teeth a north-to-south chain of overdeepened basins holds long, cold lakes. Each sits behind a stony sill; each drains south or east by a short gorge into the Swicca. Their clarity is alpine, their levels pulse on a dozenal rhythm with snowmelt, and their deltas are fat, fan-shaped tongues pushing into the piedmont. From the south, shorter rivers rise in bogs and springfields on the outer moraines and lend the Swicca a floor of resilience when the mountain slackens. Hanging valleys spill waterfalls; jökulhlaups from ice-dam failures are known, their signatures etched as scoured chutes and outsized boulders lying smug in gentle meadows.

### The Western Gulf and the Svenu divide

Between Svenu and the Southlands yawns a broad, glacially carved gulf that bites deep into the coast from the Open Ocean. On the Svenu side the shores are fjord-steep and timbered; on the Southland side they are lower -- a rake of morainic bluffs, marshy fans, and barrier spits. A shallow sill at the gulf’s mouth tamps the tide but not the weather: northerly gales fling long swell down the reach, and winter katabatic bursts tumble off Svenu's highlands. The crossing is shorter than a sea-voyage yet never trivial, and the water gate keeps Svenu insular in habit and tone while giving the Southland a string of cold ports.

### Plains, soils, and ecotones

Closest to the range, fresh outwash gravels lie under conifers and hardy scrub; terraced orchards and vineyards take to warm morainic slopes where stones drink the day’s heat. The middle belt is fine alluvium -- loams and silts laid by the Swicca's historical belts -- veined by relict levees that stand dry in flood and serve as natural roads. Farther east the profile turns continental: heavier clays that crack to the wrist between floods, alkali patches where the river’s breath runs thin, and salt-flowered crusts round the inland sea. Reed-beds and shallow lagoons bloom in wet years; hardpan and dust prevail in lean ones.

### Winds and seasons

The Wolves' Teeth drive the sky. Föhn winds spill over in late winter and early spring -- dry, glass-clear, and deceptively warm -- melting cornices and bringing the Swicca up a fortnight early. From the south, polar air can surge and pin fogs along the lake belt while dust devils walk the outer plain. Summers are crisp under the inner wall with routine storm lines clapping against the peaks each afternoon; autumn on the plain goes bright, still, and dusty while the lake country turns steel and mirror.

### Behavior and hazards of the Swicca

The Swicca is punctual but not tame. It builds and abandons courses on a human scale, shifting its meander belt by leagues across generations. Breakup jams can loft flood crests above the natural levees and cut new mouths; summer convective bursts throw brown walls off the foothills; and the delta’s distributaries do not keep promises for long. Towns thrive on the active channel and wither when it wanders; canals and cutoffs are forever under spade and stake to hold the river's favor. On the inland sea, seiches run before pressure jumps and drive brine into fresh lagoons; when the lake shrinks, windblown dust from the exposed flats chokes the eastern marches.

### Passes, groundwater, and karst

Three principal passes cross the Teeth here, each feeding a long valley whose river is a trade road in waiting and a water ladder in fact. Their piedmonts cap generous aquifers: artesian springs lace the inner belt and keep mill channels turning when surface water sags. Karst fields in buried-ice terrain pock the outer moraines -- sinkholes, losing streams, and caves that breathe when the föhn drops, quietly moving water from the lake belt to the Swicca's middle reaches.

In this shape the Southland hangs together: a cold gulf guarding its western face, the Swicca gathering the mountain's memory and laying it east, and a land that ends not in surf but in the slow, salt breath of an inland sea.
