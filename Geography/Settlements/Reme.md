Though its exact location was lost for centuries due to the _damnatio memoriae_ enacted by the [[Eternal Dominion|crusaders]] and the very nature of its Fall, Reme at its height sat on the ash-bright, volcanic east coast of [[Ertra]], in the Ash Belt the moderns tiptoe around, where shield volcanoes step into the Circle Sea and the soil is abundantly fertile.

The foundation was a hill town: matrilineal cattle folk on an old welded-tuff ridge, ringed in terraces and low stone walls. Later chronicles hint at "mothers of the enclosures" and "keepers of the herds", making it fairly clear who was taken seriously in those early councils. Over centuries that little hill town drew in its rivals. Marriage compacts, water-sharing agreements, shared pasture and, eventually, shared rites brought the neighboring hill and river towns into cadence with it. This synoecism was not some overnight administrative coup, but three or four hundred years of patient collaboration -- "we will time the sowing together this year" turning into "we will mint together" and finally "we will honor each other under the same law".

When the city began to form an [[Remean Empire|empire]]'s heart, the Old Hill, with its ancestral precincts and herding shrines, remained as a raised, roughly oval acropolis near the center. Around it, and down toward the sea, the Remeans imposed order.

## The metropolitan plan

Imagine something with the social density of imperial [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome), but drawn up like [Heian-kyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heian-ky%C5%8D): straight lines wherever the ground allows, two grand axial ways framing the city, in three broad tiers rising from the harbour to the hinterland.

One great avenue ran inland east to west, from the sea gates to the Old Hill. It was straight, colonnaded, and wide enough for two legions to march in columns without brushing shoulders with the market stalls under the arcades. The other, crossing it at the Hill, ran north-south along the safer spines of bedrock, connecting the water-gathering upland districts to the harbor terraces below. Every other street was a rational child of those two: a grid, slightly skewed to respect the local rift lines and slopes, but still obsessively regular.

Blocks were not arbitrary rectangles. Their dimensions repeated certain proportions the Ancients clearly considered sacred, the same ratios that turn up in their surviving resonant chambers and the remaining Remean cisterns in [[City of Balboa|Balboa proper]] that still hum when you speak inside them. That geometry was more than aesthetics: It made for predictable, precise acoustics. A crowd in a Remean square could be quieted by one voice in the right place. A training cadence sung at one corner of a barracks court would carry evenly to every recruit.

## Population and fabric

At its peak in the high imperial age before the fall, the city held on the order of half a million regular citizens with easily as many transients, a number not seen again around the [[Circle Sea]] until [[Virell]] finally sprawled across the Varsa in the early Aetheric Age. Within the inner walls, the standard urban block was a hollow square of multi-storey insulae wrapped around a central court. The poor had the upper floors, breezier and closer to the haze; the rich had ground-level suites opening into shaded gardens, with carefully tuned pools and plantings chosen as much for acoustic dampening as for beauty.

Streets were paved in in fitted slabs with slightly convex profiles that sent the frequent volcanic drizzles into side channels; beneath, a lattice of cisterns, sewers, and vents carried water, waste, and safely directed volcanic outgassings. The volcanoes of Ertra's eastern coast did not and do not erupt once and go away; they constantly leak, exhale, and shift. Reme's civic engineering treated that as a given. Vent shafts used natural draughts and a subtle Rarefaction bias to breathe out fumes and steam at predictable corners, issuing from grilles cut and tuned to whistle if the gases thickened beyond tolerance. Where later Ertrese towns would throw up a spirit-shrine and hope, the Remeans had already found the harmonic profile of the hazard and built it into the pavement. 

The visual effect of these streets, to later eyes, would be unnervingly clean: smooth walls, painted inscriptions, and a remarkable absence of loose rubbish and beasts of burden. When a society has citizens who can carry twice their bodyweight all day without strain, what would you need them for? Shaping was universal civic expectation here, not a curiosity, and the apparent ease with which Reme's porters moved up and down the streets with four full casks slung on a balanced yoke would put modern dock cranes to shame.

The outer districts, wrapped around the inner city like a garland, held low-rise workshops, training yards, and staging grounds for the legions. Long rectangular barracks stood in matched pairs facing onto parade fields and "cadence gardens" where formation drills could be run along precise lines cut into the paving. Beyond them, the suburbs sank their feet into the first terrace bands: kitchen gardens, orchards, and then the proper agricultural girdle of the metropolis.

## Water, heat, and the volcanoes

Eastern Ertra is shield-volcano country. Lava leaks rather than explodes, calderas inflate and subside like lungs, and the ground is riddled with lava tubes and fumaroles. The Remeans did the only rational thing: they made that volatility part of the infrastructure.

Aqueducts from upland springs and impounded rift-valleys crossed younger flows on arcades whose piers were sunk not into glassy rubble, but into carefully tested "old ground" below. Where lava tubes remained open, they were bricked and smoothed into buried conduction galleries. Some carried water; others carried heat. Later cistern-builders approach them with a mixture of awe and professional jealousy.

Bathing complexes and winter quarters were set deliberately over such galleries, not just a civic luxury, but to serve as the city's main heat exchangers. Warm air rose through hypocaust channels; hazardous gases were trapped in "haze lofts" -- tall, empty chambers where they could be bled off safely. Haze bells on towers rang when gas fronts rolled down from the vents, and people simply shifted their activities indoors or to the upwind districts. The city did not fight the rhythm of the rifts; it scheduled around it.

## Harbors and sea-face

The city's maritime front was a crescent broken into three main basins. Two were inner harbors, cut into old lava deltas and ringed in high, almost theatrical quays. One was outer, open to the [[Circle Sea#^06ff45|Eastern Gyre]], with lower walls and long ramps for the beached hulls of polyremes between campaigns. Given the city's size in an age where most places were amazed to coax thirty thousand into one valley, those docks had to turn over grain, stone and people with almost brutal efficiency.

Lighthouses and signal towers bracketed the approaches: tall, stacked structures in which each gallery's pillars carried subtly different harmonic ratios. To an outside observer they might have seemed like decorative belts, but for the fact that they hummed in specific signal tones when lit -- an early exploitation of the same thermoacoustic principles that would give rise to [[Arlosian Engines|choir engines]] a full millennium later. Fire beacons and colored pennants handled the usual petty business of arrivals and departures, but resonance already did a large part of the work.

The quays themselves were extensions of the grid. Warehouses could be mobilized in record time, turning a quiet night harbor into an embarked legion with uncanny speed. Reme's conquest of the Merhal, when it came, was not won by flashy sorcery; it was won by the fact that they could put a fully supplied army on transports in days and have its resupply already scheduled weeks in advance, a logistical feat achieved centuries before the Theocracy learned to think in supply lines.

## The Shaped city

Modern [[The Light|Lurist]] doctrine would like to pretend that [[Shaping]] was an aberrant personal vice -- a convenient nonsense, for in Reme's heyday it was the civic norm and the scaffolding of daily life.

Every resident of citizen status appears to have undergone a sequence of carefully staged somatic reforms. The bodies that moved in those streets were perplexingly gynoid to the outside eye: wide hips, balanced shoulders, faces sculpted for poise, chests and voices tuned, everything in service of a balance that Lurist chroniclers later tried to read as "female" because they had no better category. But regardless of the clear aesthetic preferences, Reme's citizens were far from copies. The Shaping colleges upon the Old Hill and embedded in each civic ward helped people adapt to their roles: porters with stronger backs and steadier legs; scribes with fine motor control and detail attention; guardians whose balance, depth perception, and endurance were quite beyond unshaped norms.

The city's plan in fact blatantly assumed such bodies. Stairs and steps would have been a little taller than comfortable for modern Lavienese legs; street gradients in the steeper wards are just this side of absurd for a population without tendon-reinforced calves. Workloads would seem impossible if you assume unaugmented muscle and joints -- but nobody in Reme did. Between the gentle corrections of defects and health issues in young years, to the rite of passage of becoming a full citizen by undergoing the alignment with the civic ideal, no citizen -- from the labourer unloading a grain barge to the Capitolina sitting in council -- went without adaptations. The ethos was not, "be what you want", but "be tuned into the best possible version of yourself for the role you need to fill".

## Volcanic religion and civic cult

The east coast of Ertra today maintains small, slightly nervous shrines to [[Noble Element|Phlogistophor]] and the many elementals of mountain and fire, which the clergy tolerate because they mark safe ground and help keep the locals from doing anything catastrophically stupid. In Reme's time, those instincts were already in place, but dressed in a more elaborate civic robe.

The caldera behind the city was ringed in processional paths and stepped galleries cut into the tuff, used to watch the pulse of the rifts. The same acoustic ratios that shaped the streets and baths were used here to "hear" tremors and gas rumbles, to issue warnings well in advance of catastrophe.

Fire, generally, was at once beloved and feared, and there is some bitter irony in that, given how Reme fell in the end. The civic cult framed the Noble Element of Fire as one to be propitiated and balanced, and for most of its thousand-year reign the city managed that trick. Only when the [[Eternal Dominion]] came and its crusader forces had beaten Reme's stretched legions in the field so often that the empire had all but retreated to its ancient core on the peninsula did their desperation finally make them reach for the one thing they had always considered too dangerous to bind. The rest of that story is ash, and glass, and centuries of homilies warning against overreach.

## Hinterland and terraces

Reme's immediate hinterland was a ring of terraces: ash-belt fields, citrus groves, olive stairs, and vineyards clinging to old flows. The volcanic soils were absurdly fertile once weathered -- as modern surveyors understate it -- but also treacherous. Terraces were narrow, walled low but thick, and replanted often as the ground crept and settled. The rural districts were not peasant backwaters in the later medieval sense; they were simply the outermost organs of the same civic body.

Field hands and steadholders wore the same gynoid cast as city dwellers, but shaped for endurance in sun and on slope: skin tuned against sunburns, joints braced against slips, backs that did not break when you carried twin amphorae up a hillside. Grain and oil moved inward along a precise web of roads and canals, all tied to the metropolitan calendar and the sexagesimal accounting system that seeped from Reme into everyone else's measurements like dye in water.

Seen from above, the whole region would have looked indecently coherent: harbor, grid, terraces, caldera, and satellite towns all ticking along to the same cadence, all inhabited by bodies shaped towards an idealized base form that was as removed from the male as from the female, for all that it remained aesthetically gynoid. It is not terribly surprising that the crusaders viewed the place as a sort of moral contaminant.

## Aftermath

In the centuries since the Fall, everything that withstood the initial conflagration has passed from memory, and what remains of Reme is mostly marked by its absence. But at the height of empire -- the Merhal breadbasket paying its tithes, its old sorcerer-kings long since replaced by Remean assessors and engineers, the polyremes dominating the Gyres, Shaping colleges in peripheral ports copying the metropolitan ratios as best they could -- the city felt, and probably was, unassailable from without.

How fitting then that it fell at long last to one who rose within to exact the [[Fall of Reme|harshest of judgements]].
