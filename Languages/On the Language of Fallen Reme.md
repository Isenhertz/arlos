# Odion Remeanum -- the Remean Language

Remean is the historical language of the Remean Empire, reconstructed from a small corpus preserved in a hostile Antidiscordian chronicle of the Long War, words embedded in surviving vocabulary, a handful of loanwords into later languages, and whatever residue can be teased out of Balboan, the one living language carrying genuine Remean descent. No grammar, dictionary, or continuous text survives. Everything below is considerably more complete than the working reconstruction of in-world scholars, but even so it is held only with confidence proportional to the evidence behind it, and open to revision.

---
## Phonology

| Phoneme                 | Spelling                                      |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| /k/                     | *c*                                           |
| /kʷ/                    | *qu*                                          |
| /w/                     | *v*                                           |
| /ks/                    | *x*                                           |
| Vowel length (ː)        | circumflex (â, ê, î, ô, û)                    |
| Diphthong offglide (◌̯) | unmarked -- written as a plain vowel sequence |

### Repertoire

**Word-initial consonants and clusters:** *k p m s d z w f r n l pr h t kʷ g b tr fr pl sp st gr kr br fl kl sk gl*

**Word-medial consonants and clusters:** *r t n d k b z m l nt p ss kʷ g ll w rt kt nd s gn ks st ns mp lt nk str f mkʷ kk skʷ rr mm rk sk tr tt nf ng nst rm pt rw h mn gr pp rs bs nkʷ sp nn nw ps rf lw rn ff rb rp bl dw kstr lg ntr rg br bt dd mb nsp nstr rd gm ksp kspl ls mpl ppl dkʷ dm bb gg ksk mpr nl sd ts kkʷ kr kssp kst lk ln lp ltr mw nfl nfr ngr nkl nkr nskr pl ppr pr rkʷ rsp sm stkʷ tkʷ ttr*

**Word-final consonants and clusters:** *s m t nt r k d ks n ns l b nk st ps rs*

**Vowels and diphthongs:** *i e a u o iː eː aː uː oː ae̯ ui̯ au̯ ei̯ eu̯ oe̯ io̯ iu̯*

Grammatical suffixation can produce word-final clusters beyond this native inventory where a case or class marker attaches to an already consonant-final root that don't otherwise occur in bare roots. This is ordinary cross-morphemic clustering, not a gap in the root phonotactics above.

Remean avoids /sk/ where it would sit between /k/ or /kʷ/ on one side and another /k/ on the other: *Quis-* (itself opening in /kʷ/) forces a following /k/ to assimilate backward into the preceding /s/ as a geminate: *Quis- + Care → Quissare*, not *\*Quiscare*.

## Vocabulary

### Proper Names

- **Amaregis Ruquarestris** -- the Remean name for the Noble Element of Water, "the one who makes things go round." *Ruquarestris* is the *-(s)tris* agentive of *Ruquare* ("to make round") -- see Verbs. *Amaregis* itself is the reconstructed original; cf. the later rendition Maregina.
- **Anabis Cuneia** -- the Remean name for the Noble Element of Ice, "Anabis of the Wedge." *Cuneia* personifies *Cuneium*, "wedge" -- see Grammar, Productive Markers, Characterization. *Anabis* itself is the reconstructed original; cf. the later rendition Anapagonia.
- **Aralaris Rubicessa** -- the Remean name for Phlogistophor, Noble Element of Fire, read in Remean theology as Ambigen, consistent with the empire's convergence-ideal extended to the Noble Elements. Kenning: Hearthkeeper -- rendered in full as *Vinastolis Ignitonerum*, "Great Warden of the Hearths" (*Vinastis*, "warden," + augmentative *-ol-* + *Igniton*, "hearth," Genitive Plural *-erum*). *Rubicessa*, "the Reddest," is the superlative-personification-erosion of *Rubicum*, "red" -- see *Sarissa*, and Grammar, Productive Markers, Characterization.
- **Erta Rubicessa** -- "Mount Rubicessa", the main shield volcano upon whose slopes Remea was built.
- **Lapiatris Ertelema** -- the Remean name for the Noble Element of Stone, "the one who manifests mountains." *Ertelema* compounds *Ertas* ("mountain") and *Eleme* ("to manifest"), personified directly -- see Grammar, Productive Markers, Characterization. *Lapiatris* itself is the reconstructed original; cf. the later calque Piatros.
- **Lur** -- the Prophet, attested only obliquely, via the genitive *Luris* in the phrase *fidion Luris*, "the cult of Lur" (see *fidion*, below).
- **Maêra Ventia** -- the Remean name for Morwynt, Noble Element of Air. The personification of *Maêras* "lightning" and *Ventis* "wind" -- see Grammar, Productive Markers, Characterization.
- **Remea** -- the mother-city itself.
- **Seziona** -- "the Six (Noble Elements); the world; the cosmos". *Sez* ("six") + collective *-ion* = *Sezion*, "the group of six", personified into First Animate.
- **Solia Eicristris** -- the Remean name for the Noble Element of Soil, "the one who creates life." *Solia* personifies *Solis*, "fertile earth" -- see Grammar, Productive Markers, Characterization. *Eicristris* is the *-(s)tris* agentive of *Eicrie* ("to create life," *Eia* + *Crie*). an otherwise unattested form.
- **Valabuium** -- the cape region combining a harbor and a hillfort, back-formed to a singular *-ium* toponym (compare *Zaemarium*) from the regular descriptive phrase *statia vala buia*, "the stations between the currents" (*Statia*, "stations" + *Vala*, "between" + *Buia*, Acc. Pl. of *Buium*, "current"). The lexicalized form *Valabuia*, "the posts of the Valabuium," for the two settlements' eventual union gave rise to modern Balboa; the descent *Valabuia* → *Balboa* shows loss of the medial syllable and a shift of initial *V* to *B*.
- **Zaemarium** -- "the Merhal," "the land beyond the sea" -- a toponym in the register of "Outremer." *Zae-* ("beyond") + *Mar-* (the root of *Maris*, "sea") + *-ium*.
- **Zuabis, Azabis, Ilivia, Raesavis, Iovita, Cassis** -- personal names of properly Harmonized citizens of the metropolitan core, Long War era. *Zuabis* is very likely built on *Zuabin*, "blood" -- "the Bloody one; the sanguine one," a kenning-style name (see *Zuabin*, Nouns).

### Nouns

- **Abans, Abante** -- "possession; property". 
- **Abeicion, Abeicionis** -- animate: "herd; livestock" (the actual animals).
- **Abeicion, Abeicionis** -- inanimate: "wealth; chattels; estate" (the pastoral concept of owning livestock translating into a measure of wealth).
- **Abestris, Abestritis** -- "owner".
- **Abestrium, Abestriî** -- "ownership".
- **Adivium, Adiviî** -- "grease; fat".
- **Aer, Aerei** -- "air; gas," specifically static, ambient air, distinct from *Ventis*, air in motion. In a volcanic region prone to poisonous haze, *Aer* carries a real sense of danger that *Ventis* doesn't (see *Fluaer*). Survives as a loanword into later languages.
- **Aifos, Aifoti** -- "soap."
- **Aina, Ainae** -- "wife". The familiar in-group register for a married partner, contrasted with the formal *Aionaris*. In the Harmonized and ambigen Remean society, this term meant both husband and wife, but the female term is used in glossing.  
- **Aion, Aionis** -- animate: "couple" (the people themselves). *Ai-* + collective *-ion*, "the group who loves."
- **Aion, Aionis** -- inanimate: "relationship" (the bond, rather than the people in it).
- **Aionaris, Aionaritis** -- "spouse". The outsider term for a married person's partner.
- **Aistris, Aistritis** -- "lover" (*ai-* + agentive).
- **Aistrium, Aistriî** -- "the act of loving; care".
- **Aitis, Aititis** -- "beloved; darling; sweetie".
- **Aium, Aiî** -- "love" (the emotion).
- **Alacolis, Alacolitis** -- "great-awl; rapier; estoc". The long fencing weapon of true brass.
- **Alax, Alacis** -- "awl; needle." Shares its root, *Ala-*, with *Alare*, "to sew."
- **Alilan, Alilenis** -- "pin" (Fourth Declension, bare *-n*). Diminutive of *Alax*, "awl; needle" -- "little awl".
- **Alium, Aliî** -- "oil."
- **Allapium, Allapiî** -- "lava", specifically the molten rock held in the cauldron.
- **Allapton, Allaptonis** -- "volcanic cauldron".
- **Alvis, Alvitis** -- "an Alvai," Remean calque of the Kin's endonym built directly on *Alvu*, "White" -- fitting, considering the fact that Reme encountered the Kins of Sun and Moon first, which tend to run fair-haired and even silvery on occasion in contrast to the dark hair of the Remeans. *Alva*, the highest-tier reverence version, is rare but independently attested, likely a coinage of the late Remean Era after the Moonkin under Badhiga had repelled the legions from the slopes of the Lupines.
- **Alvix, Alvissis** -- "belly" (lower part of body, abdomen). Animate on visible internal vitality rather than active expression -- shifting organs, digestion, and pregnancy all go on inside it, and belching and belly-grumbles are its own outward signs of that ongoing activity.
- **Alvu, Alvû** -- "the color white" (noun). Cf. *Alvicum* (adjective) and *Alvis* (the Alvai ethnonym).
- **Amas, Amati** -- "rain" (noun).
- **Andix, Andissis** -- "goose."
- **Angas, Angati** -- "boot; shoe."
- **Arafluan, Arafluanis** -- "rough, clinkery, rubbly lava with broken chunks"
- **Aralaeros, Aralaeroti** -- "volcanic vent; fumarole"
- **Aran, Arenis** -- "area; region."
- **Arca, Arcae** -- "bow" -- both the weapon category and its basic form.
- **Arcola, Arcolae** -- "giant-bow". The attested belly-bow, a cousin of the real-world gastraphetes, usable only by a Shaped legionary. Augmentative of *Arca*, infix *-ol-*.
- **Aris, Aritis** -- "doer; agent; active party" -- *are*'s own irregular agentive, standing alone with no noun attached.
- **Aristrium, Aristriî** -- "action".
- **Arva, Arvae** -- the neutral-register kinship term for the bearing-parent role. *Avva* and *Abba* are eroded forms of the same word, worn down through informal and childhood/endearment use respectively.
- **Arvila, Arvilae** -- "aunt" -- the bearing-parent's sibling, treated kinship-wise as a parent. Diminutive of *Arva*, "little/dear Arva" (infix *-il-*), in its endearment sense. Also does double duty as a more neutral third-person form of address, extended to an older woman of no blood relation -- much as English "auntie" is used for a family term stretched to a stranger.
- **Arvola, Arvolae** -- augmentative of Arva, "great mother; matriarch; head of the family" (infix *-ol-*).
- **Astilion, Astilionis** -- "constellation."
- **Astilium, Astiliî** -- "star". *Ast-* ("to sparkle") + diminutive *-il-* + *-ium* -- "the little sparkly thing."
- **Astilimagium, Astilimagiî** -- "astronomy".
- **Atra, Atrae** -- "bear." Literally "the black one," *Atr-* (the root of *Atru*) + bare animate *-a*, the same personification pattern as *Zulva*.
- **Atranas, Atranati** -- "black sand".
- **Atrias, Atriati** -- "obsidian".
- **Atrimen, Atriminis** -- "iron".
- **Atru, Atrû** -- "the color black" (noun). Cf. *Atricum*, the adjective.
- **Aurea, Aureae** -- one of the three named moons, "Aural" in modern times.
- **Autarx, Autarcis** -- "back; backside".
- **Autrex, Autrecis** -- "back (of the body)"; also polite for "buttocks."
- **Avans, Avante** -- "ripple; disturbance"
- **Avium, Aviî** -- "wave." Cf. *Ave* "to ripple; to undulate"
- **Baris, Baritis** -- "fox."
- **Belana, Belanae** -- one of the three named moons. Namesake of the Belanar calendar.
- **Belaninas, Belaninati** -- "Belanar pass". A calendrical period reckoned by that moon, on the same pattern as *Eminas*, below.
- **Belanion, Belanionis** -- "galley; sexaginty." The root here is *belan* "60" rather than the moon, refering to the standard crew size of the Remean galleys that then lent its name to a tactical unit of 60 legionaries.
- **Belenos, Belenoti** -- "season."
- **Benicas, Benicati** -- "kindness."
- **Biabelanion, Biabelanionis** -- "double-galley." The standard of the Remean navy, a monoreme galley of three rowers per oar on forty oars.
- **Bracimen, Braciminis** -- "bronze."
- **Branilis, Branilitis** -- "calf; heifer."
- **Branimarix, Branimarissis** -- "female cow" (specifically). *Bran-* + linking *-i-* + *Marix* ("dam") -- exemplary of the general breeding-pair compounding pattern (species root + *-i-* + *Marix*/*Massix*).
- **Branimassix, Branimassissis** -- "bull" (specifically). *Bran-* + linking *-i-* + *Massix* ("sire") -- the male half of the same compounding pattern as *Branimarix*.
- **Branis, Branitis** -- "cow; bull" (general, unspecified).
- **Brinta, Brintae** -- "older cousin." Affectionate *Brinna* (*-nt- → -nn-*, the general kinship gemination pattern) -- see Grammatical Structure.
- **Bruca, Brucae** -- "mouth".
- **Buium, Buiî** -- "current" (of water). Underlies *Valabuium* -- see Proper Names.
- **Calertix, Calertissis** -- "volcano."
- **Canis, Canitis** -- "dog". Grammatically animate: dogs clear the behavioral-complexity bar that lice and fish don't.
- **Capilium, Capiliî** -- "hat; cap." Literally "little head-thing" -- *Cap-* (the root of *Capis*) + diminutive *-il-* + *-ium*. Shifts to First Declension inanimate rather than preserving *Capis*'s Second Declension animate, the same way *Nexilum* does against *Nexos* -- the diminutive here names an artifact resembling the base noun rather than a smaller instance of the same referent, and that distinction seems to be what licenses the declension shift.
- **Capis, Capitis** -- "head" (anatomic). Animate not by personhood, behavioral complexity, or precaution, but because the head carries outsized weight in communication and the visible expression of will -- see *Vis* ("eye") for the parallel case, and the animacy note under Grammatical Structure.
- **Capitoleton, Capitoletonis** -- "council hall; (physical) seat of government". *Capitol-* (the root of *Capitolium*) + epenthetic *-e-* + place-of *-ton* -- the same pattern as *Legeton* and *Fideton*.
- **Capitolina, Capitolinae** -- "councilor; magistrate," from *Capitolium* ("council; government") plus the noun-to-animate-agent marker *-na* (see Productive Markers).
- **Capitolion, Capitolionis** -- "the magistracy," as the people making up the ruling class. No inanimate form is attested, which seems to have been taken wholly by *Capitolium*, below. 
- **Capitolium, Capitoliî** -- "council; government," the inanimate institution; cf. *Capitolina*. Analyzable as *Capis* ("head") + a linking *-t-* + the augmentative infix *-ol-* + the First Inanimate *-ium* -- literally "the great head," the augmented, collective seat of authority.
- **Capolicas, Capolicati** -- "arrogance." Quality-noun of *Capolicum*, "-um" replaced with "-as."
- **Capolium, Capoliî** -- "dome; cupola."
- **Cautium, Cautiî** -- "hole; ditch; furrow"
- **Cavolitium, Cavolitiî** -- "cave; cavern."
- **Cifluan, Cifluenis** -- "smooth, ropy, unbroken flowing lava".
- **Cissaemen, Cissaeminis** -- "quicksilver".
- **Cedix, Cedissis** -- "louse".
- **Cenex, Cenecis** -- "knee".
- **Cidestrilis, Cidestrilitis** -- "stiletto" -- a special knife carried in the sheath of the larger *suatra* ("sword").
- **Cidestris, Cidestritis** -- "killer; assassin". The regular *-(s)tris* agentive of *Cidere* ("to kill"), exactly parallel to *Fidestris*.
- **Cimmas, Cimmati** -- "ash-fall".
- **Cinos, Cinoti** -- "ash(es)".
- **Coduax, Coduacis** -- "tail".
- **Collegina, Colleginae** -- "co-worker; compatriot" (*Collegion*, in its institutional sense, + the animate-agent marker *-na*).
- **Collegion, Collegionis** -- animate: "the group working together; colleagues" (the people). *College* ("to work together") + collective *-ion*.
- **Collegion, Collegionis** -- inanimate: "a governing body" (the institution) -- a governing body under the old Capitolinae. Survives as a loanword into later languages.
- **Collegion formatrium** -- "collegiate of the Shapers" or "collegiate of Shaping." Ambiguous between First Inanimate nominative singular and Second Animate genitive plural -- see *Formatrium*, below, for the fuller analysis.
- **Comilos, Comiloti** -- "comb." Diminutive of *Comare*'s own root, *Coma-* ("rake") -- "little rake." Underlies *Comilare*, "to comb."
- **Corium, Coriî** -- "heart" -- not treated under the active-expression animacy criterion; a heart, unlike the tongue or hand, doesn't outwardly express anything, and the Remeans seem to have conceptually placed a person's lifeforce in the gut and head instead of the heart.
- **Corvicilium, Corviciliî** -- "necklace; gorget; torq." Diminutive of *Corvos*, "neck" -- "little neck-thing."
- **Corvos, Corvoti** -- "neck".
- **Crians, Criante** -- "product; creation; result". *Cri-* + epenthetic *-a-* + product-of *-ns*, the same Class C epenthesis as *Magians*.
- **Crinolium, Crinoliî** -- "fur; pelt." Augmentative of *Crinum*, preserving declension exactly like *Arva → Arvola* -- "big/extended hair," an animal's full coat rather than human head-hair.
- **Crinum, Crinî** -- "hair" (on head of humans).
- **Cristrium, Cristriî** -- "production; process". *Cri- + s + trium*, the front vowel *-i-* triggering the *-s-* allomorphy, the same pattern as *Fidestrium* and *Aistrium*.
- **Cubesaton, Cubesatonis** -- "territory" (Fourth Declension, inanimate). Literally "the place that is being ruled" -- *Cubesare* (the Affective of *Cubere*, "to rule") + *-ton*, the *-r-* eliding before the consonant-initial suffix.
- **Cuneium, Cuneiî** -- "wedge". See *Anabis Cuneia* (Proper Names) for the personified epithet built on the same root.
- **Dabis, Dabitis** -- "eater" (agentive). *Dab-* (the root of *Dabe*, "to eat") + *-is*. Underlies *Volidabis*, "elephant."
- **Dêcis, Dêcitis** -- "mind" (the faculty, distinct from *Magium*, "knowledge"). *Dêc-* (the root of *Dêce*) + *-is*, animate on the same seat-of-cognition logic as *Capis*, *Vis*, and *Zunca*.
- **Den, Dinis** -- "sky." possibly related to *Din* "above".
- **Dentas, Dentati** -- "tooth" (front, rather than molar).
- **Dentolas, Dentolati** -- "molar." Augmentative of *Dentas*, "big tooth."
- **Descitrax, Descitracis** -- "equinox."
- **Dias, Diati** -- "day".
- **Dinamium, Dinamiî** -- "impeller; stone-lance." A class of Remean siege weapon that worked on aetheric principles, accelerating a slug along a trough through direct modulation of Motility, activated through rhythmic triggering of successive stage. These went from small devices using two strikers to massive anti-siege installations utilizing several squads. Outsider sources often referred to these as stone-lances, a gloss also provided here.
- **Dirium, Diriî** -- "glory."
- **Dômen, Dôminis** -- "bronze".
- **Dromix, Dromissis** -- "camel."
- **Eia, Eiae** -- "life; vital force" (First Animate). *E-* ("to be") + *-ia*, personified directly the same way *Ventia* is -- "the being-one," animate by default.
- **Eicaris, Eicaritis** -- "herder; stablehand." *Eic-* (the root of *Eix*) + *-aris*, the same occupational-agentive pattern as *Legionaris*.
- **Eiceton, Eicetonis** -- "stable; stall" (Fourth Declension, inanimate). *Eic-* + *-eton*, "place-of".
- **Eicrinium, Eicriniî** -- "fertile ash-soil; farmland".
- **Eis, Eitis** -- "person; sentient being; human." Same origin as *Eix* -- attested as a more neutral term for a non-citizen, carrying none of the implied immaturity of *Peris*.
- **Eix, Eissis** -- "being; lifeform; animal." *Ei-* ("living thing") + *-ix*.
- **Elemens, Elemente** -- "one of the elements" (inanimate matter, the "mote" stage). *Elem-* + epenthetic *-e-* + product-of *-ns*, the same pattern as *Formans* and *Fidens*.
- **Elementilis, Elementiliis** -- "a sprite" -- specifically the minor elementals of animal-level intellect, in contrast to the more human-like courtiers. Diminutive of *Elementis*, infix *-il-* inserted before the animate *-is* ending (*Element-il-is*).
- **Elementis, Elementitis** -- "an elemental" -- the broad term, encompassing all sentient elementals, sprite and courtier alike. *Elemens* + the animate marker *-is*, the same *-(a)ns → -(a)ntis* animacy shift already established for *Modisarentis*.
- **Elemês, Elemium** -- "ancestors" (*plurale tantum*). Formally the regular plural of *Elemis*, "soul" -- the difference between "souls" and "ancestors" is usually contextually clear.
- **Elemilis, Elemilitis** -- "ghost." Diminutive of *Elemis*, "a lesser/faded soul" -- the same diminutive-for-lesser-being logic as *Elementilis*.
- **Elemis, Elemitis** -- "soul" (the human soul specifically, distinct from *Elementis*). *Elem-* (the root of *Eleme*, "to manifest; to embody") + *-is*.
- **Eleton, Eletonis** -- "plain; field."
- **Ella, Ellae** -- "sun". Animate on cosmological/elemental grounds -- see the animacy note under Grammatical Structure.
- **Ellanas, Ellanati** -- "year". From *Ellanum* ("solar") via the quality-noun *-as* -- "the quality of being related to the sun."
- **Ellas, Ellatis** -- "brightness; flash; lightning (distant, without thunder)".
- **Ellestas, Ellestati** -- "sunstead; solstice".
- **Ellimen, Elliminis** -- "gold".
- **Emis, Emitis** -- "moon(s)," the general term. Arlos has three named moons -- *Aurea*, *Langea*, *Belana* -- each individually First Animate; *Emis* covers the general concept or an unspecified moon.
- **Eminas, Eminati** -- "Aureal month". *Em-* (the root of *Emis*) + *-inum* "relating to the Moon" + *-as* "quality of relating to the Moon", same pattern as _Ellanas_. Reckoned by *Aurea*, implicitly the primary moon for calendrical purposes.
- **Enta, Entae** -- "older sibling". Affectionate *Enna* (*-nt- → -nn-*, the general kinship-gemination pattern).
- **Eoloressium, Eoloressiî** -- "greed". Cf. *Eoloressicum*, "greedy" -- see Adjectives.
- **Erau, Eravu** -- "choice."
- **Erimen, Eriminis** -- "brass." Specifically the "true brass" from purified zinc, the secret of which was a monopoly of Reme. Cemented brass was known as *erimen aracum* or "unrefined brass."
- **Ertas, Ertati** -- "mountain" (not hill). Very likely the toponymic root behind *Ertra*.
- **Ertium, Ertiî** -- "summit; top; the thing at the height".
- **Erx, Ercis** -- "dust."
- **Esmium, Esmiî** -- "feeling" (First Declension inanimate). *Esm-* (the root of *Esme*, "to feel") + *-ium*. Underlies *Esmicum* and, further, *Tenesmicum* -- see Adjectives.
- **Essix, Essissis** -- "donkey."
- **Esterdinium, Esterdiniî** -- "firmament; night-sky"
- **Esternovas, Esternovati** -- "foolishness." Quality-noun of *Esternovum*, "-um" replaced with "-as."
- **Estertoton, Estertotonis** -- "trireme; prime cohort." A double-strength cohort of 720 soldiers, necessary to crew the empire's line triremes that outperformed Anish sixes and sevens for centuries.
- **Falvu, Falvû** -- "the color yellow" (noun). Cf. *Falvicum*, the adjective.
- **Fidens, Fidente** -- "belief; dogma" (*fid-* + epenthetic *-e-* + *-nt*).
- **Fidestrina, Fidestrinae** -- "cleric" (*Fidestrium* + the animate-agent marker *-na*).
- **Fidestris, Fidestritis** -- "believer" (*fid-* + epenthetic *-e-* + agentive).
- **Fidestrium, Fidestriî** -- "faith" (*fid-* + epenthetic *-e-* + *-s-* + *-trium*, "act of believing").
- **Fideton, Fidetonis** -- "temple; shrine" (*fid-* + epenthetic *-e-* + *-ton*).
- **Fidion, Fidionis** -- animate: "the congregation" (the assembled believers). *Fid-* + collective *-ion*, "the group who believes."
- **Fidion, Fidionis** -- inanimate: "cult; church" (the institution; the faith itself). Attested in the phrase *fidion Luris*, "the cult of Lur."
- **Finautas, Finautati** -- "rope; braid; plait." Plausibly the quality-noun of an implied resultative *\*Finautum*, "twisted," built on *Finave*.
- **Finilans, Finilante** -- "thread." *Finau-* ("to spin") + diminutive *-il-* + product-of *-ans* -- "the little spun thing," the thin counterpart to *Finautas*, "rope." A heavy vowel elision of expected but unattested *\*Finauvilans*. 
- **Firda, Firdae** -- "horse."
- **Firdix, Firdissis** -- "mule." Shares its root, *Fird-*, with *Firda* -- fitting, given a mule is a horse cross.
- **Fisicas, Fisicati** -- "cruelty." Quality-noun of *Fisicum*, "-um" replaced with "-as."
- **Flaricium, Flariciî** -- "ointment." *Flaric-* (the root of *Flaricum*, "fragrant") + *-ium* -- "the fragrant thing," distinguishing a scented preparation from plain *Flaros* itself.
- **Flaros, Flaroti** -- "fragrance; perfume." Cf. *Flaricum*, the adjective.
- **Fluacrinum, Fluacrinî** -- "lava-hair", volcanic glass fibers stretched out by escaping gas.
- **Fluaer, Fluaerei** -- "steam", especially the corrosive steam from lava meeting seawater.
- **Fluavens, Fluavente** -- "eruption; boilover", the effusive eruptions exhibited by the shield volcanoes of Ertra's east coast.
- **Fluans, Fluante** -- "(moving) water; flow; stream; river".
- **Fluis, Fluitis** -- "Water" (the element).
- **Fluvium, Fluviî** -- "(standing) water; well; pond".

### Numerals

Each number has three distinct forms: an abstract citation form for the number-concept itself (bare root, indeclinable), a counting/adnominal form that agrees with the noun it quantifies, and an ordinal adjective. The citation forms below are the adnominal/ordinal pair's own Nominative/Accusative Singular, not an invariant shape. Formation from the abstract root is irregular and lexicalized -- see Grammar, Numerals, for the surface tendencies and the correlation between the two derived forms.

- **Un** / **Unum** / **Unicum** -- 1 / one / first.
- **Bia** / **Biavum** / **Biacum** -- 2 / two / second.
- **Trae** / **Traevium** / **Traecum** -- 3 / three / third.
- **Catra** / **Catrium** / **Catricum** -- 4 / four / fourth.
- **Quin** / **Quinum** / **Quincum** -- 5 / five / fifth.
- **Sez** / **Sezum** / **Sezicum** -- 6 / six / sixth.
- **Zeben** / **Zebium** / **Zebicum** -- 7 / seven / seventh.
- **Octra** / **Octrium** / **Octricum** -- 8 / eight / eighth.
- **Noa** / **Noavum** / **Noacum** -- 9 / nine / ninth.
- **Dezen** / **Dezum** / **Dezicum** -- 10 / ten / tenth.
- **Evin** / **Evium** / **Evicum** -- 11 / eleven / eleventh.
- **Zel** / **Zelum** / **Zelicum** -- 12 / twelve / twelfth.
- **Zelun** / **Zelunum** / **Zelunicum** -- 13 / thirteen / thirteenth. *Zel* ("twelve") + *Un* ("one"), "twelve-one." Regular adnominal/ordinal formation begins here -- see Grammar, Numerals, for the full multiplicative-additive system past twelve.
- **Belan** -- 60 / sixty. An independently named unit, not a multiple of *Zel* -- compare *Belana*, the moon on a stable and predictable 60-day cycle. Underlies *Biabelan* (120), *Catrabelan* (240), and further multiples -- see Grammar, Numerals.
- **Tot** -- 360 / three hundred sixty (*60 × 6*). An independently named unit, compare *Totum*, "all" -- a full cycle of *Belan* itself. Underlies *Quintot* (1800) and further multiples -- see Grammar, Numerals.

### Adjectives

Adjectives take a dedicated citation-form marker, *-um*, distinct from the ordinary noun-forming First Inanimate *-ium* -- see Grammar, Adjectives, for the reasoning.

- **Acinum** -- "near." Also used in the temporal sense (Cf. *Acinicer* "soon"), alongside its superlative *Acinescum* as "next."
- **Aeranum** -- "of the air; pertaining to air" (*aer* + *-anum*, "relating to").
- **Aericum** -- "airy" (*aer* + *-icum*, "having the quality of").
- **Aitum** -- "beloved" (*ai-* + *-tum*).
- **Alanum** -- "other; another."
- **Allinum** -- "smooth (tactile); flat; even; uniform; (metaphorically) uneventful."
- **Alum** -- "some."
- **Alvicum** -- "white."
- **Amplum** -- "big."
- **Artum** -- "done; completed." The irregular resultative of *Are* ("to do").

- **Benicum** -- "kind". *Ben-* (the root of *Benum*) + *-icum*, a distinct derived sense from the base adjective, specifically the goodness one shows toward others. Cf. *Benicas*, "kindness."
- **Benum** -- "good." Comparative *Benios*, superlative *Benescum*.
- **Bitrum** -- "acidic" (fermented sourness: yogurt, vinegar).
- **Borum** -- "rough (visual); uneven; unlevel; defective".
- **Buarum** -- "slow".
- **Capolicum** -- "arrogant".
- **Cissum** -- "quick; swift; fast."
- **Cratum** -- "hard."
- **Descum** -- "same."
- **Dinamicum** -- "animated; mobile; motile"
- **Eium** -- "fertile; fecund; life-giving"
- **Elenum** -- "smooth (visual); glossy; sleek; polished; refined." A possible thematic proximity to *Ellum* ("bright") and *Ella* ("sun"), of unclear origin.
- **Ellanum** -- "solar; relating to the sun." *Ell-* (the root of *Ella*) + *-anum*. Cf. *Ellanas*, "year" -- see Nouns.
- **Ellitum** -- "light" (in color).
- **Ellum** -- "bright." Root *Ell-*, the same root as *Ella*, "sun."
- **Embiavum** -- "ambigen; androgyn; hermaphroditic." *Em-* + *Biavum* (the adnominal "two") -- "the two-having one." A dignified descriptor for the Harmonized citizenry's converged nature.
- **Eoloressicum** -- "greedy". Cf. *Eoloressium*, "greed" -- see Nouns.
- **Ertanum** -- "high." *Ert-* (the root of *Ertas*) + *-anum*, the same derivation as *Ellanum* from *Ella*.
- **Esmicum** -- "sensitive."
- **Esternovum** -- "foolish". *Ester-* (intensive) + *Novum* ("new") -- "much too new," ie, lacking experience. Cf. *Esternovas*, "foolishness."
- **Falvicum** -- "yellow." From *Falvu* ("the color yellow") + *-icum*, same vowel-elision pattern.
- **Femilum** -- "female." Diminutive (infix *-il-*) of an unattested plain *Femum*. Used of breeding animals.
- **Fisicum** -- "cruel". *Fis-* (the root of *Fisum*) + *-icum*. Cf. *Fisicas*, "cruelty."
- **Fisum** -- "bad; evil."
- **Flaricum** -- "fragrant." *Flar-* (the root of *Flaros*) + *-icum*.
- **Fontanum** -- "child-engendering; paternal; male", the counterpart to *Arvinum*. Contrast *Mascilum*.
- **Formatum** -- "formed" (*forma-* + *-tum*, no epenthesis).
- **Fretum** -- "blunt" (no edge present) -- contrasted with *Resuatum*, "dull" (an edge lost).
- **Frigum** -- "cold" (of weather).
- **Iastum** -- "bitter."
- **Ilum** -- "several."
- **Legerum** -- "aggressive; combative" (*leg-* + epenthetic *-e-* + tendency *-rum*).
- **Legionaranum** -- "relating to legionaries" (*legionar-* + *-anum*).
- **Legionaricum** -- "like a legionary" (*legionar-*, the root of *legionaris*, + *-icum*).
- **Limicum** -- "sour (natural sourness: fruit); tangy; tart." Cf. *Limos*, "citrus."
- **Lubum** -- "soft."
- **Magicum** -- "learned; knowledgeable". *Magi-* (the root of *Magium*) + *-icum*. Cf. *Magicas*, "knowledgeability; sophistication."
- **Mascilum** -- "male." Possibly the diminutive (infix *-il-*) of an unattested plain *Mascum*, but cf. *Mascix* "sire; breeding male."
- **Midicum** -- "blue."
- **Miduranum** -- "pertaining to flax." *Midur-* + *-anum*, the same relational-adjective derivation as *Ellanum*. Attested in *alium miduranum*, "linseed oil."
- **Miduricum** -- "made of linen." *Midur-* (the same alternate stem) + *-icum*.
- **Moium** -- "wet; damp." Root *Moi-*.
- **Murum** -- "dark" (in color).
- **Nalum** -- "any." *Ne* + *Alum* ("not-some").
- **Nannum** -- "recent; fresh; not previously there; current, of-the-moment" -- distinct from *Novum*'s "brand new".
- **Necum** -- "no; none."
- **Niavum** -- "sweet."
- **Novum** -- "new" (inanimate agreement). Animate agreement, *Novis*, glosses as "young" instead: "lacking age" reads as *new* for an object and *young* for a living being.
- **Octatricum** -- "victorious." *Octatr-* (the root of *Octatris*) + *-icum*.
- **Olectum** -- "malodorous." Lexicalized resultative of *Olecare* ("to stink") -- the regular form would predict *\*Olecatum*.
- **Ormietum** -- "asleep; resting."
- **Pecolum** -- "heavy."
- **Platum** -- "filled." The regular resultative *-tum* form of *Plare* ("to fill") -- the literal event-result, distinct from *Pletum*, "full," below.
- **Pletum** -- "full." An irregular, lexicalized resultative of *Plare*, vowel-shifted from the regular *Platum* -- see Grammar, Conjugation, for the *Platum*/*Pletum* distinction.
- **Polium** -- "many."
- **Porrum** -- "far; distant; faraway." Cf. *Porras*, "distance; periphery; hinterland."
- **Quericum** -- "easeful; peaceful; calm; serene."
- **Quesum** -- "each; every" (distributive, contrasted with the collective *Totum* and the mass quantifier *Polium*).
- **Redêcicum** -- "unwise". *Redêc-* (the root of the unattested *\*Redêce*, "to un-think") + *-icum*. Cf. *Redêcans*, "folly" -- see Nouns.
- **Remeanum** -- "Remean; relating to Remea." *Reme-* + *-anum*, root left fully intact.
- **Resuatum** -- "dull" (an edge lost). *Re-* ("reversal") + *Suatum* ("sharp") -- "un-sharp."
- **Rissicrinicum** -- "woollen." *Rissicrin-* (the root of *Rissicrinum*) + *-icum*.
- **Ruccarum** -- "generous."
- **Ruquum** -- "round."
- **Rubicum** -- "red." From *Rubu* ("the color red") + *-icum*, with the root's final vowel eliding before the suffix (*Rubu → Rubicum*, not *\*Rubuicum*).
- **Sarum** -- "pointed." Cf. *Sarissa*, "long-spear; pike," via the superlative -- see Nouns.
- **Selcum** -- "salty." *Selc-* (*Selx*'s own oblique stem) + *-um*.
- **Senianum** -- "wise; insightful". *Seni-* (from *Senium*, "old age") + *-anum* -- "relating to old age." Cf. *Senianas*, "wisdom; insight."
- **Senum** -- "old."
- **Sicum** -- "dry" (substance).
- **Situm** -- "light" (in weight).
- **Sontolum** -- "loud; blaring; noisy."
- **Spunicum** -- "hempen." *Spunic-* (the root of *Spunix*) + *-cum*, the doubled *-c-* simplifying to one.
- **Stadum** -- "upright; vertical; tall." Cf. *Stadare*, "to stand," via the causative.
- **Suanexilicum** -- "unmarried" (usually animate agreement only, since this describes a person). *Sua-* (negation) + *Nexil-* (the root of *Nexilium*, "sash") + *-icum* -- "not sashed," referencing the marital-status distinction that wearing the *Nexilium* itself marks.
- **Suatum** -- "sharp."
- **Sueium** -- "barren"
- **Tenesmicum** -- "humble". *Ten-* ("small") + *Esmicum* ("having the quality of feeling") -- "small-feeling." Cf. *Tenesmicas*, "humility."
- **Tenum** -- "small." Comparative *Tenios*, superlative *Tenescum*, adverb *Tenicer* ("quite; somewhat").
- **Totum** -- "all (of a number)."
- **Tracum** -- "long (not wide)."
- **Uncrum** -- "dirty."
- **Ûtum** -- "able." Cf. *Ûtas*, "ability."
- **Vericum** -- "green." From *Veru* ("the color green") + *-icum*, same vowel-elision pattern as *Rubicum*.
- **Vericum** -- "honest". Built on *Verium*, "truth" -- a true homonym with the entry above, not a polysemy; the two are unrelated words that happen to share a spelling. Cf. *Audvericum*, "dishonest."
- **Vidatum** -- "seen" (*vida-* + resultative *-tum*, no epenthesis on the vowel-final stem).
- **Zantrum** -- "clean."
- **Zuaretum** -- "brave". Independent coinage. Cf. *Zuaretas*, "bravery; courage."
- **Zuluricum** -- "made of cotton." *Zuluri-* (the root of *Zulvarix*) + *-icum*, the doubled *-i-* simplifying to one.

### Adverbs

The productive class is formed by replacing an adjective's *-um* with the invariant *-icer* -- see Grammar, Productive Markers, for the formation rule and its *-icum*-specific elision. Temporal, frequency, additive, and degree adverbs sit alongside these as a closed class of non-derived function words. A handful are lexicalized outliers rather than regular formations: *Acer* ("ever"), *Alacer* ("sometimes"), and *Necer* ("never") don't reduce to the regular rule at all.

- **Aeps** -- "often."
- **Aericcer** -- "airily" (*aericum*, the suffix's leading *-i-* eliding against the root's own *-ic*, leaving the geminate *-cc-*).
- **Acer** -- "ever." Underlies *Alacer* and *Necer* -- see the section intro, above.
- **Alacer** -- "sometimes." *Al-* ("some") + *Acer* ("ever") -- "some-ever."
- **Biam** -- "again." *Bia* ("two") + *-am* -- "a second time."
- **Duc** -- "still; yet."
- **Ecu** -- "also; too."
- **Eole** -- "very."
- **Imma** -- "always."
- **Legericer** -- "aggressively" (*legerum* minus *-um*, plus *-icer*).
- **Legionariccer** -- "legionarily" (*legionaricum*, same *-i-* elision plus geminate as *Aericcer*).
- **Nan** -- "now."
- **Ors** -- "even."
- **Pleticer** -- "enough." The regular *-icer* adverb of *Pletum* ("full") -- "fully," narrowed to "sufficiently."
- **Quericcer** -- "peacefully; calmly.
- **Tenicer** -- "quite; somewhat." The regular *-icer* adverb of *Tenum* ("small") -- "smally," narrowed to a hedging degree sense.
- **Toticer** -- "completely; entirely; fully." The regular *-icer* adverb of *Totum* ("all").
- **Uldo** -- "then."
- **Unicer** -- "only; just." *Un* ("one") + *-icer* -- "one-ly," exclusively singular.
- **Vas** -- "almost."
- **Vidaticer** -- "visibly" (adverbial of the Resultative *vidatum*).
- **Zuam** -- "already."

### Pronouns

- **Alanis** -- "someone else".
- **Alis** -- "someone". *Alum* ("some") + *-is*.
- **Cêr** -- "who", the interrogative pronoun.
- **Descis** -- "the same one; the aforementioned one". *Descum* ("same") + *-is*.
- **Ilês** -- "some people" (*plurale tantum*). *Ilum* ("several") + *-ês*, no singular, the same pattern as *Omnês*.
- **Mea** -- first person.
- **Nix** -- "no one". *Nec-* (root of *Necum* "no; none") + the Third Declension *-ix*, simplified from the expected *Necix*.
- **Omnês** -- "everyone," a rare plural-only (*plurale tantum*) Second Animate form -- no singular exists, since the sense itself has none.
- **Polês** -- "many people" (*plurale tantum*). *Polium*'s root, *Poli-*, plus *-ês*, with the *-i-* contracting before the ending -- the same pattern as *Ilês*/*Omnês*.
- **Quesis** -- "each one; every one". *Quesum* ("each; every") + *-is* -- contrasted with the collective *Omnês*, "everyone."
- **Sea** -- third person.
- **Tea** -- second person.
### Particles

- **Anno** -- "don't! stop!" *Are*'s own informal Negative Imperative (*Are* → *Anno*, Grammar, Conjugation, Irregular verbs), used as a standalone exhortation the same way *Ao* is -- the negative counterpart to it.
- **Ao** -- "go on! continue!" The bare Imperative of *Are* ("to do") used as a standalone exhortation -- the same core "do it!" sense that also appears in the periphrastic formal Negative Imperative (*quaran ao*, "do not drink!").
- **Assi** -- modal particle, casualizes an imperative: "try doing X", "just do X".
- **Ema** -- modal particle, hedged probability, "I guess; probably; maybe". This occurs in several dedicated grammatical positions: as a free preposition, it is the hedged bet; as a sentence-final modal, it seeks the listener's agreement on a matter of sentiment; thirdly, it forms a parenthesis with a Volitional phrase to fix its conjectural reading against the proposal and resolve senses -- see Grammar, Conjugation, Volitional.
- **Embe** -- modal particle, marking something as shared or obvious knowledge: "as we both know; obviously" -- see Grammar, Modal Particles and Conjunctions.
- **Eo** -- "so be it! indeed!" The bare Imperative of *E-* ("to be") used as an interjection of agreement, and as the plain affirmative answer to a yes/no question -- see Grammar, Questions and Answers.
- **Essionna** -- "by the Six!" An interjection of surprise, contracted from *Es Sezionâ*.
- **Inno** -- modal particle, "actually," gently correcting or nuancing what was just implied -- see Grammar, Modal Particles and Conjunctions.
- **Lei** -- modal particle, resignation, "alas; that's simply how it is" -- see Grammar, Modal Particles and Conjunctions.
- **Nam** -- polar-question particle, expecting a positive-confirming answer -- see Grammar, Questions and Answers.
- **Ne** -- "not," a free-standing negative particle for negating nouns, adjectives, or whole non-verbal statements. Also the plain negative answer to a yes/no question, regardless of the question's own phrasing -- see Grammar, Questions and Answers.
- **Nectrê** -- "none of that!" A heavily contracted form of *Necum têrei*, "none of that (behavior of yours)," .
- **Nenu** -- polar-question particle, *Ne* + *Nu* contracted, posing a negated question and expecting a negative-confirming answer -- see Grammar, Questions and Answers.
- **Nu** -- neutral polar-question particle, no expectation either way -- see Grammar, Questions and Answers.
- **Quer** -- modal particle, explicit permission in an imperative, "be at ease; feel free" -- see Grammar, Modal Particles and Conjunctions. Lexicalized from *Quericum* ("peaceful, calm") as permission-granting.
- **Quovo** -- modal particle, reassurance, downplaying doubt, "it'll be fine" -- see Grammar, Modal Particles and Conjunctions.
- **Scai** -- modal particle, high uncertainty or surprise. Sentence-final it often reinforces *nu/nenu/nam* in question formation, and in verb-fronted informal speech it can replace the question markers outright: "Veneri scai?". In exclamations it is a rhetorical tag ("really; indeed; after all") or, as a standalone, bare surprise ("What! How!").
- **So** -- urgency or warning. Marks strong assertion and conviction -- correction, resolve, an expectation of compliance -- especially as sentence-final modal, such that even a verb in Plain form can feel like an informal imperative.
- **Tae** -- "Woe! Oh!" A cry of pain or grief. Fuller form *Itae* also in use.
- **Tando** -- modal particle, softens a genuine question into something warmer and more curious than blunt interrogation. Sentence-final, it marks moderate uncertainty or doubt, often explaining a decision -- see Grammar, Modal Particles and Conjunctions.
- **Vera** -- modal particle, surprise or contrafactual expectation: "actually; really; wow; damn"
- **Verei** -- the contradictory affirmative, asserting "yes" specifically against a negative implication from a negatively-phrased question, or contradicting a negative assumption in an ordinary statement. See Grammar, Questions and Answers.
- **Vessare** -- a mild oath. Contracted from *Verasare*, the Affective (passive) form of *Verare* ("to know") -- "it is known!," an assertion of certainty that drifted into an oath as a lexicalized, fixed interjection rather than a live verb form in active use.

### Prepositions

See Grammar, Prepositions, for the full case-government system.

- **As** -- "towards," fixed to Accusative. Marks goal-of-motion, keeping bare Accusative free to mark only the direct object. *As Remeam*, "towards Remea."
- **Cu** -- "with" (comitative), fixed to Ablative. Marks accompaniment of a person or group, distinct from the instrumental sense already carried by bare Ablative. *Cu legionê*, "with (alongside) the legion" -- contrast bare *legionê*, "with (by means of) the legion."
- **Din** -- "above; over; onto," alternating: + Locative = "above" (static); + Accusative = "onto; towards above" (motion). "From above" stacks *Ê* on top: *Ê din* + Accusative.
- **Ê** -- "out of, from within," fixed to Ablative. A doublet alongside *es*, the two plausibly descending from an original split. *Ê Remeâ*, "out of, from within Remea."
- **En** -- "within" / "into," alternating: + Locative = "within"; + Accusative = "into."
- **Es** -- "from (the direction of)," general and unspecified source, fixed to Ablative. *Es Remeâ*, "from (the direction of) Remea."
- **Essu** -- "from beneath," fixed to Ablative.
- **Quis** -- "beside; next to; alongside," fixed to Locative.
- **Sû** -- "under" / "to beneath," alternating: + Locative = "under"; + Accusative = "to beneath."
- **Ura** -- "outside; without; at the edge," alternating: + Locative = "outside (of)" (static); + Accusative = "to outside; out to" (motion).

### Conjunctions

A closed set of clause- and phrase-level connectors -- see Grammar, Modal Particles and Conjunctions, for the compositional logic behind *Cuzei* and *Nezei*.

- **Ave** -- "but."
- **Cu** -- "and." The comitative preposition doubling as conjunction.
- **Cuzei** -- "although." *Cu* ("and") + *Zei* ("if") -- "and-if."
- **Dan** -- "that" (complementizer, "I know that...").
- **Nezei** -- "unless." *Ne* ("not") + *Zei* ("if") -- "not-if."
- **Quas** -- "because."
- **Quem** -- "therefore."
- **Ser** -- "or."
- **Volo** -- "when" (subordinating).
- **Zei** -- "if."

### Prefixes

A set of prefixes on the same preverb-to-prefix cline that already produced *Cu* and *En*'s dual life as both free preposition and compounding element. They split by how grammaticalized their sense is -- see Grammar, Conjugation, Prefixes, for the reasoning behind the split and the case each dual-function preposition governs.

**Dual-function** (standalone preposition and compounding prefix both):

- **Aut** -- "back; away" (undoing), + Ablative. Pairs with *Autro* as a reinforcing doublet, the same relationship *Sû*/*Subter*-style pairs have elsewhere.
- **Autro** -- "backward; behind."
- **Ax** -- "away from; off," + Ablative. More specific than *Es*'s general "from" -- off of a surface, specifically.
- **Din** -- "above; over; onto," + Locative (static) / Accusative (motion) -- see Prepositions. Prefixes as *din-*, assimilating to *dim-* before labials, ordinary nasal place-assimilation.
- **Gen** -- "against; in the way of; opposing," + Accusative.
- **Meter** -- "after" (spatial and temporal), + Accusative.
- **Quis** -- "beside; next to; alongside," + Locative -- see Prepositions. Prefixes as *quis-*/*qui-*, geminating to *quiss-* before a word-initial *c* -- an emergent avoidance of /sk/ following /k/ or /kʷ/, the following consonant assimilating backward into the *-s-* rather than surfacing separately.
- **Quo** -- "forward; for, on behalf of," + Dative for the benefactive sense, Accusative for the directional sense.
- **Re** -- "down; away; reversal," + Ablative.
- **Ruc** -- "around; again, returning," + Accusative.
- **Ura** -- "outside; without; at the edge," + Locative (static) / Accusative (motion) -- see Prepositions.
- **Vala** -- "between; amid," + Accusative.
- **Ventra** -- "across," + Accusative.
- **Vin** -- "in front of; before" (spatial and temporal), + Locative. Plausibly related to *Vis* ("eye") via "what is apparent" -- what stands before the eye.

**Bound-only, grammaticalized sense** (no independent word -- these prefixes attach directly, never stand alone):

- **Ester-** -- "above; over; excess; intensive," the intensive sense being the grammaticalized bleaching of the spatial "over/excess" senses -- compare how Latin *super-* carries the same range.
- **Sua-** -- "apart; separate; negation," un-/not- being the grammaticalized bleaching of the separative sense.
- **Zae-** -- "outside, beyond, past; changed into" -- the "change" sense is the grammaticalized end of an originally spatial "beyond" meaning.

**Bound-only, quantifier class** (no independent word, but for a different reason -- numerals and quantity-words don't naturalistically double as free-standing prepositions cross-linguistically):

- **Bi-** -- "two-; twice-; doubled; again-" (repeated). Ties directly to *Bia*, "two."
- **Poli-** -- "many-." Ties directly to *Polium*, "many."
- **Un-** -- "one-; single-." Ties directly to *Un*, "one" -- notably the *opposite* of the negative sense "un-" carries in English.

### Verbs

Citation (Plain) forms, serving as the model exemplars for each stem class -- see Grammar, Conjugation, for full paradigms.

- **Abere** (Class A) -- "to keep; to retain." *Ab-* (the root of *Abe*, "to own") + a continuative sense, "to keep possessing."
- **Abe** (Class B) -- "to own; to possess." Cf. *Abestrium*, *Abium*.
- **Aclecâre** (Class A) -- "to read aloud; to infer from observation." *Ax* ("away from; off") + *Lecâre* ("to read") -- *Ax*'s underlying /ks/ drops its /s/ before the consonant-initial root, leaving plain /k/ (spelled *c*).
- **Aerare** (Class A) -- "to fill with air" (*aer* + the *are*-light-verb).
- **Aie** (Class C) -- "to love." Potential *Aive*, "to be able to love" -- see Grammar, Conjugation.
- **Alare** (Class A) -- "to sew; to stitch."
- **Amare** (Class A) -- "to rain; to snow; to fall from the sky".
- **Amplare** (Class A) -- "to enlarge." Causative *-are* on the adjective root *Ampl-*.
- **Aquêle** (Class B) -- "to take off" (clothing). *Ax* ("off") + *Vêle* ("to put") -- *Ax*'s /ks/ loses its /s/ before the consonant-initial root (as in *Aclecâre*), and the resulting *-c-*+*-v-* then coalesces to *-qu-* (as in *Ruquenare*): *Ax+Vêle → Acvêle → Aquêle*.
- **Aralare** (Class A) -- "to heat." Causative *-are* on *Aral-* (the root of *Aralum*, "hot").
- **Arare** (Class A) -- "to give honor."
- **Are** (irregular) -- "to do."
- **Arve** (Class C) -- "to give birth to"
- **Aste** (Class B) -- "to sparkle." Cf. *Astilium*, "star" -- root *Ast-* plus diminutive *-il-* plus *-ium*.
- **Audifare** (Class A) -- "to tear down."
- **Audvêle** (Class B) -- "to put away; to hide something." *Aut* + *Vêle* ("to put") -- *Aut*'s final *-t-* voices to *-d-* before *v*.
- **Autare** -- "to undo" (irregular, inheriting *Are*'s own irregular conjugation).
- **Autâvie** (Class C) -- "to leave behind; to abandon; to move away." *Aut* + *Âvie* ("to inhabit; to dwell") -- "to un-dwell."
- **Ave** (Class C) -- "to ripple; to undulate."
- **Âvie** (Class C) -- "to inhabit; to dwell."
- **Axecre** (Class B) -- "to cut off; to shear; to remove."
- **Benare** (Class A) -- "to improve." Causative *-are* on *Ben-* (the root of *Benum*, "good").
- **Biabe** (Class B) -- "to regain." *Bi-* ("again") + *Abe* ("to own") -- "to possess again."
- **Capitare** (Class A) -- "to lead."
- **Care** (irregular) -- "to give."
- **Catere** (Class A) -- "to earn"
- **Cattare** (Class A) -- "to buy."
- **Cave** (Class C) -- "to dig; to hollow out."
- **Cidere** (Class A) -- "to kill."
- **College** (Class B) -- "to work together." Cf. *Collegion*.
- **Comare** (Class A) -- "to rake." Underlies *Comilos*, "comb" -- see Nouns.
- **Comilare** (Class A) -- "to comb."
- **Crie** (Class C) -- "to make; to produce; to create." Cf. *Crians*, *Cristrium*.
- **Cubere** -- "to rule" (irregular, alongside *Cidere*/*Gradere* -- see Grammar, Conjugation).
- **Cuiare** (Class A) -- "to trade." *Cu* ("with") + *Iare* ("to sell") -- "to sell-with," the same compounding pattern as *Cuvenare*.
- **Cusede** (Class B) -- "to meet (in council); to convene." *Cu* ("with") + *Sede* ("to sit") -- "to sit together," a deliberate, formal contrast with *Cuvenare*'s spontaneous encounter.
- **Cuvenare** (Class A) -- "to meet." *Cu* ("with") + *Venare* ("to come") -- "to come-with."
- **Dabe** (Class B) -- "to eat."
- **Dêce** (Class B) -- "to think." Cf. *Sûdêce* ("to remember") and *Nedêce* ("to forget") -- see Grammar, Productive Markers, for the preposition-plus-verb compounding pattern.
- **Descare** (Class A) -- "to align." Causative *-are* on *Desc-* (the root of *Descum*, "same") -- "to make the same."

## Grammar

### Syntax

Subject-Object-Verb is the pragmatically neutral default (*mea dim vidare*, "I see you"), but order is otherwise free: because case endings, not position, carry the grammatical relations, any case-marked constituent can front for topical or contrastive emphasis with no change to its ending and no additional marking. In fact, formal speech uses OSV order to the object when it is the addressee.

Moreover, Remean is a high-context language, meaning that any sentence part recoverable from situational context or the preceding conversation can be dropped. Most commonly this means a bare verb is the minimum viable sentence, though there are figures of speech where leaving a bare adjective-noun (or noun-noun) juxtaposition carries the grammatical sense on its own. This can be most often seen with the copula in casual speech.

### Animacy

Remean grammatically distinguishes nouns not by grammatical or biological gender but by animacy: whether something is considered alive or not. This is complicated by a social and cultural layer that can elevate an otherwise-inanimate referent to animate standing, or stratify animate referents themselves by how much respect they're owed.

For example, the word for "thing" in Remean is _sens_: something that exists. Whereas something that lives (or at least has some animacy ascribed to it) is _eix_, and its gloss accordingly reads "animal; being; living thing". People, however, are not considered base animals in ordinary speech, so there exists a more respectful word for "person; human", which is _eis_. Both _eix_ and _eis_ derive from the same root, which can also be found in _eia_ "life; vital force; animacy" and the simple copula _e_ "to be; to be alive; to live". (Contrast the latter with _sere_ "to exist", which shares its root with _sens_ above.)

*Eia*, *Eis*, and *Eix* are a prototypical instance of the three-tier Characterization system (see Productive Markers, Characterization, for the full mechanism and its other examples) -- one root, refracted through three tiers of reverence.

#### First, Second, Third Animate Declensions

The three tiers surface as three declensions, built on the identical logic Characterization already establishes: **First Animate** (stem + *-a*), **Second Animate** (stem + *-is*), and **Third Animate** (stem + *-ix*), the same descending order of reverence *Eia*/*Eis*/*Eix* themselves show. Most individual coinages are lexicalized rather than freshly derivable on demand, but the underlying logic -- which tier a given referent earns -- stays transparent.

Personhood extends by undeniability of agency and will, a category reaching past humans to the Noble Six themselves, their courtiers, and lesser elemental sprites. In fact this goes so far as to treat trees in general with the highest repect -- _zulva_ -- simply because courtiers and sprites of Soil are so often found in this shape as well.

#### Agreement

**Adjectives, participles, comparatives, superlatives, and possessives all agree with the noun they modify in animacy as well as case and number.** However, agreement does not track the target's own specific declension. Any animate noun, whichever of the three animate declensions it belongs to, forces its attending adjectives into agreement in the Second Animate paradigm _-is_; any inanimate noun forces the First Inanimate paradigm _-um_, with its linking *-i-* dropped throughout (unlike the noun paradigm itself, which keeps it).

**The copula itself also splits by animacy, the same way its modifiers do.** The irregular verb *e* ("to be") is used for animate referents; when the referent is inanimate, *sere* ("to exist," A-irregular, root *se-*) is used. The split carries through every derived use: conjecture via the Volitional works identically on the inanimate side, *Seri* standing in for *Eri* -- *Navium ema cissum seri*, "the ship is probably fast," against *Legionaris ema zuaretis eri*, "the legionary is probably brave."

**Reflexives and relative clauses track the same system.** The reflexive splits by agency into *six* ("himself; herself; animate itself") in Third Animate and *idex* ("inanimate itself") in Third Inanimate; since both are usually found as objects, their nominative forms are never used and we find the accusative _sissim_ and _idecim_ instead. The relative pronoun *Cis*/*Cid* agrees in animacy with its antecedent the same way any other modifier does.

#### Respect-Agency

Respect-agency runs through a wide range of the vocabulary well beyond trees. Weapons are usually granted a high tier, revealing a martial culture that sees more in a blade than a tool of war (e.g., *Arca*, *Suatra*, *Sarissa*; *Pavis*, *Secris*, *Cidestrilis*). The celestial bodies that move against the firmament are all 1st-Animate nouns that have lexicalized enough to be considered proper names: *Ella*, the sun, and the three moons *Aurea*, *Belana*, *Langea*. The six elements themselves, coded Second Animate (*Ignis*, *Ventis*, *Fluis*, *Frigis*, *Lapis*, *Solis*), are not to be mistaken for the mere thing, but as the animate metaphysical principle governing the respective element in the world, a difference that in English could only be expressed by a stragegy of contrasting capitalisation: mundane fire (*ignium*) is *of* Fire (*Ignis*), but is not its equivalent, the way a breeze isn't equivalent to the whole climatic system producing it.

Another category where respect-agency tiers are encountered in everyday life is in how Remean classifies animals. In the basic tier of agency or animacy we find "fish" _pescix_, "louse" _cedix_, or "insect" _scix_ as well as many others. However, certain animals garner higher tiers of respect-agency, either through long cohabitation with humans, recognition of cognitive complexity, or simple awe: thus "dog" _canis_, "cow" _branis_, "hare" _nubis_ and other animals of importance to agriculture or pastoral life are found in 2nd Animate, while we find 1st-Animate respect-agency evidenced in "bear" _atra_, "lion" _rasata_, "cat" _nuca_, or "whale" _pescola_ and its diminutive "dolphin" _pescolila_ (a rare instance where a lexicalized augmentative produced a diminutive that lexicalized in turn). Cognition might also be the reason why we find "sheep" _rix_ as a basic-agency noun, whereas "goat" _geta_ sits in 1st -- in many cultures goats are seen as clever and sheep as proverbially dull. As lexicalized forms, high-tier nouns can occur in compounds even when the resultant word drops in register, as evidenced in "fishtail griffin" _pescoladabis_, which literally means "whale-eater gryph" and forms the root of modern Balboan _pescoda_ for the same animal.

#### Relative Standing

Respect animacy has deeper repercussions in Remean grammar outside its use as a marker of reverence in word formation; it also governs voice selection. It forces a speaker to observe an implicit hierarchy by comparing two referents in a clause against each other.

This comparison runs along a ranked hierarchy that runs from the present speech actors themselves, over third-party referents, and down the ladder of respect animacy to the most remote abstractions, as follows: speech actors (first and second person) rank highest, followed by third-person referents that are human (or otherwise sentient), then the High, Neutral and Basic tiers in order, with the collective -ion markers included in Neutral wherever it is read animatedly. Thereafter follow natural forces, then concrete inanimate objects, and finally abstractions at the bottom.

The full hierarchy ladder thus runs as follows:
*Speech Actors (1st & 2nd Person) > Sentient Referent (3rd Person) > High Tier > Neutral Tier and Animate Collective > Basic Tier > Natural Force > Object > Abstraction*

Whenever the semantic agent of a clause ranks below its patient on this scale, the clause must take the Affective -- the patient's higher standing forcing its casting as grammatical subject. A louse (Basic tier) acting on a bear (High tier) can only be phrased passively: *Atra es cedissê vidasare*, "the bear is seen by the louse," never the active construction *Cedix atram vidare*, "the louse sees the bear." Two referents of equal or ambiguous rank trigger no such requirement but are simply governed by the case system as agent and patient, since no different in standing is implied.

As a side effect, this means speakers of Remean may refer to the natural order of the world in three different rhetorical registers. At its most basic, as in any other language, they may speak of events as a mere natural force. At a more reverent tier, a speaker would speak of them as the workings of the element, an animate force at work in the world, not merely a consequence, and lastly and most reverently, the natural force being described would be subsumed in the quasi-divine workings of the Noble Elements themselves. These three registers are a consequence of the lexically encoded tiers of respect paid to the elemental forces at play in the world, differentiating without issue between the mere consequence of impassive forces and acts of elemental or primordial will.

### Inanimacy

#### First Inanimate

| | Singular | Plural |
|---|---|---|
| Nominative | -ium | -ia |
| Accusative | -ium | -ia |
| Genitive | -iî | -iôrum |
| Dative | -iô | -iîs |
| Ablative | -iô | -iîs |
| Locative | -iô | -iîs |
*Example: Latium, Latium, Latiî, Latiô, Latiô, Latiô; Latia, Latia, Latiôrum, Latiîs, Latiîs, Latiîs.*

Identifiable by its *-ium* ending, First Inanimate is the widest and most productive of the inanimate declensions, and covers several distinguishable but overlapping tendencies rather than one clean category. Genuine abstract states sit here (*Querium* "peace," *Verium* "truth," *Magium* "knowledge"), alongside the *-(s)trium* "state or capacity of X-ing" family (*Aistrium* "loving; care," *Magistrium* "teaching; doctrine," *Fidestrium* "believing; faith"). 

A second, equally productive tendency covers artifacts: some diminutive-derived (*Capilium* "hat," *Manilium* "bracelet," *Corvicilium* "necklace"), others simply verb-derived or otherwise artifactual without a diminutive step at all (*Latium* "clothing," *Adivium* "grease," *Navium* "ship"). 

However, not every First Inanimate noun fits cleanly into either tendency -- *Astilium* ("star") isn't "made for" anything the way *Capilium* is; *Lapidium* ("jewel; worked gem") is clearly an artifact without deriving from a verb stem at all; *Capitolium* ("government") is an abstraction following the artifact-style pattern, but by the augmentative route rather than the diminutive one. These tendencies are thus best read as generalized and highly productive rather than clean-cut categories a given noun must be forced into.

Some First Inanimate nouns show bare *-um* rather than the full *-ium* such as *Crinum* ("hair") and *Rissicrinum* ("wool," built on it). 

Adjectival forms in inanimate agreement follow this same pattern, but without the linking *-i-*, which is absent from adjectives specifically -- see Agreement, above.

| Nominative | -as, -os | -atâs, -otôs |
| Accusative | -atam, -otom | -atîs, -otîs |
| Genitive | -ati, -oti | -atum, -otum |
| Dative | -atâ, -otô | -atibus, -otibus |
| Ablative | -atê, -otî | -atibus, -otibus |

Outside that rule, Second Inanimate follows few strong patterns, though loose clusters of body parts, time units, and non-derived garments sit here.

Alongside a cluster of passive body parts (see Animacy, above), Third Inanimate hosts a substantial group of instrument/tool derivations off a verb root, such as *Alax* ("needle," off *Alare*) and *Scirpax* ("loom," off *Scirpe*).

#### Inanimacy and Respect

Inanimacy carries no internal grading of respect -- something inanimate simply cannot be graded that way. If an inanimate referent is judged worthy of respect, it is elevated wholesale into one of the animate declensions rather than gaining standing within its own. This is frequently an old, deeply lexicalized shift, most visibly attested in body parts thought expressive of will and agency -- head, eyes, tongue, hands -- while other body parts stay ungraded. (Outliers exist: the belly is animate without expressing will or agency at all, deriving its animacy instead from the entirely reasonable observation that it's where digestion and gestation happen.)

Elevation to agency is a mechanism expressed through several distinct patterns: a plain switch of declension (*Ignium → Ignis*, *Atru → Atra*, *Rubicessum → Rubicessa*); the productive *-(s)trium*/*-(s)tris* contrast between an action's state and its agent; adding *-is* to the oblique stem of the product-of marker *-ns* to form the animate product-marker *-ntis*; a singular agent extracted from a larger *-ion* collective by adding *-aris* (*Legion → Legionaris*, "a legionary") or *-ina* (*Modisarion* → *Modisarina*, "a citizen"), with the latter pattern also attested in the switch from artifact to user of said artifact (*Navium* → *Navina*); and a handful of other, less regular or more oblique patterns beyond these.

### Fourth Declension

Fourth Declension differs from the first three in a genuinely structural way: rather than the readily legible, overt animate/inanimate split the other declensions show, animacy here depends on grammatical marking in context rather than any lexical signal at all -- a feature known as **innate animacy**.

The clearest example can be seen in the collective *-ion* marker. *Aion*, the *-ion* form of *Aie* ("to love"), can mean both "relationship" and "couple", but the distinction isn't given at a glance, only by which agreement the context calls for: inanimate agreement means the bond, animate agreement the people in it. The same holds for *Collegion*, *Legion*, *Modisarion*, and the rest of the category. This split is productive, not obligatory, and some *-ion* words are only ever attested in one sense, with no natural correspondence on the other side. *Pennation* is a clean example: it's used only inanimately, "alphabet; corpus; written works," since "group of writings" has no natural animate reading, and "group of writers" is already covered by the ordinary plural of *Pennatris*, "scribe."

Innate animacy is only a subset of Fourth Declension, though -- the large majority of its membership is straightforwardly inanimate, and *-ion* is far from the only active formant here. The "product-of" marker (*Ignans*, "embers"; *Formans*, "shape"; *Elemens*, "element") and the "place-of" marker (*Igniton* "hearth," *Legeton* "battlefield," *Cubesaton* "territory") are both highly active contributors to Fourth Declension's own corpus, entirely apart from the collective marker.

**The *-er* subtype.** The Nominative Singular is the bare root itself, so every other case adds its ending directly, with no truncation:

### Numerals

The numbers one through twelve each show two forms, split by function: an abstract citation form for the number-concept itself, a bare-root and indeclinable word (*Un*, *Bia*, *Trae*...), with a distinct adnominal form used for actually counting, given here (*Unum*, *Biavum*, *Traevium*...) in its Nominative Singular only. The relationship between the abstract root and the adnominal citation form is irregular and lexicalized rather than following one productive rule, though several loose tendencies are visible: consonant-final roots tend to take the ending directly with no linking element (*Un → Unum*, *Quin → Quinum*, *Sez → Sezum*, *Zel → Zelum*); vowel-final roots often insert a linking *-v-* before the ending (*Bia → Biavum*, *Noa → Noavum*); and several forms truncate a final syllable of the root entirely before suffixing (*Zeben → Zebium*, not *\*Zebenium*; *Dezen → Dezum*, not *\*Dezenum*; *Evin → Evium*, not *\*Evinium*). None of these tendencies rise to a rule -- *Catra → Catrium* takes no linking consonant despite ending in a vowel, breaking the pattern *Bia*/*Noa* otherwise suggest. 

**Ordinals.** Formed through the "quality of" marker *-icum*, affixed by the same rule as the adnominal form:
- **Regular, no elision**, on consonant-final roots that took no linking element in the adnominal form: *Un → Unicum*, *Sez → Sezicum*, *Zel → Zelicum*.
- **Truncated roots**, reusing the same truncation the adnominal form already established: *Zeben → Zebicum*, *Dezen → Dezicum*, *Evin → Evicum*.
- **Suffix-elides** (*-icum*'s own *-i-* drops instead): *Bia → Biacum*, *Trae → Traecum*, *Noa → Noacum* -- precisely the three roots that took the linking *-v-* in their adnominal form (*Biavum*, *Traevium*, *Noavum*).
- **Root-elides** (the root's own final vowel drops, the same pattern as *Rubu → Rubicum*): *Catra → Catricum*, *Octra → Octricum* -- precisely the two roots that took *no* linking element in their adnominal form (*Catrium*, *Octrium*).
- **Exception**: *Quincum* elides despite *Quin* being consonant-final and needing no elision in its adnominal form (*Quinum*) -- a lexicalized irregularity, plausibly simply from being a very high-frequency ordinal.

**Beyond twelve.** Remean counting is base-60 overall, with the duodecimal (base-12) set outlined above underneath. Past twelve, the system turns genuinely compositional, and does so in two distinct tiers with different rules:
- **Thirteen through fifty-nine** build additively on *Zel* ("twelve"), with no connecting word -- thirteen is simply *Zelun* (*Zel + Un*, "twelve-one"), not *Zel-cu-un*. Adnominal and ordinal forms past twelve are fully regular: *-um*/*-icum* directly on the number root, eliding any vowels at the junction. The multiples of *Zel* below 60 elide *Zel*'s own initial *z-* and the multiplier's final vowel: *Bia* ("two") *+ Zel → Biel* (24); *Trae* ("three") *+ Zel → Trael* (36); *Catra* ("four") *+ Zel → Catrel* (48). An additive remainder attaches after a space: *Biel bia*, "twenty-six" (24 + 2) -- adnominal *Bielbium*, ordinal *Bielbicum*, both fully fused with no space, as per the rule that adnominal/ordinal forms elide.
- **Sixty and up** run on two independently named units rather than continued multiples of *Zel*: *Belan* ("sixty," compare *Belana*, the moon on a stable and predictable 60-day cycle) and *Tot* ("three hundred sixty," *60 × 6* or a full cycle of *Belan* -- cf. *Totum*, "all"). Multiplication at this tier works differently from the *Zel* tier: no elision at all, just plain concatenation of the multiplier onto *Belan*/*Tot* -- *Bia + Belan = Biabelan* (120), *Catra + Belan = Catrabelan* (240), *Quin + Tot = Quintot* (1800). A space separates each multiplied grouping, including from any further additive remainder: *Biabelan trae*, "one hundred twenty-three" (120 + 3) -- adnominal *Biabelantrum*, ordinal *Biabelantricum*, again fully fused. Numbers spanning multiple tiers simply stack groupings left to right, largest to smallest, each separated by a space: *Quintot catrabelan trael noa*, "two thousand eighty-five" (1800 + 240 + 36 + 9).

### Prepositions

Prepositions split into two structural types depending on whether the relation they mark has a static/motion distinction.

**Fixed-case (no plausible directional split):**

| Preposition | Case       | Sense                                           |
| ----------- | ---------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| *cu*        | Ablative   | with (comitative -- a person or group)          |
| *es*        | Ablative   | from (general, unspecified source)              |
| *ê*         | Ablative   | out of, from within (container-specific source) |
| *essu*      | Ablative   | from beneath                                    |
| *as*        | Accusative | towards (goal-of-motion)                        |

**Case-alternating**

| Preposition | + Locative | + Accusative |
| ----------- | ---------- | ------------ |
| *en*        | within     | into         |
| *sû*        | under      | to beneath   |
_Example: as Remeam ("towards Remea"), en Remeae ("within Remea"), en Remeam ("into Remea"), ê Remeâ ("out of, from within Remea"), es Remeâ ("from the direction of Remea"), sû Remeae ("under Remea"), sû Remeam ("to beneath Remea"), essu Remeâ ("from under Remea")_

**Bare case vs. use of _cu_ and _as_.** Bare Ablative carries the instrumental ("with" as tool) sense. *Cu* + Ablative instead marks "with" as accompaniment specifically. *As* exists for the same reason in reverse: without it, bare Accusative is the direct object, whereas _as_ + Accusative is a statement of a goal, mirrored in the meaning of the prepositions _en_ and _sû_ when used with the Accusative.

### Adjectives

Adjectives agree with the noun they modify in case, number, and animacy -- see Animacy, above, for the full agreement paradigms and worked example. Comparatives, superlatives, and possessives all take the same two-paradigm split.

Adjectives may also stand alone to name an abstract state with no noun attached at all.

#### Comparison

The **comparative**, *-ios*, fully replaces the citation *-um* rather than being inserted before it, and the resulting form then declines as a Second Inanimate form instead. When the modified noun is animate, the comparative takes the ordinary Second Animate paradigm instead, by a pattern of rhotacism where the comparative's own final *-s-* changes to _-r-_ before the 2A ending is affixed: *Amplios* + *-is* yields *Amplioris*.

The **superlative** works as a simpler infix instead, inserting *-esc-* before the ordinary ending and staying entirely in the normal adjective paradigm.

**The standard of comparison** takes a bare Ablative with no preposition: *Legionaris canê amplioris e*, "the legionary is bigger than the dog" (Ablative Sg.). The superlative similarly uses the bare ablative, singular or plural: *Legionaris amplescis legionaritibus e*, "the legionary is the biggest among the legionaries".

### Pronouns

Remean pronouns are really three families that share a common root system: personal pronouns for each of the three grammatical persons, and a parallel demonstrative and interrogative system built on four related deictic stems.

#### Personal Pronouns

Speaker, listener, and third person share First Animate declension. Remean treats each possible "speech actor" as holding high reverence, even if the third person may not be an agent against the other two -- see Grammar, Animacy, Relative Standing.


|                           | Me- (speaker) | Te- (listener) | Se- (3rd person)     |
| ------------------------- | ------------- | -------------- | -------------------- |
| Pronoun                   | *Mea*         | *Tea*          | *Sea*                |
| Possessive<br>(inanimate) | *Meum*        | *Teum*         | *Seum*               |
| Possessive<br>(animate)   | *Meis*        | *Teis*         | *Seis*               |
| Reflexive                 | *Meam*        | *Team*         | *Sescum*<br>*Sescam* |
The Possessive overlaps in meaning with the pronoun's own Genitive, and which is used is a question of rhetoric more than grammar. Because of Remean's lack of gendering markers, *Sea* is a single undifferentiated form referring to "He" and "She" as well as an animate "It"; by convention English-language glosses render Remean pronouns in the feminine form when they refer to Citizens, reflecting how the Harmonized ideal reads from without.

The reflexive for speaker and listener is the Accusative of the pronoun -- *Mea meam vidare*, "I see myself" -- but since an ordinary third-person Accusative would be ambiguous between a reference back to the subject and a reference to some other third party, Remean uses a dedicated lexicalized reflexive here with *Sescum* (inanimate) and *Sescam* (animate) from *Se-* plus the superlative infix *-esc-*, "the very one; the very same".

Because of the animacy requirement of these pronouns -- only something alive can potentially be a speech actor -- inanimate nouns are referred to using the demonstrative instead, see below. Furthermore, polite speech carries a social taboo on using pronouns to directly refer to a speech actor of higher social standing, and in such contexts using titles, ranks, occupations or other designators as stand-ins is an unwritten rule speakers from the periphery were known to get wrong.

#### Demonstratives

For the demonstratives, Remean reuses the stems already familiar from the pronouns: *Me-* for the proximal, something close to the speaker; *Te-* for something near the listener; *Se-* for a distal something away from both speaker and listener.

In contrast to the pronouns in First Animate, the animate deictic system uses forms in Fourth Declension, a paradigmatic case of innate animacy.

|               | Animate              | Inanimate              | Location              |
| ------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------- | --------------------- |
| Near me       | *Mêr* "this being"   | *Meium* "this thing"   | *Meton* "here"        |
| Near you      | *Têr* "that being"   | *Teium* "that thing"   | *Teton* "there"       |
| Away          | *Sêr* "yonder being" | *Seium* "yonder thing" | *Seton* "over yonder" |
*Example: sêr, sêrem, sêrei, sêrê, sêrei, sêrê; sêrês, sêrêm, sêrerum, sêrebus, sêrerum, sêrebus.*

As a neutral grammatical class, the use of a demonstrative does not by itself signify a contrast to the original reverence tier of the referent. Such a use is a readily understood rhetorical figure, however, and in polite speech the humble use of *Mêr* instead of *Mea* is in fact expected. Similarly, textual fragments from the Long War reveal that Remeans did not extend the use of the grammatical pronouns to their enemies, referring to them by the demonstratives instead even in direct speech, a demotion into a lower tier of relative standing clearly motivated by hostility and not grammatical necessity.

#### Interrogatives

The interrogatives *Cer/Ceium* "who/what" form from the root *Ce-* and follow the animacy split discussed under Demonstratives above:

*Examples: 
Cêr odente?* "Who spoke?"

*Ceium sente?* "What happened?"

In addition to the interrogative pronouns, whose declensions govern "who/whom/whose/to whom/by whom" in the animate and inanimate paradigm, there are also the interrogative adjectives *Ceis*/*Cecum* "which" and *Cestum*/*Cestis* "what kind of", always agreeing with the noun they modify in agency and case as any adjective would: 

*Examples: 
Ceis modis odente?* "Which citizen spoke?"

*Cecum lapium amplescum sente?* "Which stone was the biggest?

*Cestum verex teium sere?* "What kind of leather is this?"

*Cestis e sea?* "What is she like?"

*Ceis*/*Cecum* also act as the relative pronoun, marked for whatever case the referent's role in the subordinate clause necessitates, and agreeing in animacy with the antecedent:

*Examples: 
Legionaris ceitim mea suatram zicessente lai Zuamariô cidesaretis ente.* "The Legionary to whom I would have sent the sword unfortunately was killed in the Merhal."

*Uraflûton cecô sea aviente furvatibus relemesarente.* "The eruption island which she lived in was destroyed by tremors."

Lastly, a group of invariant interrogative adverbs round out this grammatical region. *Cetoni*, *Ascem* and *Esce* govern locative, goal-of-motion and source (and the latter two may occur in the fuller forms *as cetonem* and *es cetone* in formal speech):

*Examples:
Cetoni capitoleton sere?* "Where is the council hall?"

*Ascem grîfilitês pernante?* "Whither did the gryphs fly?"

*Esce canis venante?* "Whence came the dog?"

Next to these *Cei* asks "why", *Certa* asks "when", and *Icer* asks "how; in what way":

*Examples:
Cei gellegion legentan?* "Why did the enemy not fight?"

*Certa sere nan?* "When is now; What time is it?"

*Icer legionês octante?* "How did the legions win?"

### Conjugation

Remean knows three regular stem classes: **Class A** stems end regularly in *-a*, irregularly in _-e_; **Class B** in a consonant; and **Class C** in *-i* or *-u*, exemplified by *vida-* "to see," *fid-* "to believe," *ai-* "to love", and *salu-* "to greet". Two of these deserve closer examination:

**A-irregular**, exemplified by *cide-* "to kill" and *grade-* "to walk": roots ending in *-e*, taking Class A's own endings throughout despite not fitting that class's nominal root ending in _-a_. It is be assumed that they once were regular _-a_ forms, now frozen at the ablaut grade: where *Vida-* alternates, shifting to *Vide-* in forms that call for it and reverting elsewhere, *Cide-* and *Grade-* have no unablauted counterpart to revert to, so every form in the paradigm attaches its Class A ending to the *-e* stem directly. *Cubere* ("to rule") and *Catere* ("to earn") belong to this same subclass.

**C-u**, exemplified by *salu-*/*salv-* "to greet": here the root ends in the glide /u~w/, alternating between *u* before a consonant and *v* before a vowel. The phoneme is the same throughout, but its realisation in written form alternates.

#### The Regular paradigm

|                 | A (*vida-*) | A-irr. (*cide-*) | B (*fid-*) | C-i (*ai-*) | C-u (*salu-*/*salv-*) |
| --------------- | ----------- | ---------------- | ---------- | ----------- | --------------------- |
| **Positive**    | vidare      | cidere           | fide       | aie         | salve                 |
| **Potential**   | vidave      | cideve           | fidue      | aive        | saluve                |
| **Irrealis**    | videsse     | cidesse          | fidesse    | aisse       | salusse               |
| **Affective**   | vidasare    | cidesare         | fidare     | aisare      | salvare               |
| **Past**        | vidante     | cidente          | fidente    | ainte       | salvente              |
| **Volitional**  | videri      | cideri           | fideri     | airi        | salveri               |
| **Negative**    | vidaran     | cideran          | fidan      | airan       | salvan                |
| **Imperative**  | videreo     | cidereo          | fideo      | aireo       | salureo               |
| **Adnominal**   | vidaretum   | cideretum        | fideretum  | airetum     | salvetum              |
| **Resultative** | vidatum     | cidetum          | fidetum    | aitum       | salutum               |
**Notes:**
- **Positive.** Both A classes take *-re*; every other class takes bare *-e*. This is the citation form that encompasses the Plain Present and Future tenses.
- **Potential.** The marker is *-v-*/*-u-*, the same glide C-u's own root shows, spelled *-ve* after any vowel-final root and *-ue* after a consonant-final one (*fid- + ve → fidue*, avoiding the consonant-plus-*v* cluster) -- one phoneme, conditioned by what precedes it.
- **Irrealis.** Three-way split by root shape: Class A ablauts its own final *-a* to *-ess-* directly (*vida- → videss-*); Class B takes a fully epenthetic *-ess-*; every other class (A-irregular, C-i, C-u) takes bare *-ss-* with no epenthesis.
- **Affective.** Class-conditioned, not sound-conditioned: A, A-irregular, and C-i take *-sare*; B and C-u take *-are*.
- **Past.** Bare *-nt-* for every class except B and C-u, which take *-ent-*.
- **Volitional.** A-irregular and C-i take bare *-ri* directly; every other class takes *-eri* -- Class A ablauts its own final vowel rather than taking a separate epenthetic *-e-*, so what looks like *-eri* is ablaut-plus-*ri*.
- **Negative.** B and C-u take *-an*; every other class takes *-ran*.
- **Imperative.** B takes _-eo_; every other class takes _-reo_, with A ablauting. 
- **Adnominal.** B takes *-eretum*; C-u takes bare *-etum*, dropping the linking *-r-* every other class keeps. Every other class takes *-retum*.
- **Resultative.** The direct stem plus *-tum*, except for B, which takes epenthetic *-etum*.

**Casual-register Class A reduction.** In everyday speech, Class A verbs are commonly reanalyzed as Class B: the *-re* Positive ending is reinterpreted as belonging to the root itself rather than being a separate class marker, so the "root" a casual speaker conjugates from is the whole citation form minus its final *-e* (*Quare → Quar-*), and every other form then follows ordinary Class B endings rather than Class A's own *r*-linked, ablauting pattern. *Quareri* ("let's drink," casual Volitional of *Quare*) is the working example -- the formal Volitional following Class A's regular rule would be *Queri*. This reduction is a general, productive feature of casual register, not a lexicalized irregularity specific to *Quare* -- any Class A verb is liable to show the same simplification in informal speech.

**Four fully irregular verbs sit outside the five-class system entirely.** *E* ("to be"), *Are* ("to do"), *Care* ("to give"), and *Nare* ("to become") each conjugate on their own irregular pattern:

|                 | *e* "to be" | *are* "to do"                    | *care* "to give" | *nare* "to become" |
| --------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ |
| **Positive**    | e           | are                              | care             | nare               |
| **Potential**   | eve         | (*Uve*, suppletive -- see below) | carve            | narve              |
| **Irrealis**    | esse        | asse                             | caesse           | narisse            |
| **Affective**   | sare        | asare                            | casare           | nasare             |
| **Past**        | ente        | ante                             | cante            | nante              |
| **Volitional**  | eri         | arri                             | caeri            | naeri              |
| **Negative**    | an          | arran                            | can              | naran              |
| **Imperative**  | eo          | ao                               | cao              | nao                |
| **Adnominal**   | eretum      | arretum                          | caetum           | naretum            |
| **Resultative** | etum        | artum                            | cartum           | natum              |
#### Conjugational Orders

**First order** -- Negative, Positive, Volitional -- is exclusive and necessary: every finite verb form carries exactly one of these three, and a bare stem with none of them is not a grammatically complete word.

**Second order** -- Potential, Irrealis, Affective, Past -- are infixes, stacking in this fixed sequence, always onto a stem that goes on to take its required first-order marker. Each step's own class-conditioning follows from the shape the stem has taken by that point, not from the base verb's original class. *Leg- + u (potential) + sa (affective) + retis (adnominal, animate) = legusaretis*, "who can be fought."

**Final order** -- Imperative, Adnominal, Resultative -- is exclusive and terminal, and the Adnominal and Resultative agree in animacy with what they modify, the same as any ordinary adjective. For final-order modification, the Negative forms irregularly on *-(r)anetum* for the Negative Adnominal, *-(r)antum* for the Negative Resultative (the Negative Imperative is formed periphrastically, see below).

**Periphrastic constructions.** Three specific combinations route through *Are* ("to do") rather than direct suffixation:
- **Imperative after Negative or Volitional** uses *Are*'s own Imperative, *ao*, instead of a suffix: *vidaran ao*, not *\*vidaraneo*. This informally elides to the fused *-nno* (*vidaranno*), which is not considered formally correct.
- **The inclusive imperative** uses the verb's own Volitional plus *Are*'s Imperative: *videri ao*, not *\*viderireo*.
- **Negative proposal** uses the verb's own Negative plus *Are*'s Volitional: *quaran arri*, not *\*quaraneri*.

**Density and periphrasis coexist by design.** Second-order stacking allows genuinely dense forms: *vidavessarentanetis legionaris*, "the legionary who might not have been able to see," chains Potential, Irrealis, Affective, Past, Negative, and Adnominal into a single verb form. The verb chain's length alone already strains legibility at such a depth, to say nothing of its enunciation in the mouths of hurried speakers. For this reason the periphrastic alternative exists: *legionaris as vidaretum uventanetis* (literally "the legionary towards-seeing who-was-not-able"), with the Adnominal used absolutely (hence no animacy agreement), avoids a heavily-layered stack for something both readable and speakable. The option remains, and a capable orator chooses based on clarity and economy.

**The suppletive Potential of *Are*.** *Are*'s own Potential is not the regular *-v-*/*-u-* infix but a wholly separate verb, ***Uve***, "to be able (to do)." Its root is *Uu-*,, and every vowel-initial ending reduces the doubled root to single *u* plus *v* (*Uu- + e → Uve*, the Positive), while every consonant-initial ending keeps both vowels, which then merge into one long *û*: *Uu- + ente → Uvente* (Past, vowel-initial *-ent-*, so the *v*-reduction applies), but *Uu- + sse → Ûsse* (Irrealis, consonant-initial, both vowels merging), *Uu- + tum → Ûtum* (Resultative, "able," the same merger), and *Uu- + etum → Uvetum* (Adnominal, vowel-initial *-etum*, so *v*-reduction applies again, not merger).

#### Volitional

Covers resolve, proposal, and conjecture. It is not a simple marker of desire -- wanting something is a separate matter the Volitional doesn't reach, only wanting to _do_ something, the speaker's disposition toward a proposition: settling on it, putting it forward, or supposing it.

*First-person resolve* is a decision towards a future action, often made in the moment: *Êgraderi* "I'll leave."

*Proposal*, a suggestion that includes the speaker, filling a gap the Imperative leaves open by proposing an action to a first-person plural: *Queri!*, "let's drink" -- readily turned into a question through ordinary verb-fronting: *Queri cu meis?*, "shall we have a drink together?"

*Conjecture*, often with *Eri*, the copula in Volitional. A predicate plus *Eri* frames the whole predication as supposition rather than assertion: *Legionaris zuaretis eri*, "the legionary is probably brave," against the flat *Legionaris zuaretis e*, "the legionary is brave." Because a bare Volitional could easily be misread in its proposal or resolve senses, the modal particle *Ema* ("probably") binds to the phrase to make the conjectural reading explicit: *Sis ema meam videri* "She probably sees me"; *ema benum eri* "It's probably good". Formal registers apply it consistently; everyday speech leans on context, uses _ema_ in a sentence-final construction, or drops it entirely.

*Negation* is periphrastic as mentioned above: the negated verb plus *arri*, the Volitional of *Are* ("to do"). *Quaran arri* covers both "let's not drink" for the proposal and "I won't drink" for the resolve, and context would supply which sense is meant. Where a previous proposal is answered in the negative, the verb drops out of the construction entirely and the bare negation particle stands in its place: *Queri? Ne arri.* "Shall we drink?" "No, let's not."

*The inclusive imperative.* The proposal sense intensifies into an inclusive command -- roughly what English reaches for with a bare present tense, "We march!" -- by appending *ao*, the Imperative of *Are*. *Legeri* "let us fight (as one)" is a legible exhortation, but *Legeri ao* "we shall fight (as one)" is a much stronger one.

#### Irrealis

A marker of unreality -- counterfactual, hypothetical, and wished-for states.

**Conditionals.** *Zei* ("if") marks a plain conjunction with no reality-marking of its own; it is the verb form that distinguishes a real condition from an unreal one. Both clauses take the Irrealis when the condition is unreal, so *Zei* alone marks which clause is the protasis. Remoteness is read off the Irrealis stem's own tense rather than needing a third form:

|                | Protasis             | Apodosis                                                         |
| -------------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Real           | *Zei dis venare*     | *mea dim vidare* -- "if you come, I see you"                     |
| Present unreal | *Zei dis venesse*    | *mea dim videsse* -- "if you were to come, I would see you"      |
| Past unreal    | *Zei dis venessente* | *mea dim videssente* -- "if you had come, I would have seen you" |
*The three conditional tiers.*

The Irrealis is not confined to conditionals. Its Volitional expresses a wish or regret at any person (*Videssei*, "would that one saw"), while its Imperative carries reproach or unactionable advice about something that cannot now be undone. The use of *Zei* splits the tone here: With it, the construction reads as an insubordinated conditional, a protasis whose apodosis is left unspoken, and the effect is wistful: *Zei odessenteo!*, "if only you had spoken!" Without it, the bare Imperative lands as flat rebuke. Polarity then sets what is being faulted -- the positive form reproaches an omission, the negative form reproaches the act itself: *Assentenno!*, "you shouldn't have done that!"

#### Participles

The Adnominal, *-(r)etum* and the Resultative *-(e)tum* work as a genuine present/perfect-participle contrast on the same root: *vidatum*, "seen" (resultative, patient-oriented) against *vidaretum*, "watching; who watches" (adnominal, agent-oriented) -- *vidaretum alva*, "the watching mother; the mother who watches."

Stacking the Past marker under this same participle produces an anterior/perfect sense -- "having X-ed" -- rather than an ongoing or habitual one, consistent with Past itself denoting a single completed event rather than marking any distinct imperfective aspect: *vidante* ("saw") + *-retum* → *vidanteretum*, "having watched"; *fidente* ("believed") + *-retum* → *fidenteretum*, "having believed."

Because the Affective is passive, a four-way aspect-by-voice grid can be established:

|           | Active                                                  | Passive                                                    |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Ongoing   | Adnominal: <br>*vidaretum*, "watching"                  | Adnominal-of-Affective: <br>*vidasaretum*, "being watched" |
| Completed | Adnominal-of-Past: <br>*vidanteretum*, "having watched" | Resultative: <br>*vidatum*, "seen"                         |
*The four participles by aspect and voice.*

#### Prefixes

Remean's set of prefixes are better characterized as **preverbs**: separable verb prefixes that are still synchronically identical to free prepositions, and may be used as such. However, some preverbs have functionally fused with the verbs they modify and can no longer be separated.

The split between dual-function and bound-only tracks how grammaticalized each prefix's sense is, and for one further reason besides. Prefixes that keep a concrete spatial meaning (*Aut*, *Autro*, *Ax*, *Cu*, *Din*, *En*, *Gen*, *Meter*, *Quis*, *Quo*, *Re*, *Ruc*, *Ura*, *Vala*, *Ventra*, *Vin*) retain independent word status. Prefixes that have drifted into abstract, grammaticalized senses -- *Ester-* as intensive ("completely"), *Sua-* as negation, *Zae-* as "changed into" -- have shed independent word status.

*Bi-*, *Poli-*, and *Un-* are bound-only not through semantic bleaching, but by their nature as numerals and quantity-words that don't naturalistically double as free-standing prepositions.

### Questions and Answers

Polar (yes/no) questions require verb-fronting: the verb moves to the front of the sentence, a dedicated word-order signal distinct both from the pragmatically neutral SOV default and from ordinary topical/contrastive fronting, which fronts a noun rather than the verb. Verb-initial position is reserved specifically to mark that a question is being asked.

A three-way particle optionally precedes the fronted verb:
- **Nu** -- neutral, no expectation either way. *Nu vidave meam?* "Can you see me?"
- **Nenu** -- *Ne* + *Nu*, contracted. Poses a negated question and expects a negative-confirming answer. *Nenu vidave meam?* "Can't you see me?" (expects *Ne*/*Vidavan* "No/I can't", contradicted by *Verei*)
- **Nam** -- independent, expects a positive-confirming answer. *Nam vidave meam?* "Surely you can see me?" (expects *Eo*/*Vidave* "Yes/I can")

In casual speech, the particle is dropped entirely and bare verb-fronting alone can be enough to mark the question: *Quarei cu meis?* "Shall we have a drink together?" (*Quarei*, the casual-register Volitional of *Quare* -- see Conjugation, above.)

**Answering.** Remean answers yes/no questions by truth value, not by echoing the question's surface polarity: the negative answer is a single plain form regardless of how the question was phrased, but the affirmative splits in two depending on whether it confirms a positive question or contradicts a negative one.

- **Eo** -- plain affirmative, confirming a positively-phrased question. Reuses the existing interjection of agreement (the bare Imperative of *E-*).
- **Verei** -- the dedicated contradictory affirmative, asserting "yes" specifically against a negative question's implication. It extends to statement-level contradiction generally, correcting or overriding a negative assumption regardless of whether that assumption was posed as a question.
- **Ne** -- plain negative, regardless of how the question was phrased. The existing negation particle used for its truth value.

### Modal Particles and Conjunctions

A closed set of short, invariant discourse particles adjusts a sentence's pragmatic force -- shared knowledge, reassurance, permission, warning, and the like -- without changing its truth-conditional content. Several modal particles have a dedicated grammatical function as sentence-final modals.

**Conjunctions** are a closed set of clause- and phrase-level connectors, mostly independent short words rather than derived forms. .

#### Sentence-Final Modals

The smaller set specifically used at the end of a sentence mark the speaker's own emotional stance toward what's just been said: whether it is an assertion, a bid for the listener's confirmation, or a hedge against certainty.

**Assertion**, speaker-oriented, scales from tentative to commanding.
- *Sta* is a weak assertion, often marking a decision or a suggestion the speaker is simply bringing to the listener's attention: *Meae êgradere sta*, "We should leave; We'll be leaving, then."
- *Eci* is higher in conviction, and presents a sentence with emphasis or urging: *Tea êgradereo eci*, "You absolutely should leave." 
- *So*, when used in this sentence-final mode, is the strongest expression of the speaker's will and conviction, whether in issuing a correction or an expectation of compliance: *Êgradere so*, "I will leave (and I expect you to come with me)"

**Confirmation**, listener-oriented, invites the addressee's agreement in favor of one-sided assertion.  
- *Nam*, the independent question marker, is used in sentence-final position to seek straightforward confirmation without forming a full question: *Sea are nam*, "she does, doesn't she?"
- *Ema* expresses a matter of sentiment and invites the listener's agreement: *Sea are ema*, "she does, I guess."
- *Quovo* projects confidence and expects acknowledgement: *Sea are quovo*, "she does, you'll see."

**Uncertainty** modals hedge the speaker's expectation of being in the right.
- *Tando* marks moderate uncertainty or doubt, often explaining a decision: *Meium sere tando*, "it might be this one; it would be this one."
- *Scai* marks high uncertainty or surprise, either as a rhetorical tag or a question marker in casual speech: *Meium scai?*, "it can't be this one, can it?; so it is this one after all; it's this one, really?" 

### Register and Respect

Remean speech in formal situations expects an elevated register to fit such interactions, and has four registers built from the level of the speaker's self-effacement on the one hand and the choice of direct or periphrastic assertion of the verb on the other. Each choice escalates deference on its own axis, and the two combine freely, giving four registers from two decisions rather than four decisions to memorize separately.

#### The Four Registers

|                                          | Ordinary self-reference (*Mea*) | Humble self-reference (*Mêr*) |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Finite verb**                          | Normal                          | Humble                        |
| **Periphrastic stem + *E*/*Are*/*Nare*** | Polite                          | Deferential                   |

**Normal**, used between equals, is ordinary Remean without modification: standard SOV order, the ordinary first-person pronoun *Mea*, the verb asserted directly.

*Example: Mea legionarim vidare.* "I see the legionary."

**Polite**, used with elders, guests, or anyone owed situational respect, holds the verb at a periphrastic remove while keeping the speaker direct: *Mea* remains, word order is unchanged, but the main verb surfaces as a bare stem governed by an auxiliary rather than as a finite form in its own right.

*Example: Mea legionarim vide are.* "I am one who sees the legionary."

**Humble**, a highly formal register that defaces the self in deference to someone of higher standing, replaces the ordinary first person with the proximal demonstrative *Mêr* and optionally usually fronts the object, while keeping the verb itself finite and direct.

*Example: Legionarim mêr vidare.* "The legionary, this one sees."

**Deferential**, the most formal register attested, combines both moves at once: object-fronted word order, *Mêr* in place of *Mea*, and the periphrastic construction together.

*Example: Legionarim mêr vide are.* "The legionary, that one is one who sees."

#### Humble Self-Reference

Humble self-reference is built on effacement of the speaker through use of the demonstrative in place of the pronoun: *Arvam mêrei mêr vidare.* "This one's mother, this one sees". However, a non-grammatical convention in polite speech is for a speaker in these registers to avoid ordinary personal pronouns entirely. Instead a title, kinship term, or office stands in place for whatever second or third party needs naming. Using a plain pronoun instead is not ungrammatical, only markedly less polite, and would be considered a kind of rustic bluntness, the sort of thing a Citizen of the mother city would be quick to notice in the speech of a courtier from the periphery, however correct the grammar otherwise is.

#### The Periphrastic Auxiliary

Polite and Deferential speech both route the main verb through an auxiliary rather than asserting it directly, and which auxiliary depends on what kind of verb is being deferred. Ordinary action verbs -- *Vidare*, and anything of similar outward, event-like character -- take *Are*. Verbs of a more internal character -- *Verare* ("to know"), *Âvie* ("to inhabit"), *Abe* ("to possess"), states rather than events -- take *E-* instead, the copula's own more static character suiting a verb that describes a condition rather than an act. In formations implying a change in such a state, *Nare* is used.

*Examples:* 
- *internal verb: Alva Remeam âvi e.* "My mother is living in Remea".
- *action verb:  Alva legionarim vida are.* "My mother does watching the legionary."
- *state-change verb: Alva ormi nante.* "My mother became sleeping."
	- Compare *Alva ormietis e.* "My mother is sleeping," a sentence in the normal register.

## Kinship Terminology

- *Arva* (bearing-parent) and *Fonta* (engendering-parent) are split by conception role, as sex is of little meaning in the ambigen Remean society (though there is reason to believe _Arva_ is the older term, predating widespread Harmonization, whereas *Fonta* does not).  *Arvila* refers to the siblings of the one's bearing-parent, while *Genta* is used for the siblings of the Fonta.
- *Enta*/*Monta* refer to one's older and younger siblings of the same parents as the speaker. *Brinta*/*Matrua* refer to all children of one's *Arvilae* and *Gentae*, ie, one's cousins.
- Descending, *Nata* refers to one's own child, while *Nepa* refers to a sibling's or cousin's child.
- *Seminila* is a grandparent regardless of side, and *Zaenata* (*Zae-* + *Nata*, "beyond the child's generation") are one's grandchildren.
- *Aionaris* ("spouse," *Aion* + agentive *-aris*) is the formal term used by outsiders to refer to a married person's partner; in everyday speech *Aina* "wife" is the more common term used by one spouse for the other, as well as outsiders with sufficient familiarity with the couple. In-laws are handled economically through an invariant categorizer for "by marriage". This is a metonymic derviation from *Nexilium*, the wedding sash tied around each spouse by the other during the ceremony. The lexicalized form _Nexilis_ is placed after any kinship term to derive its affinal counterpart: *Arva Nexilis*, *Fonta Nexilis*, *Enta Nexilis*/*Monta Nexilis*, *Brinta Nexilis*/*Matrua Nexilis*.

**Kinship gemination** is a dedicated hypocoristic (affectionate/diminutive) register specific to this vocabulary. Its clearest form is a *-nt- → -nn-* total assimilation: *Enta → Enna*, *Monta → Monna*, *Brinta → Brinna*, *Genta → Genna*. *Matrua → Matta* and *Seminila → Semmila* are shaped differently -- a *-tr- → -tt-* assimilation in one case, syncope of a medial vowel plus gemination of *m* in the other -- but read as the same underlying impulse: affectionate kinship terms contract toward a geminate consonant.

### Productive Markers

**From an adjective:**

- **→ Adverb, replace *-um* with invariant *-icer***. *Legerum → legericer*, "aggressively." Adjectives built on *-icum* show a distinct wrinkle: the suffix's own leading *-i-* elides against the root's final *-ic*, and the *c*'s left standing adjacent survive as a geminate rather than reducing further -- *aericum → aericcer*, not *\*aericicer*; *legionaricum → legionariccer*, not *\*legionaricicer*. No other adjective family shows this behavior -- *-tum* and *-rum* adjectives swap cleanly with no contraction.
- **→ Noun, quality of being X, replace *-um* with *-as***. *Legerum → legeras*, "belligerence"; *vidatum → vidatas*, "visibility."
- **→ Noun, thing that is X, replace *-um* with *-ans***. See product-of marker for verbs.
- **→ Verb, causative ("to make X"), reuses *-are***. Rather than a dedicated marker, the same denominal light-verb already established for nouns extends naturally to adjective stems -- suffixed to an adjective root, *-are* reads as causative rather than "to do", and conjugates as Class A instead of its irregular form. Attested across a substantial set: *Ampl- + are = Amplare*, "to enlarge"; *Trax- + are = Traxare*, "to lengthen"; *Aral- + are = Aralare*, "to heat"; *Frig- + are = Frigare*, "to cool"; *Desc- + are = Descare*, "to align"; *Ben- + are = Benare*, "to improve"; *Nov- + are = Novare*, "to renew"; *Sic- + are = Sicare*, "to dry out"; *Ten- + are = Tenare*, "to reduce." Two examples extend the rule beyond a base adjective root: *Vidat- + are = Vidatare*, "to visualize; to reveal; to show," built on the resultative adjective *Vidatum* rather than a simple root; and *Frigesc- + are = Frigescare*, "to freeze," built on the superlative stem *Frigescum* -- "to bring to the most-cold state."

**From a noun:**

- **→ Adjective, having quality of X, *-icum*** (after a consonant-final root; on a root ending in *-u*, that final vowel elides before the suffix). *Aer + icum = aericum*, "airy"; *legionar- + icum = legionaricum*, "like a legionary." The *-u*-final case is attested by the color adjectives: *Rubu + icum = Rubicum*, "red," not *\*Rubuicum*; *Veru + icum = Vericum*, "green," same pattern. This elision is specific to *-u*-final roots, not vowel-final roots generally -- see *-anum*, below, for a root ending in a different vowel that behaves differently. Where a noun has its own oblique stem (the *-c-*/*-ss-* infix that surfaces before its own case endings), that stem serves as the derivational base: *Selx*'s oblique *Selc-* gives *Selcum*, "salty," bare *-um* rather than the expected *-icum* by triggering haplology that simplifies \**selcicum* to *selcum*.
- **→ Adjective, relating to X, *-anum***. *Aer + anum = aeranum*, "of the air"; *legionar- + anum = legionaranum*, "relating to legionaries"; *Reme- + anum = Remeanum*, "Remean; relating to Remea." Phonologically distinct from *-icum* since the two senses -- qualitative vs. relational -- differ.
- **→ Verb, "to do X," *-are***. *Legion + are = legionare*, "to fight as a group"; *aer + are = aerare*, "to fill with air." Though suffixed with the irregular verb _are_, many forms are lexicalized as regular Class A verbs.
- **Place-of, *-(e)ton***. *Leg- + e + ton = legeton*, "battlefield"; *fid- + e + ton = fideton*, "temple; shrine"; *magi- + ton = magiton*, "school" -- Class C takes no epenthesis here, distinct from its behavior before *-tum* (see Epenthesis, above).
- **→ Animate agent, occupational, *-aris***. Names the person whose trade or role the base noun denotes, crossing into Second Declension animate regardless of the base noun's own declension. *Legion + aris = Legionaris*, "legionary"; *Eic- (the root of Eix, "animal") + aris = Eicaris*, "herder; stablehand"; *Lati- (the root of Latium, "clothing") + aris = Latiaris*, "tailor"; *Aion + aris = Aionaris*, "spouse" -- "one of the couple," distinct from both *Aistris*, "lover" and _Aina_ "wife" in naming a formalized bond rather than a romantic one.
- **→ Animate agent, replace *-ium* with *-ina***. Turns an institutional or abstract inanimate noun into the animate person associated with it, crossing into First Animate (*-a*). *Capitolium ("council; government") → Capitolina*, "councilor; magistrate"; *Navium → Navina*, "sailor"; *Fidestrium → Fidestrina*, "cleric." Attaches equally to a Fourth Declension *-ion* base, not just First Inanimate *-ium*: *Collegion*, in its institutional sense, *→ Collegina*, "co-worker; compatriot", and _Aion_ "couple" → _Aina_ "wife" -- the rule is "replace whatever inanimate abstraction with *-na*," regardless of the base's own declension, not specifically an *-ium*-to-*-ina* swap. Structurally parallel to the verb-based *-(s)tris*/*-(s)trium* pair below, which likewise splits an animate agent from an inanimate abstraction off a shared root.
- **→ Animate agent, add *-is* to a *-(a)ns* formation, giving *-(a)ntis***. The structural mirror of *-ium → -ina*, applied to the other inanimate-forming marker: when an *-(a)ns* "product of X-ing" noun needs to refer to a person rather than a thing, *-is* is added to the formation's own oblique *-nt-* stem -- the stem every case but the Nominative Singular already reveals -- crossing declension from Fourth to Second Animate. *Modisarens* ("something brought together; something unified") *→ Modisarentis*, "a Harmonized person; citizen." General rule, not specific to this one word.

**From a verb root:**

- **→ Animate agent, "one who Xs," *-(s)tris***. The default agentive across the verb system. On a consonant-final Class B root, epenthesis and the front-vowel allomorphy both apply together: *Fid- + e + s + tris = Fidestris*, "believer"; *Scirp- + e + s + tris = Scirpestris*, "weaver." Regular Class A verbs take no _-s-_: *Penna- + tris = Pennatris*, "writer." This agent-marker seems distinct from the simple affixing of the animate _-is_ evidenced in *Dab-* ("to eat") *+ is = Dabis*, "eater" or *Gelleg- + is = Gellegis*, "opponent", possibly following a pattern of habituality; we could plausible infer an unattested \**fidis* to mean "habitual believer", that is to say, "zealot". Other attested forms of this pattern of "verb stem + _-is_" are _Aris_ "doer; enactor" (possibly simply an irregular form) and _Elemis_ "soul" (contrast with grammatical but unattested *Elemestris* "one who manifests"). Cf. also the epithetic form seen in _Ertelema_, where the verb root _elem-_ takes the high-tier 1A _-a_ instead of _-is_.
- **→ Adjective, resultative ("Xed"), *-tum***. *Vida- + tum = vidatum*, "seen"; *forma- + tum = formatum*, "formed"; *ai- + tum = aitum*, "beloved," with the common epenthetic _-e-_ appearing after Class B stems. This is the patient-oriented participle, contrasting with the agent-oriented Adnominal participle in *-(r)etum* (see Conjugation, above). The regular output can also drift once lexicalized: *Plare* ("to fill") regularly gives *Platum*, "filled" -- the literal event-result, but the separate ablauted form *Pletum* has lexicalized in the more abstract sense "full" (a given state, rather than the aftermath of an event). Both forms coexist to distinguish which sense is meant.
- **→ Adjective, tendency ("Xive"), *-rum***. *Leg- + e + rum = legerum*, "aggressive; combative." Class A and C forms are untested (predicted *vidarum*, *airum*).
- **→ Noun, the state or capacity of X-ing (abstract), *-(s)trium***. The same agentive formant seen in *-(s)tris* above, *-(s)trium* names the state or capacity of an action itself, treated as a thing, the same kind of direct gerund-nominalization English *-ing* abstractions make (*believing*, *teaching*, *possessing*). *Fid- + e + s + trium = fidestrium*, "faith" -- the state of believing itself, not the state of being a believer. The front-vowel *-s-* allomorphy that governs *-(s)tris* carries over here identically -- *fidestrium*, matching *fidestris* -- so the two suffixes share their conditioning exactly, also attested on a diphthong root: *ai- + s + trium = aistrium*, "the state of loving; care," matching *aistris*, "lover." The Class C form is attested as well: *cri- + s + trium = cristrium*, "the state of producing; process," and by the same logic *Magie*'s abstract noun is *Magistrium*, "teaching," not *Magitrium*.
- **→ Noun, artifact used by/in X-ing, *resultative + -ium***. Distinct from *-(s)trium*'s abstract state/capacity sense: this names the physical object, a nominalisation of the resultative by shifting to declining inanimate (*-ium*) instead of adjectival (*-um*). Compare *Pennatium* "letter; rune; sign" to the bare resultative *Pennatum*, "written". Possibly identical or congruent with the simpler derivation seen in _Latium_ "clothing" from the verb _latare_ "to wear"; the unattested form _*Latatium_ might simply elide to _Latium_. But cf. also _Navium_ "ship" and _nave_ "to float", where the expected resultative formation would yield \**Nautium*. Most nouns attested in this simple artifact derivation can be considered very old formations, suggesting the resultative rule might itself be an analysis of this older pattern.
- **→ Noun, physical product of X-ing, *-(a)ns***. Distinct from *-(s)trium*'s abstract state/capacity sense: this is the concrete output. Best illustrated in the relation between *magi- + a + ns = magians* "instruction", _Magistrium_ "teaching" and _Magium_ "knowledge". The epenthetic vowel here is *-a-* for Class C (both C-i and C-u), while Class B takes _-e_: _magians_, _salvans_, _scirpens_. No epenthesis is needed where the stem provides the vowal as it does in *Modisare* (the Affective of *Modie*) *+ ns = Modisarens*, "something brought together; something unified." Every case but the Nominative Singular reveals the same oblique *-nt-* stem the *-ns* citation form itself obscures -- see Fourth Declension, above. Where the resulting concept needs to refer to a person rather than a thing, *-is* can be added to that oblique stem to re-mark it animate: _Modisarentis_ "Harmonized One; citizen" is the prototypical case, but see above.
- **→ Noun, the instrument used for X-ing, linking vowel where needed + Third Declension inanimate *-x***. Narrower and thinner than the other verb-derived nouns above, but attested twice independently. *Scirp- (the root of Scirpe, "to weave") + linking -a- + x = Scirpax*, "loom"; *Ala- (the root of Alare, "to sew," already vowel-final, so no linking vowel needed) + x = Alax*, "awl; needle."

**General:**

- **Stem crossing between declensions, with or without an infix.** A stem -- the bare root itself, or a root modified by the diminutive or augmentative infix, below -- can take a declension ending outside its own established paradigm, with no dedicated crossing-marker like *-na* or *-(a)ntis* doing the work; the ending itself carries the whole semantic shift. An infixed stem crossing to the thing-forming *-ium* names an artifact resembling the base referent, rather than a smaller, younger, or greater instance of the same one: *Cap- + il + ium = Capilium*, "hat" ("little head-thing"); *Man- + il + ium = Manilium*, "bracelet"; *Nex- + il + ium* -- attested as *Nexilum*, "sash." The augmentative takes the identical path: *Capitolium* ("government," literally "the great head-thing") is *Capi-* + linking *-t-* + augmentative *-ol-* + thing-forming *-ium*. A bare root shows the identical freedom with no infix involved at all: *Font-* (the root of *Fonta*, the kinship role) *+* Third Declension inanimate *-ax = Fontax*, "root" (botanical) -- the same wellspring/source concept, animate for the person, inanimate for the physical thing. Which inanimate declension gets used isn't fixed by the mechanism itself -- *-ium* for *Capilium*, Third Declension *-ax* for *Fontax* -- it's whatever fits the specific concrete sense wanted. It is also the mirror image of bare personification (Characterization, below), which crosses an inanimate concept into an animate being.
- **Diminutive, infix *-il-*, and Augmentative, infix *-ol-***, the same structural slot, opposite meaning. Both attach to a root, producing a new stem that ordinarily continues straight into the base noun's own case endings, staying in its established declension: *Arva → Arvila*, "little/dear Arva"; *Arva → Arvola*, "great mother; matriarch"; *Dentas → Dentolas*, "molar"; *Nexos → Nexolos*, "swordbelt." For most animal terms specifically, the diminutive narrows further to mean "the young/kit of that species", overlapping the endearment sense -- *Nubis* ("hare") *→ Nubilis*, "kit of the hare."Like any other stem, an infixed one remains free to cross into a different declension where the sense calls for it: *Capis → Capolium*, "dome; cupola" (literally "big head thing") -- see Stem crossing, above.
- **Characterization follows animacy's three tiers of reverence.** A root can take an animate case ending directly, with no dedicated derivational infix, personifying or characterizing what the root names.
  - ***-a*, the high/reverent tier.** Reserved for mythic, cosmological, or otherwise elevated personification. *Remea* (the city, "the mother-city"), *Zulva* ("tree," precautionary reverence), *Ventia*/*Solia*/*Cuneia* (Noble Element epithets, root + *-ia*), *Seziona* (*Sez* + collective *-ion* → *Sezion*, "the group of six," itself then personified), *Eia* (root *E-*, "to be," taking the same *-ia* variant *Ventia*/*Solia*/*Cuneia* show rather than plain bare *-a* -- "the being-one," the abstract force of life itself), and *Atra* ("bear," *Atr-* + *-a*, "the black one") -- a kenning placing the animal in the reverent tier.
  - ***-is*, the neutral tier.** Ordinary characterization and personal or group naming, with no reverence implied. *Dêcis* ("mind," the faculty rather than an agent, *Dêc-* + *-is*), *Alvis* ("Alvai," an exonym built on the color root *Alv-*), *Eis* (root *E-*, "person; human"). Compare  the animate agent marker above.
  - ***-ix*, the basic tier.** The default level, with no reverence or special characterization implied beyond ordinary animacy.

**Compounding:**

- **Preposition or particle prefixed directly onto a verb root**, shifting the base verb's sense metaphorically. The compound inherits the base verb's own conjugation class and any irregularity it carries -- prefixing doesn't reset it. Four examples attested: *Sû* ("under") *+ Dêce* ("think") *= Sûdêce*, "to remember" -- "to think beneath," retrieving what's buried under active thought; *Ne + Dêce = Nedêce*, "to forget" -- "to not-think"; *Ê* ("out of") *+ Gradere* ("to walk") *= Êgradere*, "to leave" -- "to walk out of," inheriting *Gradere*'s own irregular *e*-final conjugation; *Cu* ("with") *+ Venare* ("to come") *= Cuvenare*, "to meet" -- "to come-with." All four attach directly with no linking element, since in each case the preposition/particle ends in a vowel or the verb root begins with one.
- **Noun incorporated directly into a verb root**. *Eia* ("life") *+ Care* ("to give") *= Eicare*, "to give life," inheriting *Care*'s own irregular conjugation, the same way the preposition-plus-verb compounds inherit their base verb's irregularity.
- **Oblique-stem use in derivation.** Several nouns have a true stem that is oblique in the citation form -- the true *-ss-*/*-c-*/_-nt-_ ending of the stem surfaces only in case endings other than the nominative. It is that oblique stem, not a falsely constructed root derived from the citation form, that serves as the base for further derivation. Examples: *Rissicrinum* ("wool") built on *Rix*'s oblique *Rissi-*, not \**Ri-*; *Olecare* ("to stink") built on *Olex*'s oblique *Olec-*, not \**Ole-*; *Selcum* ("salty") built on *Selx*'s oblique *Selc-*, not \**Sel-*.
- **Prefix attached directly onto a noun root**. Prefixes established for verb compounding attach just as readily to nouns, carrying the same sense across. *Zae* ("beyond") *+ Latium* ("clothing") *= Zaelatium*, "cloak; coat" -- "over-clothes." *Re* ("down") *+ Nexos* ("belt") *= Renexos*, "breeches; trousers" -- "that which is worn below the belt."
- **Noun incorporated directly into another noun root**. Ordinary noun-noun compounding, the root of one noun prefixed onto the citation form of another, with the resulting compound normally taking the second noun's own declension. *Man-* (the root of *Mana*, "hand") *+ Angas* ("boot; shoe") *= Manangas*, "glove" -- "hand-shoe." This default can be overridden by the referent's own animacy: *Poli-* ("many") *+ Ornos* ("horn") *= Poliornis*, "stag; deer," surfacing animate despite *Ornos* itself being inanimate, because the compound names an animal.
- **Noun-noun compounding with contraction or elision.** *Mesas* ("honey") *+ Scix* ("insect") *= Mescix* "honey-insect; bee", not \**Mesasscix*. This is distinguishable from direct compounding by the fact that the prefixed noun elides or contracts from its citation form, not from its root.
- **Prefix attached directly onto an adjective root**, extending the same prefix set to adjectives. *Re* ("reversal") *+ Suatum* ("sharp") *= Resuatum*, "dull" (an edge lost) -- "un-sharp."
- **Adjective root incorporation**. *Ten-* ("small," the root of *Tenum*) *+ Esmicum* ("having quality of feeling") *= Tenesmicum*, "humble" -- "small-feeling."
- - **Boundary phonology in prefix compounding.** Once the prefix set (see Grammar, Conjugation, Prefixes) started compounding productively onto verbs, several distinct resolutions emerged at the prefix-root boundary, beyond simple concatenation (attested plainly in *Biabe*, *Autâvie*, *Rucode*, *Quopennare*, *Rebenare*, *Suafide*, *Genode*, *Autare*, *Enigne*):
  - **Consonant-matching gemination**, where the prefix's final consonant fully assimilates to the root's initial one: *Ruc + Care = Ruccare* (*c+c*), *Gen + Lege = Gellege* (nasal *n* assimilating to a following liquid *l*), *Em + Morsie = Emmorsie* (*m+m*).
  - **Voicing assimilation specific to *Aut*'s final *-t-***, attested twice: *Aut + Difare = Auddifare* (full assimilation to a following *d*) and *Aut + Vêle = Audvêle* (voicing without full gemination before *v*).
  - **Long-u vowel breaking before initial vowel**: *Sû* breaks its long vowel to form *Suv-* before the vowel-initial *Ode*, giving *Suvode*.
  - **Vowel contraction and merger**, two different resolutions for adjacent vowels at a boundary: *Re + Eleme = Releme* (identical vowels simplifying to one) against *Sua + Eve = Suaive* (dissimilar vowels merging into the diphthong *ai* rather than simplifying). Contrast with the resultative _Ûtum_, where the verb stem _Uu-_ carries meaning and thus forces long-vowel _û_ before the consonant instead of simplifying.
  - **Consonant-cluster simplification**: *Ax*'s underlying /ks/ drops its /s/ before a consonant-initial root, leaving plain /k/ (spelled *c*) -- *Ax + Lecâre = Aclecâre*.
  - **Haplology**: a repeated sequence at the boundary collapses to one instance rather than doubling -- *Ventra + Trare = Ventrare*, not *\*Ventratrare*.
  - **Vowel-cluster contraction across the whole boundary**: *Ventra + Ordie = Ventordie*, the *-a* and *O-* merging directly into one vowel. The resulting cluster _-ror-_ undergoes metathesis to _-orr-_, simplified to _-or-_.
  - **Consonant coalescence**: *-c-* + *-v-* at a boundary merges into *-qu-*, the cluster's /k/ and /v/ resolving into the single /kw/ sound *qu* already spells elsewhere -- *Ruc + Venare = Ruquenare*, "to return" ("again" + "to come").
  - **C-assimilation**. A *c* following _s_ doesn't survive as a separate consonant where it would immediately follow a syllable containing /k/ or /kʷ/, assimilating as a geminate instead: *Quis- + Care → Quissare*, not *\*Quiscare*. _Rubic-_ + superlative _-esc-_ → _Rubicessum_, not \**Rubicescum
