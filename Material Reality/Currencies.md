## **Lavion** -- _Livre Lavienese_

**Prestigious Coinage in Harmony with the Creed**
The Livre Lavienese is the prestigious coin of the Circulean's foremost power, and as such widely accepted. Rooted in the pious reign of Queen Dowager Ismara in the 1060s AR, the Livre was defined as an intentional mirror of the central creed of Lurism: _One is Three is Six is All_, represented through the **£** Livre (unity of the realm, Lur's Light), the **§** Sol (triune harmony), the **₫** Denier (six elements), and finally the **ɤ** Gros (360 as mark of divine completeness).

This 1:3:6:360 ladder was later amended to include the **₳** Argent as a coin of convenience, pegged at 30ɤ. The full ladder thus runs as follows:
- **ɤ** Gros (copper coins, usually pan-pressed on one side only)
- **₳** Argent = **30 ɤ** (small silver coin)
- **₫** Denier = **12 ₳** = **360 ɤ** (large hexagonal silver coin)
- **§** Sol = **6 ₫** = **2160 ɤ** (small triangular gold coin)
- **£** Livre = **3 §** = **6480 ɤ** (large gold coin)

Foreign merchants find the steps confusing, but the prestige of Lavion in diplomacy ensures its continued use; furthermore, the widespread adoption by Lavienese merchants of the Academic dozenal notation lead to the emergence of a mercantile shadow notation in multiples of dozens, which greatly simplifies accounting. These "shadow notes" are the **⨳** Grand (a gross of Gros, so 144ɤ), the **₰** Lune (1728ɤ), and the **⨝** Great Livre (20736ɤ), which represent 100₁₂, 1000₁₂, and 10000₁₂ respectively -- ie., ιοο, ιοοο, and ιοοοο in modern dozenal.

Hence we find that 1420s clerks post totals in **positional dozenal**, such that a sum of "1 Livre, 2 Sol, 5 Denier, 287 Gros" that is represented as "ι£ γ§ π₫ ιββɤ" in the classic ladder becomes "ϑππβɤ" (12887 Gros) in the modern dozenal notation. This mercantile shadow notation first lives only in bookkeeping and promissory notes; Grand, Lune or Great Livre do not appear as coins, as an early attempt to move the Livre to a dozenal standard failed to pass the Commons for fear of appearing impious. Market boards and taxes will quote the official ladder, the book with be in clean mercantile notation, and transactions above a certain threshold are simply settled by notarised bill of exchange or the aforementioned promissory notes.

Despite the widespread use of such notes, in 1420 Lavion has yet to print official paper currency. The Livre and Sol are both struck in gold, the former mostly used for large settlements between noble houses, foreign trade, and royal taxation. Denier and Argent are silver coins and the largest everyday coin that most people will interact with; wages and suchlike are commonly posted in Argents or Denier per Span, or Sol per year. Finally, the simple copper Gros is the most common coin in daily use, and its small size makes purses jingle when transported in large numbers.

By the 16th century's imperial age, things have changed considerably. Empereur Roburic II does push through a monetary reform and adopts dozenal currency wholesale, abandoning the ancient  1:3:6:360 ladder of Queen-Dowager Ismara as "infantile" after his falling out with the Church leads to the establishment of the Imperial Patriarchate. The Grand and the Lune, long firmly established in trade contexts, enter circulation in 1519 AR as 144ɤ and 1728ɤ paper notes; the Argent is fixed to 12ɤ instead of 30ɤ; Sol and Denier fall into disuse; and Livre begins to refer to the Great or Imperial Livre worth 20736ɤ, with the earlier coinage referred to as the Old or Royal Livre. 

## **Stranu** -- _Guilder of the Commonwealth_

^1c86a9

**Sexagesimal money of account, with many fractional coins for exact change**
The Guilder arose as a standardisation of the mint during the 9th century AR; the name "Crown" was proposed, but considered too aristocratic for the ethos of the Commonwealth.

It became the **coin of maritime trade** during the Age of Corsairs and remains the most accepted Circulean coin in the distant Rim of Fire, as well as the money of account in all major guilds and trading houses. In the 1420s its value compared to the Livre is generally pegged at **₲1 = £4**, though tariffs and exchange fees will impact the clean conversion.

The ladder itself is cleanly sexagesimal, with one gold **₲ Guilder** equalling 60 silver **M Mark** (silver), itself divided in to 60 silver **$ Shilling** (silver), and finally 60 copper **p Pence**. Specialised fractional coins are common: denominations of ½, 2, 3, 6, 10, 12, and 30 exist for marks, shillings, and pence, so exact change is easy without cutting or weighing, though trying to remember all the individual names of these smaller subdivisions drives many foreign merchants to distraction. Shilling are also printed in paper form, making them a welcome alternative to cumbersome coinage or more elaborate promissory notes.

 Despite (or because of) this complexity, the banking establishments of the Commonwealth are the most sophisticated in the Circle Sea: Their promissory notes backed by Guilder reserves are widely accepted in major harbors, including Balboa, Riv-î-Tep, and even parts of far-away Vel Kiriya.

By the 16th century, the Guilder is predominantly a paper currency for anything larger than a Pence, but stubbornly keeps its complicated base-60 notation.

## **Svenu** -- _Pund & Skilling_

**Bullion-based economy** 
In Svenu, there is no standardized mint until the Imperial age. Before then, payment is bullion-based, with the Pund (**₻**, 1 pound of silver by weight) as the accepted means of financial exchange. Traditionally this came in the form of hacksilver -- weighted bracelets and bars pre-scored into twelfths called _skilling_ that could be broken off cleanly without a scale, a convenience in remote ports.

Outside Svenu, hacksilver is tallied by weight to ensure no clipped or underweight pieces slip through, then settled in whatever coin is at hand. Conversely, merchants in Svenu readily accept foreign gold and silver coins, though further inland people will be hard pressed to give change.

Other than weight, the shape and size of the Pund is far from standardized, allowing large variety: Bars are stamped with the mint seal, while armbands or torques are often decorated with knotwork or personal/family marks; and in fact such decorated hacksilver can carry a slight prestige premium exchange rate over the raw silver.

By the 16th century, the monetary practices of the Circulean have given rise to individually minted skillings, modern coinage in the form of wedges such that multiples form a circle; these exist as _tvieki_, _thrieki_, and _fjorfeki_ -- "halfs", "thirds", and "quarters", thus worth 6, 4, 3 sk respectively.

## **Vel Kiriya** -- _Velkirian Dinar_

**Imperial coinage dominating the Eastern Gyre**
Velkirian commerce is predominant in the east, owing to the historic stability and riches of the northern realm. Its reach is evident in the Merhal region, as the city-states of Riv-î-Tep use the Velkirian system wholesale, though though occasionally strike local designs for domestic pride.

The gold **Đ** Dinar is the largest denomination, mostly used in tribute, large-scale trade, or to pay military stipends. It is worth 120 **₭** Kurush, silver pieces that dominate ordinary commerce. Below that, the copper **₧** Piastre handle most daily transactions at 40₧ to a Kurush.

The 120-fold division is a legacy of an older sexagesimal reckoning, doubled for ease of fractional calculation, as Vel Kiriya is yet to switch to dozenal notation. Letters of credit in Guilder are honored in the big harbors, but cash settlement will always skew Velkirian along the Eastern Gyre.
