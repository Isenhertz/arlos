### Overview

In the so-called Rivian Epoch -- approximately the thousand years before Reme's rise -- a succession of sorcerer-kings dominated the lowlands of Riv-î-Tep, centered on the Merhal Floodplain along the eastern Circle Sea littoral. Their polities were anchored in temple-cities engineered as resonance machines, inaugurating with the First-and-Only and continuing through his Successors to the later Lesser Kings. Across centuries these regimes weaponized hydrology, acoustics, and coerced bodies -- notably the _Hatekin_ drawn from the Alvai Kin of the Night Jaguar -- to extract labor and impose order. Later fragments tell of an end by absorption rather than apocalypse: after 1024 BR, the rising Remean power advanced eastward along the Gyre, domesticating or dismantling resonant infrastructures, taxing temple economies, scattering the _Hatekin_, and converting miracle into maintenance.

### The First-and-Only

**Emôn-Dju-Bâ-Mhâhat-Nmu First-and-Only** rose in the shadow of prehistory, but his reign marks the beginning of what scholars now term Antiquity. Made powerful and immortal by means unknown (likely lost arts of massed harmonic choirs of blood-bound slaves, effigial sacrifices and architectural resonance of a grand scale not attempted since), he ruled for centuries until he was usurped and toppled by Set-Ahotep-Imekh Breaks-The-Crown around 1650 BR. More sorcerer-kings followed, often competing and displacing one another, until Reme's conquest of the Merhal in 1024 BR.

The First-and-Only ruled from a city known only from fragments of surviving vassal states as **Pta-Amon-Mhâhat Riv-î-Tep Adjeh**, believed to now lie under the silts of the Mer-Hal delta. His court gave the entire region its toponym -- Riv-î-Tep -- that remains in use even today, but all surviving accounts agree that it was a theatre of terror: ritual combats, blood harmonics, and forced rites of binding shaped the population into instruments of his power. The **Alvai Kin of the Night Jaguar** were enslaved and corrupted under his reign, becoming the feared _Hatekin_ -- a lesson in the brutal utility of shaping not just the elements, but the very souls of beings.

Many surviving cities in the Merhal plains owe their founding mythos to the First-and-Only, or one of his many successors, all of whom attempted and failed to surpass his grandeur.

### The Two Successors

**Set-Ahotep-Imekh Breaks-The-Crown** (r. c. 1650 to 1450 BR). Lover, lieutenant, and executioner of the First-and-Only, he shattered his inviolable rule in a rite that cracked the sky. The usurper expanded the grand choirs of blood with machinic architectures of resonance: mile-deep shafts sunk into the ground and latticed with copper-plated bones, terraced step-fields where irrigation pulsed by harmonic cadence, and causeways of fused basalt binding vassal cities to his court. He kept the _Hatekin_ as his terror-guard, but collared them in electrum masks engraved with nulling sigils. In the end he was undone in the intimacy of his own sanctum, toppled by the one he had raised closest.

**Fa-Hê-Rhet-Bâ-Estu She-With-Skin-of-Silver** (r. c. 1450 to 1350 BR). The silver was not metaphor but artifice: a skin of quick-forged electrum impressed with water-lines and moon-seals, a body made mirror. She called herself Sorcerer-King in the manner of the age and tolerated no lesser styling. Her rites prized reflection and recursion -- mirror-galleries that sang back a single note in thousandfold, canals tiled in polished obsidian, and cohorts of eunuch adjudicators whose faces were plates of bright metal. In her time, the court drifted between river-capitals whose names the present only half remembers -- Tla-ê-Amah, Il-î-Amah, and Sip-ê-Apah among them -- each tuned to her itinerant pageant. After a century on the throne she was overthrown by three of her sworn lesser lords, who parceled her silver into temple weights and divided her domains.

### The Lesser Kings

From She-With-Skin-of-Silver's fall, around 1350 BR, the Merhal belonged to no single throne. The **Lesser Kings** -- sorcerer-kings in name if not in magnitude -- ruled city by city for some three and a half centuries, their reigns overlapping, colliding, and wearing the people thin. Broken stelae and clay tallies preserve dozens of their names, few in any certain order, and none of them commanded what the High Kings once had.

**Thesun-Khetep** of Omt-î-Thes, the city he founded, was the last of them to attempt anything larger. Around 1050 BR he subdued the long-independent Cult of the Golden Bull at Op-ê-Aru, the final effort of any Lesser King to bring the Merhal under one hand.

**Shen-Uru-Atum Binds-the-Reeds** held the Mer-Hal delta in the same years, and tried to knit the lowlands with living dykes of woven reed and clay, seeded with sprites of Water and Soil to self-heal cuts.

Both fell in 1024 BR. Reme's legions, fresh from the completed synoecism of the Ertran cities, broke Thesun-Khetep at the walls of Omt-î-Thes and marched on into the delta with engineers and iron discipline. They cut Shen-Uru's living dykes with stone saws tuned to Tension, burned the reed-sprites with dry salt, and took the delta city by city. Shen-Uru's barge was found beached and empty below a sky of black gryphs that were not gryphs.

Within a generation the Merhal breadbasket lay under Remean assessment. The Hatekin were scattered into the jungles of the north and east, the temple-cities paid tithe in grain and labor, and the grand architectures of resonance were repurposed into mundane works: weirs made mills, obelisks became milestones, and choir-pits echoed with market shouts. Thus ended the Rivian Epoch -- not in a single fall, but in a long unmaking where empire proved more patient than sorcery.
