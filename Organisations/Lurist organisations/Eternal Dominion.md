_"Not of Crown nor Foculier, but of Oath and Sword."_

The Eternal Dominion is the crusading tradition of [[The Light|Lurism]]. The name has served in turn for a hope, a mandate, a theocratic polity, a scatter of sworn companies, and, in the present age, the Church Militant: the knight-monasteries that hold the passes, relic sites and pilgrim roads of the [[Circle Sea]] under the nominal headship of the [[Church of the Light|Hierophant]]. Its motto is older than any Order that now bears it. It comes from the unbound companies of the Dark Ages, who swore to an oath and not to a see.

## The Phrase

The words belong to Judge Bar-Samniya of the Outer Way (288-251 BR), who first wove the harmony of the six into doctrine and spoke of "the eternal dominion of the Light". His sayings were preserved in the *Book of Judges*, which Saint Virellian compiled in the closing years before the Fall of Reme. In Bar-Samniya's own mouth the phrase was a hope: that the Light's harmony would one day prevail over the world as morning prevails over night, without anyone needing to impose it.

The harder reading belongs to Judge Tar-Essari the Stern (177-136 BR). In An-Suur the temple-arbiters had held deposits, witnessed contracts and stood surety between rival cities, and when their neutrality came under suspicion the cities counted them oathbreakers, the gravest offenders their moral reckoning knew. The Verse-Keepers inherited the arbiters' functions, and with them the question of what the faithful owed to a world that broke its oaths. In that generation dominion stopped being something to await and became something owed: a mandate to correct, carried out by those willing to swear themselves to it. The oath, and not the crown or the office, became the Dominion's measure of lawful authority, and the later motto is its compressed form.

## The First Militants (177-7 BR)

For a century and more the Communion of the Enlightened had been beneath [[Remean Empire|Remean]] notice. That ended with the martyrdom of Judge Saint Illut-Kharun at the stake in 177 BR, sentenced by a tribunal of Remean provincial officials. A devotional movement became a persecuted one, and the faithful began to arm. Companies of sworn lay-brothers formed to guard congregations, caravans, pilgrims and the copies of the *Verses*, taking their oaths before the Verse-Keepers founded in those same years. They bore no common name. They were the Judges' hosts, and their war-priests marched under the Judges' authority and not their own.

The conflict escalated across the next century and a half. Judge Nallume the Binder led besieging fleets against the Remean provincial capital of [[Paltos]]. Judge Mazuren the Patient wore down Remean outposts in the west through long attrition, leading the crusading fleets. Wherever they reached, they carried the dozenal notation of the Creed to replace the empire's sexagesimal reckoning. The Dominion's own chroniclers count these companies as its first brothers, though the name was not yet theirs.

Not every follower of Kharun took up the sword. His disciples fled north and became the apostles of the [[Eastern Rite|Kharunites]], who kept to the road and its law and stood apart from the war.

## The Theocracy of Eternal Dominion (7 BR-336 AR)

The name became an institution with the investiture of Maverel the Undimmed in 7 BR. A congress of Dominion commanders and senior canonists enthroned the First Hierophant, and the polity that followed called itself the Theocracy of Eternal Dominion. Within a year it commissioned the fleet meant to deliver the war's culminating stroke against [[Reme]], sailing from the harbors of [[An-Suur]].

The fleet never arrived. The Fall of Reme in 0 AR scrambled the Western Gyre and swept the crusaders far south of their target, and they came ashore to find a devastation with no name. They called it the Light's own verdict on the empire's arrogance, and that reading became the Theocracy's founding story. The zeal that had built the fleets was left without its climax and sought other heathens.

### The Century of Crusades

The First Crusade (0-8 AR) was a direct continuation of the Remean war, carried into the highlands of the Wolves' Teeth against the [[Moonkin]], whose half-animal bodies the war-priests read as one more result of shaping the flesh. The Second (21-25 AR) hunted for hidden Shapers in the high valleys. The Third (40-47 AR) burned Alvai sanctuaries and necropoles older than the Prophet. The Fourth (66-72 AR) was a sequence of punitive pogroms and forced baptisms that sealed the Scattering.

Beside the campaigns the Dominion governed. Monasteries became schools and pilgrim roads became trade roads. The Theocracy suppressed the old elemental cults along the Varsa, consolidated An-Suur and Stranu, and pressed missionaries and warbands south through the passes. Balboa, pragmatic and mercantile, opened its gates to the faith to keep peace with the northern powers. Every tithe, toll and tribunal was justified as part of the great balance, and the more the Theocracy promised unity, the more it needed enemies to define it.

## Resistance

The Moonkin held the Wolves' Teeth longer than any army of the age expected. Their homeland had stayed free against the empire itself, and their captains fought as they always had: patient as snow, trading ground for seasons, content that a living clan was victory enough. Each crusade cost the Dominion more than the last and ended in greater cruelty. The Scattering broke the highland bands into diaspora, but a last enclave high in the range was never found.

The [[Sunkin]] of [[Lu-An]] were never brought under heel by conquest or conversion, and no Lion-Blood banner ever marched as a Lurist levy. In the Dark Ages a pretender hierophant's crusade against them, staged from the march of Fiurisci, failed. The [[Zanori]] of the grass confounded the Dominion's cavalry, which pursued their raiders too deep into the plains and met recurved bows and thrown spears at full gallop.

In a later age the Dominion marched up the Swicca against [[Karsova]]. The Karsovans rode and rafted, trading ground for time until the winter river froze their foes to a standstill, and the Charge across the Swicca that turned the tide and drove the crusaders out remains a household tale there. [[Svenu]] and its kin refused missionaries and the Dominion alike, and its raiders struck coasts no Circulean captain dared approach. The Kharunites of the Eastern Rite lay beyond the Theocracy's reach, and when the Dominion later tried to contain them, it was from fortress-monasteries along the Salt Road.

The Theocracy's end came from within. In 327 AR the [[Starkin]] prince Ejitwe gathered exiles, outcasts and disillusioned converts in the jungles of the Anish homeland, where the Theocracy had grown fat, and in 336 AR his partisans breached [[An-Mirda]] after forty days of siege. The Thronebreaker burned the archives and left the city in ruins as a warning.

## The Dark Ages (336-998 AR)

The Sack of [[An-Mirda]] killed the Theocracy but not its cadres. The crusader companies, sworn to an idea and not a place, kept driving south and east in failing campaigns against the Southlands and the plains, winning victories they could not hold and suffering defeats that taught them nothing. Crusader polities went on claiming the Theocracy's name for centuries, and An-Mirda itself never left Dominion hands: it is governed to this day as a theocracy by a council of hierarchs who answer to the Patriarch of the North.

Without a strong primate to harness them, the unbound companies hardened their creed into the form the motto records. They held that the Light's authority flows through holy action and sworn oath and not through clerical consecration. In the far-periphery fortress-monasteries of the Lupines and the Salt Road, some drifted into a quasi-Manichaean reading of the Light, and from there into the elemental heresy that makes Fire, in the person of Phlogistophor, the Light's Executioner.

The Second Hierophancy tried for centuries to bring them under rule. By the seventh and eighth centuries crusading had become campaigning by commission, with Orders chartered for frontier defense, punitive excursion and the relief of harried towns. The reforming pulse of the early 800s demanded their obedience to synod and purse, and the work was still unfinished when the death of Hierophant Solvrand III in 888 AR began the Interregnum. In those leaderless decades the Orders guarded holy sites, patrolled pilgrimage paths and learned to measure victory in peace kept. Zeal became administration, and administration became peace of a plain sort.

The Settlement of the Varsa in 998 AR completed the taming. Genovrast de Lavion's recognition of the Hierophant gave the Church a single lawful voice again, and the crusading bands were consolidated into the Church Militant, sworn to the Hierophant's warrant and answerable to synod. A mutual suspicion between the Dominion and the sees outlived the settlement.

## The Church Militant

The Hierophant is the Church Militant's nominal head, and the Dominion confirms the bond by oath. The Preceptors of the major Orders meet in a Grand Chapter, a rare gathering used to resolve disputes between Orders and to elect a High Marshal, who is the ceremonial and sometimes the functional head of the Dominion in the field. The Orders keep their knight-monasteries on mountain passes, old trade roads and relic sites.

The Order of the Revelation was once the most prestigious, and its seat in An-Mirda was lost in the Sack. Its remaining brothers are scattered across ruins and act as relic-keepers. The Order of the Kindled Standard is a fanatical purifying order, often accused of brutality, and still active along the southern frontiers. The Order of Saint Adamar is famed for discipline and deep theological learning and keeps strong ties to the Patriarchate of the North. The Order of the Gilded Anchor keeps a small preceptory in the old port of [[Principality of Balboa|Balboa]], where it guards relics recovered from Circle Sea expeditions.

The Orders are the natural home of Antidiscordianism. The Dozenians keep their severe discipline in the Dominion's knight-monasteries, the Canticlers serve in its courts, and the Lucentians stand watch over its reliquaries and ruined sancta. The Orders preserve martial liturgies, sacred chants and relic-trials that the main Church no longer practices. The Dominion's officers hold rank by styles of their own: Knight-Prelates, styled His Martial Grace, rank as marquises in military procession and as bishops in clerical affairs, and Canon-Militant hold the courtesy of abbots.

The Ordinate of the Flame is the Dominion's ecclesiastical ally when the Church Militant is summoned, and the two cooperate in rooting out heresy in fringe provinces. A Writ of Censure requires the joint consent of a regional Patriarch and the Hierophant or an Exarchal proxy, a constraint learned from the excesses of the Second Hierophancy. The Orders guard pilgrims, shrines and remote parishes where clergy are sparse, as the Custopontians do with hostels and mediation. Some Patriarchates, above all the North and the Lupine see, regard the Orders as guardians of spiritual purity.

Where governance is weak, in mountain passes, river deltas and desert fringes, the Orders sometimes act as de facto rulers, and peasants and local lords alike look to them for justice, defense and theological arbitration. They number a few thousand knight-brothers and twice that many lay warriors and initiates. They are no longer kingmakers, but they retain influence in regional affairs, particularly in succession crises and under external threat.

### Secular Powers

The Kingdom of Lavion tolerates the Orders, especially where the Crown's reach is thin. During the War of the Three Kings (1117-1121), some Orders on the northern slopes of the Wolves' Teeth sided with the King in the South, in a quarrel over the charters and budgets the Settlement had laid on them and the Crown's claim over their muster. In the reign of Queen Lurie-Mavrelle the Dominion is viewed with quiet suspicion, and she allows it to operate so long as it claims no territorial sovereignty.

Balboa keeps an armed neutrality with the Dominion. Lauralotta di Balboa is not hostile but regards it as anachronistic and ideologically dangerous, and she keeps a close eye on its presence in the upper passes of the Wolves' Teeth. To the faithful and to their enemies alike, the Dominion remains a symbol of unbending faith and a purer, nobler past, both feared and romanticized.
