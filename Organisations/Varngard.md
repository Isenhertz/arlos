## The Gulfward Council of Oar and Axe

Varngard sits between the Western Gulf and where the upper Swicca tumbles down from the Wolves' Teeth, along a navigable river called the Varn. Its founders were Svenic merchant-warriors who sailed through the Teeth's lee, planted stockades on morainic bluffs, and bound scattered market-towns into a league. They came as winterers and middlemen, not conquerors, and their blood mixed early with whatever forelanders were here before them. In time the league learned the river as well as the sea, running shallow prams up the Varn and out again with furs, wax, linseed, pitch, and cold-cured fish. The gulf keeps Svenu close but separate; Varngard trades with Svenic ports while jealously guarding its own harbor rights and the chain-booms that seal its inner roads when trouble rides the wind.

Varngard's land is a crescent of orchard hills above overdeepened lakes, flattening into loam-rich fans that meet the river’s natural levees. The eponymous capital Varngard leans into a stony headland where an old sill breaks the fetch. Up-coast lie Haldorshavn and Vargness, fishing towns fattened into customs posts; inland stand lake-cities like Tervin and Albrunna, which load barges under the teeth-like spurs. The portage between the Varn and the Swicca is one of the major hubs of the region, giving the eastern Southlands a viable path to the Gulf, and thence the Ocean.

Government follows a conciliar model: each chartered town sends a dozen electors to the Great Hall of Oar and Axe -- boatmasters, war-captains, guild heads. A grand councillor -- the Master of the Oar -- presides as first among equals for a term reckoned in dozens of months; a war-master -- the Master of the Axe -- commands levy and fleet when the warning-horns sound. The old Svenic matrilineal habits did not survive whole, but women of the guilds keep the purse and a public voice, and estates can pass to daughters without scandal. Moonkin Alvai refugees from northern pogroms founded the Silver Stair quarter in Varngard rather than scatter across the land; they brought polished glass, iron-working, and a distrust of overreach. Oar and Axe protects them because their craftwork and quiet counsel have saved the ports more than once.

Faith here keeps to Lur's Light in a Redemptionist cast while tolerating small-house invocations to Morwynt for a fair wind and Maregina for a safe return. Memories of Eternal Dominion crusaders still come with the taste of smoke -- the worst sack fell on Haldorshavn barely two centuries ago, and the council rebuilt its walls in squared stone after swearing an oath on twelve iron nails. Raids from Svenu and Lu-An taught the coast to drill: each town keeps a ready corvée of archers and demands a bill and axe in each household; each harbor watches the water with chain, fire-raft, and signal-mast. Trade policy is militant in its own way: tolls are tiered by hull width and draft, lighterage is a guild monopoly, and the council keeps a close eye on who travels through the portage.

For a northerner used to melodious Virellic or vibrant Balboan, Varngard's names run oddly oldfashioned, with a Svenic cant: men called Hrodmar, Varnabog, Jernalfr, Siguord; women called Brynild, Alet, Gudrana, Skara, Hristja.
