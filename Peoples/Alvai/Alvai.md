The Alvai, a long-lived, non-human people, are intimately bound to certain Animal Spirits. Each major **Kin** of the Alvai carries the physical and spiritual mark of one of the great Spirits. Their animal traits -- such as furred limbs, tufted ears, slit eyes -- are not mere biology, but a living inheritance from their patron Spirit. This bond is at once sacred and irreversible.

In general terms, the Alvai tend to be tall and lithe, but the spectrum here is wide: a Starkin adult might reasonably be measured at a foot and a half-pole tall and be deemed average, whereas the tallest of Sunkin warriors who ever lived, Majira the Sundered, reportedly stood a full pole tall at rest, towering over all her contemporaries like a solitary mountain over the lowlands.

Common to all the Kin (as they call themselves) are animalistic features; each differs according to their patron, but all are furred from the waist down. Body hair generally tends to come in tufts along spine or forearms (pale, blond and silvery tones in Moonkin, coppery gold and reds in Sunkin, dark blues and blacks as well as greys in Starkin), and their hair can in some become a mane marrying the shoulders to the head; facial hair, too, is common. Claws and fangs again differ by patron: the Starkin bear sharp digitigrade talons with a raised heelspike where the Sunkin, also digitigrade, have padded feline hindpaws; the Kin of the Moon instead have fully planted but furred and visibly clawed feet.

A similarly shared trait is the nature of their voice, described as both sonorous and ethereal -- a fact due to their naturally occurring bitonality, akin to what in humankind is approximated in techniques like throat singing. Alvai speak with two independent tones at once, which features heavily in the dialects of their native language [[Ngerhemäir]], whose syllables have a high and a low tone that differentiate a word's meaning. In human tongues that lack such tones it can alternately be enchanting or haunting, depending entirely on which tone the Alvai chooses to give their words.
