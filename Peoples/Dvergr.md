Though considerably more insular than other civilisations that crowd the [[Circle Sea]], the Dvergr that inhabit the highlands and mesa of their homeland of [[Sarm]] do have a presence outside their ancestral holdings. Their trading posts dot the ports and markets of [[Kingdom of Lavion|Lavion]], [[An-Suur]] and as far south as ice-bound [[Svenu]] and [[Varngard]] beyond the [[Wolves' Teeth Mountains]]. True settlements -- called Holdfasts or Halls -- are rare outside Sarm, but exist intermittently throughout the Circulean, each a polity onto itself.

Any compounds that house larger groups than a handful of traders are invariably guarded by a highly drilled detachment of Dvergr *falangi* or "spearmen", whose tightly arrayed ranks present any opponent with an almost impenetrable wall of shields bristling with triangular points. Though their tactics are not known to be innovative, their efficiency and effectiveness is undebatable, and their word first gave the Circulean the concept of the "phalanx" that was then so ruthlessly mastered by the ancient [[Remean Empire|Remeans]].

But what the world sees as a race of squat, bearded, stoic males is a half-truth -- the visible crust of a much deeper stratum. In reality, Dvergr civilization is caste-bound, eusocial, and geode-born. Each caste serves a vital function, not imposed by law but encoded in form and purpose -- an organic structure, inherited from their progenitor, Grandmother Lode, the Noble Element of Stone.

## Physiology
The Dvergr are shaped by darkness, stone, and the long memory of caverns. Their bodies skew stout and tendon-thick, with big dark-adapted eyes that hold a pistoned iris, feet that grip like a second pair of hands, and a pelt that runs from crown to heel. Facial hair is not ornament but organ: moustache, brow, and cheek carry dense vibrissae that read air the way fingers read embossed surfaces, letting a Dvergr feel pressure shifts, distant tread, and the faint wind of a breathing shaft.

They speak with the tongue, the "low speech" of everyday work, but move through the dark by "stone speech", a lattice of soft ultrasonic clicks providing a sonar sense so habitual that even their lullabies are such pulse-rhythms; this also gives rise to their "wall song", which uses the same clicks for subtle, far-reaching communicative purposes, one without an outsider audience.

In youth their posture is a low, long-armed stoop; with age they thicken, scapulae vaulting forward until the eldest castes habitually knuckle-walk. Crucially, Dvergr growth never stops -- it continues through life, modulated by diet, labor, and clan rites, such that the elder shapes that surface folk call "trolls" are simply seniors of the line, grotesque only to eyes that do not know how to read a Dvergr's years.

No outsider has ever seen a female Dvergr, but this is not for some reason of taboo. It is so because Dvergr, as a people, do not procreate in the way that the many facets of humankind and indeed the Kin of the Alvai do. Neither are Dvergr male at all -- they merely express features that in other peoples are read as such. In fact, their expertise in mining and stonecraft is not, as often portrayed, based in a greedy hunger for gems and fine metals, but instead forms the basis of their continuance: for it is a fact not discussed nor disclosed to outsiders that Dvergr are born from geodes.

### Youth
In the depths, they scout seams and bring home geodes that ring true in a harmonic chord that fits the Hold. In the creche each geode primed by seedwrights with a mineral serum of trace proteins and microflora that encodes the Hold's long memory. But lineage still matters: for each geode, a vizier contributes a seedstone, shaved from calcified plate and set like a kernel at the geode's heart. Under warmth and a steady attention the lattice reorders around serum and seedstone until cracknight, when the shell parts and a pup slides free. A Dvergr's lineage is both audible and civic at once: in a given Hold, all cohorts share a simple baseline chord in their stone speech, like the great houses of humankind share a banner, and each line in turn bears the quiet stamp of a vizier's seedstone in appearance and disposition. 

The reason surface folk hold on to the superstition that all mountains are home to dwarves is because in some cases, certain geodes quicken on their own to the the world's slow metronome. These wildborn seamlings are rare and precious to the Dvergr, the stuff of sagas. A Hold that finds one will bring it home and raise the child like any other, but the sign of the wild will follow them for life.

### Adult Age
Licheners -- caretakers given a special charge -- cultivate milk-stone to feed young and old alike. These pale felts of lichen line the brood-troughs in many separate grades of "brew" -- ones for fine motor and tunnel work, for plate and tendon, for health. Caretakers mix these with specific broths, mash, and mineral licks to guide the new generation, for in Dvergr society, it is not his clothes that maketh man, but his diet. By late youth -- measured in some decades by human reckoning -- the body forks by habit and diet to either remain compact and tireless, or to put on tendon and plate faster -- Workers or Warriors, depending on the Hold's need and circumstance. The transition is not heroic, merely practical, and when need calls, the change may be induced even late in life.

Either way, growth never quite stops. Workers will eventually outgrow the mining tunnels before their second century, and thence move naturally into the caste of Porters -- the load-bearing answer to stone stairs that no beast can climb -- while Warriors age into Guardians, knuckle-walking hunchbacks laden with plates, strong enough to shoulder entire seams during quakes, hurl boulders from the back ranks, or anchor shield-walls. These, when seen outside the hold, are what has given rise to folktales of "stone trolls" in the deep mountains. For both these plated protectors and the Warriors from which they spring, plate-carving is both an expression of the self as well as a medical art for pain relief; many will turn to Caretaker bonewrights to file down calcified ridges, cap errant spikes, and keep nerve pinch at bay.

### Old Age
As growth continues, in late life -- the third or fourth century -- the body chooses to return to stone. Metabolism slows, plates lay down like masonry, whiskers stiffen, and the voice drops to a cellar note a human would more feel than hear -- the slow dignity of the Vizierate. As bone continues to thicken and joints calcify, a Dvergr's posture drops further until even walking is ceremony, a procession with slings, rails, and laughing apprentices to steady the old one's pace, and when forced to travel longer distances, a vizier will call on palanquins carried by Porters. In a hold, its viziers arbitrate shifts and feuds, keep law and memory, and guide the younger generations -- day by day less mobile, mind still sharp -- until at last the thrum of their inner chord ceases to be heard, and they become a funerary stele of their own making. A Dvergr mausoleum is thus not a yard of graves but a hall of elders finally at rest, an archive of faces, ridges, and lineage.

## Creche Practices
Creche practice varies by tradition. Old Halls keep the ancient way -- geodes chosen by taste and ring, seedstones set forth with oaths, warm shelves of brick and wool, lullabies in stone-speech, and a strict taboo on etching the crystal face. Modern Halls add lapidary craft -- the inner face grooved, "inked" with metals, salts and nutrient paste, hum-stacks calibrated to hold a precise note -- but still both paths share the same civics. Reproduction is a hold-wide rite -- setting days when cohorts of geodes are brought from seam to shelf, seedstone processionals where elders leave a sliver of themselves to pledge a future, cracknights when the parish above hears the soft tap-song of hatching through the halls. Succession to the hall of elders is natural, and among the Dvergr power has never been held singly or by might alone.

Their law reflects these stakes. Geode theft -- from seam or shelf -- is treason against tomorrow. Seedstone tampering is worse still, a sacrilege against the elders' vow. A blight on the milk-stone is an alarm that outranks pride, calling every caste to scrub and sing and save the jars. Tampering with mineshaft or ventilation is grounds for exile if not worse. The result is a people whose birth, growth, and memorial are one continuous stonework: from seam to shelf to shift to stele, every Dvergr is set by Stone and returns to it, adding one more tuned pillar to the living architecture of the Hold.

## The Dvergr Beard: Sensorium of Stone

Among the Dvergr, the beard is not ornamental, nor merely a marker of age or dignity. It is a primary sensory organ, a bundle of hyper-sensitive whiskers capable of detecting minute vibrations, shifts in pressure, and even subsonic resonance.

These whiskers are true vibrissae -- hair set in thick follicles packed with nerves -- arrayed at brow, moustache, cheek, and jaw. Not every hair on a Dvergr's face is a whisker, but the learned can identify the longer, thicker hairs from the ordinary ones. With age and plate growth the whiskers stiffen and lengthen, shifting sensitivity downward toward the low rumbles that Viziers read best. Loss is not merely cosmetic, but a sensory maiming on par with blinding or cutting the ear -- the world goes dull, corridors lose their edges, and the hum of stone speech turns to mush.

Given the significance of these beards and the importance of keeping the whiskers in good care, combs are tools of calibration more than vanity, different cuts with gauges for a specific fan angle and tooth pitch for a given whisker bundle. Beard oils and waxes are as important. Beads, sleeves, and split-rings act as quick-change fittings: color tells caste and current duty at a glance; weight and placement tune a whisker's resonance the way a luthier weights a string.

In war, Dvergr tuck their beards behind specialized helmet plates and grilles, allowing sensation while protecting the sensitive filaments in combat. These protections have a limit, as whiskers do grow back, unlike eyes or ears -- so the Dvergr armory makes some allowances in accounting for beard protection, but none that would go beyond the pale.

## The Unrooted: Dvergr Beyond the Holdfast

Though Dvergr society is defined by cohesion, not all Dvergr remain embedded within the structure of their birth-vein. Throughout the history of Arlos, a small but consistent trickle of “Unrooted” Dvergr has found its way into the surface world, not as envoys or warrior detachments, but as individuals.

To humans, they appear little different from any other Dvergr, albeit perhaps more curious, more willing to speak, more at ease with the uncertain rhythms of the wide world. While the Dvergr language has no true word for "exile" or "wanderer" -- only circumlocutions that express detachment from home and harmony -- and the idea that one can exist meaningfully on one's own in the outside world is deeply unsettling to the caste-bound cohorts, there exist several reasons or way a Dvergr might become solitary:

1. ***Voluntary Departures***
   Some Workers, during missions abroad or long service at surface compounds, develop a personal dissonance -- a yearning, inexplicable and unresolvable, that drives them to remain behind after their rotation ends. They often take up solitary trades or dabble in dangerous undertakings, and though they are rarely loquacious, they are often intensely perceptive.
2. ***Severed by Accident or Trauma***
    In rare instances, a Dvergr may become cut off from their holdfast through catastrophe, interference, or war. These individuals often suffer a deep spiritual vertigo, like a deafening silence. Some adapt, becoming cautious sages or mad tinkerers. Others never recover, and walk the world like stones cracked from the quarry, purposeless but still enduring. If there is talk of a savage troll in the high country, it will be one such maddened soul.
 3. ***The Quiet Agents***
    There is reason to believe that some Unrooted are not truly unrooted, but rather deep plants placed deliberately within human societies to observe or influence. If so, they maintain irregular contact with their home halls if at all, and likely report to their viziers through means unknown.
