# Greetings and Partings

## Elemental boons as everyday invoking

Asking for elemental boons is a widespread form of everyday invoking, and it fits nearly every culture on the record. It is highly contextual, so the request can be chosen to fit the situation. A guest may ask a blessing on the stone of a host's house, and a traveller may be wished a speedy trip upon the wind. Such greetings and partings are invoking in the everyday sense, the folk courtesies owed to the forces of the world, and distinct from aetheric practice. Under centuries of Lurist influence such phrases have softened from what in earlier times might have been actual prayer, such that modern phrasing need not name the [[Noble Element]] itself; but when they are named, it is on purpose.

## Remea

The standard greeting among Remean citizens is the forearm clasp, wrist to wrist, so that the pulse points meet. The choice follows from Remean Shaping, which works through pulse and breath resonance, so the greeting joins two people at the place that matters most to the culture's craft. The clasp is always symmetric, whatever the rank of the two parties. Deference is shown by speech and by who releases first. A farewell uses the same clasp, held longer and released by the one who is leaving.

For kin, lovers and cohort-mates there is a breath exchange. It also has a ceremonial role, for instance at Harmonization. The spoken forms are formalized and centre on health, in both greeting and farewell. The verb *salve* means "to greet". Its passive, formed with the Affective, gives the imperative *salvereo*, "be greeted", which serves as a neutral greeting.

Fire and Water lead in elemental invocation. Rubicessa keeps the hearth and Amaregis protects at sea. The circumstance decides which takes precedence, and other elements are invoked when they fit the occasion better.

## Anish

The Anish greeting is a three-part gesture. Both hands go to the heart, then to the lips, and then spread outward with the palms up. The other party mirrors it. Between loved ones the middle step becomes an articulated blown kiss. Either side may then initiate a clasp of both palms. In an oath, the same clasp is held until both sides have finished speaking the words, and this mutual clasp is the origin of the later immixtio. The Anish do not use a forearm clasp, because that reads as Remean.

The spoken forms are highly formulaic inquiries about health and business, and the correct order matters. Host and visitor each ask a fixed set of subjects.

Before the Revelation, Water held the highest precedence in Anish elemental invocation. After the Revelation, invoking the elements became rustic and was censured during the Long War, though it was never eradicated. It survives mostly in household and sea use.

## Balboa

[[Balboa]] combines the Remean and Anish forms into a crossed-arm clasp, in which the two parties' arms cross over each other. It is the Remean-style clasp with the Anish both-hands form added. The superior's arm lies on top. Women count as superior in polite greeting even among peers. The inferior lifts the superior's hand for a hand kiss. For intimates the Remean breath exchange softens into cheek kisses.

Two spoken forms mark the culture. *Nesci* comes from an Ancient Remean phrase meaning "I shall be your inferior". It is casual and used among peers only, a self-abasing formula worn smooth by daily use. *Nescimeri* is the fuller form toward betters and still carries real self-lowering.

Water leads everywhere in Balboan elemental invocations, and is unusual in how openly it invokes Maregina in one of her periphrastic epithets.

## Lavion

Lavion greets by bowing, in a manner inherited from the tribal retinue practices and customs of the peoples inhabiting the [[The Varsenne Basin|Varsenne basin]] before the arrival of Lurism. It runs from a superior's nod of attention to full prostration before the Hierophant. Oaths use the clasped hands, which Lavion took from the Anish through Lurism and which is integral to feudal fealty: the higher party clasps the lower party's hands, which follows the early church's upward-only reading of the Six Relations.

Spoken greeting has two layers. A peace formula with a fixed reply serves church, court and formal contact. A time-of-day form that invokes the Light, in the manner of "Light of the Morning to you" and its kin, serves ordinary life.

The formal peace formula names the Six together, as the Light's order. Folk life follows the ancient pattern of asking one element to bestow a boon as the situation warrants, quietly and without much clerical comment.

## Svenu and Varngard

In [[Svenu]] a greeting is a handclasp that runs into a shoulder bump. Assent is shown by a weapon-clash, a foot-stomp, or the drumming of fists. Respect toward a bonded element is shown only when the bond is present, in keeping with the bonding customs of moling and adaling, skipling and aettling. A host presents a guest to the clan's hearth-spirit or the crew's ship-spirit. The spoken forms are "Well met", answered "Well come", and the farewell "Be hale".

Formal greeting invites the recipient in the name of the Hearthkeeper. The parting names the kin of the road ahead, such that for a voyage at sea they call upon Sister Storm and the Sea-Mother, while for roads the parting asks for the blessing of Sproutfather or Iron Brother, or in the depths of winter, the mercy of Granny Ice-Death.

[[Varngard]] keeps roughly the Svenish forms, with a bear hug added after the shoulder check. Houses keep their own customs, so the greeting at the door shows which side a house stands on. Lavion-oriented houses bow. The spoken forms are those of Svenu plus imported Lavion-inspired Lurist phrasings. Elemental invocation follows the Svenish pattern. Progressive houses open with the Lurist formula and still let the kin names slip in.

## Stranu

The Stranic greeting is a handshake, descended from the Svenish clasp, with a shoulder touch from the other hand, which mirrors the Anish double clasp. Formal occasions add a ritual of offered and declined precedence, in which each party presses the other to go first. The spoken form is a Concordian peace formula with a fixed reply. In elemental invocation Water and Soil are most often heard, matching the culture of communal rice terraces and the importance of water law in the region.

## Kharunite realms

The Kharunite greeting begins with an eyebrow flash and a met gaze. It continues into the unveiling, or the upward sweep of the palm across the face that imitates it. Near an elemental manifestation (such as a brazier, a water feature, or even just a rock of significance) the sweep starts at the element present, linking it to the individual.

The realms differ in the contact that follows. In [[Merhal]] a person washes with the water at hand and then clasps. Spit is a rustic substitute for the water. In [[Lu-Pto]] a person spits on the ground as an offering. Both customs are offerings to Water, but the spitting in particular invites much misunderstanding from foreigners. In Kiriyan court customs meanwhile, deference is shown and graded by what is kissed: mouth, cheek, hand, feet, in descending order of equality. The foot-kiss takes the place of what is prostration in the west, and is reserved for the King of Kings alone.

The spoken form is a fixed two-part inquiry about the road behind and the road ahead, asked even of people who have not travelled. Formal contact invokes all six elements in a sequence around the ring. The sequence starts at the element nearest to hand (usually the one already included in the gesture) and then follows the ring order in the full form such as may be heard at the beginning of a Kharunite sermon.

## Ukili'ata

The Ukili'ata greeting involves no contact. The arm sweeps toward the horizon and flows into a welcome to the vessel or home, indicating the threshold or the planks. The formal welcome is an exchange of tokens. The spoken forms ask where the other is bound and where from. Air is invoked first, then Water.

## Alvai

The [[Alvai]] peoples differ by kin. [[Moonkin]] in Circulean cities greet in the local form and add a low bitonal word beneath it, which other Kin hear and outsiders do not. The svartalfr keep the old highland guest-right forms in a purer state. [[Starkin]] leave an offering at a boundary mark and wait. The visitor stays at the mark until the band takes the offering up and receives them.

[[Sunkin]] within a pride extend their claws, tilt the head to expose the neck, and draw the other's forehead into head-to-head contact with the clawed hand. Toward outsiders Sunkin show an open palm and hold the gaze, a gesture as imperious as it is unmistakable.

Alvai do not usually invoke the elements in their greetings or partings, though they ask for elemental boons as much as any other mortal. The Kin among themselves invoke the patron spirit only.

## Dvergr

Among themselves the [[Dvergr]] exchange clicks and touch their own fists knuckle to knuckle, held at different heights as befits the caste and position of the recipient. Toward outsiders they use only the fist gesture, with a spoken greeting built on a mother-stone invocation. Stone is the only element invoked, through Grandmother Lode.

## Karsova and Lu-An

The riding cultures of [[Karsova]] and [[Lu-An]] greet with a drink offered without dismounting, which is safe-passage hospitality. The spoken form includes an inquiry after the livestock. Formal occasions use a scarf exchange, which is distinct from the Kharunite veil exchange. Water is invoked for the drink and Air for the road ahead.
