The Zanori are a centauroid people native to the wind-scoured grasslands and sun-drenched savannahs of [[Lu-An]], far in the southeast of [[Rimec-Suur]]. Their bodies blend the lithe upper torsos of furred humanoids with the lithe lower forms of swift-running quadrupeds, with slender but powerful limbs, tufted tails, and digitigrade talons. Most striking are their backward-curling horns, which vary in form from slender spirals to broad lyre shapes, often decorated with bands of pigment, caps, or tokens of past battles and trades.

Notoriously insular, the Zanori travel in tight-knit kinbands across lands they claim not as possessions, but as ancestral memory -- sites of old births, deaths, and vows. Each band is led by a triarchy of the strongest male, the oldest female, and a Skykeeper, chosen by vision or challenge, who communes with the weather-spirits and reads the flight of gryphs to chart their path through the seasons. Their language, rich in whistled inflection and ululating tone, is difficult for outsiders to master, and without script.

Their repute across the [[Circle Sea]] ranges from awed to uneasy: with recurved bows of sinew and horn, and long bone-tipped spears thrown from full gallop, Zanori raiders have shattered infantry since the days of [[Remean Empire|Ancient Reme]], and confounded cavalry forces of the [[Eternal Dominion]] foolish enough to pursue them too deep into the grass. Yet not all their encounters are bloody. Zanori caravans have been known to emerge from the morning haze bearing dyed silks, rare roots and seeds of medicinal value, and bone chimes that sing with the wind.

No outsider can know in advance whether a band seeks trade or tribute when it draws near. Tribute may be demanded in the form of tools, food, or captives -- not out of cruelty, but as repayment for trespass on unseen boundaries. Trade, when offered, is often conducted through elaborate gesture and symbolic gift-giving, where the wrong color of cloth or the wrong season of offering might offend or amuse. Many a merchant has walked away baffled, whether enriched or very suddenly poorer.

Among scholars of distant [[Virell]] and the [[Grand Academy]], debates continue over the Zanori's origin: whether they are a people bound by some forgotten pact to a [[Noble Element]] or [[Animal Spirit]], or whether they are a result of pre-Lurist shaping arts now long lost. But to the Zanori, such questions are meaningless -- they are what they have always been: the children of the prairie, their hoofbeats a living thunder, belonging to none but the open sky.
