# A Chronology of the Circle Sea Basin
 
*From Prehistory to the Imperial Age*
 
This chronology gathers the principal events of the Circle Sea basin in order. Dates are given in the near-universal civil convention, which counts outward from the Fall of Reme: years before that event are marked **BR** ("Before Reme") and years after it **AR** ("After Reme"), with the Fall itself fixed at **0 AR**. That civil count aside, the beginnings of historical reckoning differ by tradition: the *recorded* historical record is conventionally opened at the reign of the first Sorcerer-King, Reme kept its own account from the founding of the mother-city, and Lurist historiography proper begins later still, with the Prophet and the Fall. Era boundaries are approximate and, in the transitional centuries, deliberately overlapping.
 
---
 
## Prehistory (earlier than the 20th century BR)
 
- The proto-[[Anish]] population in [[An-Suur]] begins to spread along the jungle coast, becoming the first Circulean people to turn to the sea.
- Agriculture arises in the [[Merhal]] floodplains.
- The [[Ukili'ata]] arrive from the [[Open Ocean]] in the west on hydrofoil skims and catamarans, having abandoned their home in the [[Rim of Fire]]; they will guard their knowledge of these technologies ferociously.
- Early riverine polities form at the confluence of the great rivers Mer and Hal in the Merhal estuary; kelp, resin, and shell become prestige trade goods across the [[Circle Sea]]'s eastern coasts.
- [[Sexagesimal]] tallies emerge, likely in response to [[Belana]]'s predictable 60-day cycle and the need to predict the floods of the Mer-Hal floodplains.
- Elemental worship is widespread and natural, from simple animist veneration of household sprites to formalized pacts with local courtiers, or prayer to the [[Noble Element|Noble Six]].
## The Rivian Epoch (20th century BR to 11th century BR)
 
- Emôn-Dju-Bâ-Mhâhat-Nmu First-and-Only achieves immortality and frightening power, rising as the [[Sorceror-King]] and ruling the Merhal from Riv-î-Tep; historic reckoning begins with his reign, though the reign's own beginning is lost to the depth of time it opens the record for. He binds [[Noble Element|Morwynt]], the Noble Element of Air, captive, and by the bound might of her own captivity raises Riv-î-Tep bodily into the sky.
- Three [[Animal Spirit|Animal Spirits]] are broken into service of the First-and-Only, their [[Alvai]] Kin forever altered by their spirits falling.
- Under the First-and-Only and his successors, the sorcerer-kings rule the northeast, extending deep into [[Lu-Pto]] and [[Lu-An]], but butt up against the [[Dvergr]] of [[Sarm]] along the western escarpments of the floodplains.
- Set-Ahotep-Imekh Breaks-The-Crown ends his predecessor's roughly three-and-a-half-century reign around 1650 BR (supposedly goaded into action by Morwynt, still held captive within the sky-city she was bound to sustain) and casts his body into the estuary; he rules in turn for some two centuries before he is done in by his own lover, Fa-Hê-Rhet-Bâ-Estu She-With-Skin-of-Silver, around 1450 BR.
- Freed of her chains by the First-and-Only's death -- whence her kenning "Chainbreaker" -- Morwynt turns on the sky-city that had been both her prison and an affront to her element, and ends it; no single tradition agrees on where the wreck came down, variously placed in Lu-An, in Lu-Pto's [[Sifted Fields]], or among long-vanished side-arms of the Mer. She then seeks out her tormentor's body in the estuary where it was cast, cannot find it, and is left without closure; legend holds she hunts him still, the [[Wild Hunt]] at her heels. (Later Lurist tellings recast the object of her search as the First-and-Only's Soul rather than his body, loosed to run its long cycle of refinements.)
- Fa-Hê-Rhet-Bâ-Estu, last of the High Kings and styled king in the same manner regardless of her sex, reigns a further century from Tla-ê-Amah and elsewhere before falling, around 1350 BR, to a triumvirate of usurpers rather than to a single hand as her two predecessors had; where one conspirator had twice sufficed before, three were needed here, and it is hard not to read more into that difference and the fact that after her, only Lesser Kings follow.
- Rivian invocation traditions reach their heights under the High Kings: bloodcrafted slave choirs sing in tuned chambers used in civic rites and statecraft, enabling the ancients to achieve feats as yet unmatched, before or since.
- The rule of the Lesser Kings begins around 1350 BR: many, contemporaneous, and city-based, ruling the Merhal's floodplain piecemeal for the better part of three and a half centuries, markedly diminished in whatever the High Kings once commanded.
- [[Svenish]] boat-clans first arise in the southwestern fjords, raiding and trading along the Open Ocean rim, beginning a long tradition of raiding and intermingling.
- City-temples consolidate the Merhal with irrigation law, levy labour, and sexagesimal accounting; caravan roads bind the coasts from Lu-Pto to Sarm.
- Trade flourishes between Ukili'ata homestead fleets and Anish ports; Rim-wise seamanship becomes a cultural badge.
- The first kings of [[Vel Kiriya]] rise in the highlands far north and establish satrapies along the upper Mer, skirmishing with the later Lesser Kings of the delta.
- Early city-states of eastern [[Ertra]] cohere, then expand eastward into Lu-An, coming into early conflict with the Lesser Kings from the south.
## The Remean Era (11th century BR to 1st century AR)
 
- Around 1050 BR, Thesun-Khetep, last of the Lesser Kings, subdues the long-independent Cult of the Golden Bull at Op-ê-Aru. It is the final attempt of a Lesser King at any sort of consolidation of the Merhal.
- A decisive win over Thesun-Khetep at the walls of Omt-î-Thes in 1024 BR gives [[Remean Empire|Remea]] the keys to the breadbasket that are the floodplains; subjugation of even lesser opponents follows swiftly. Grain and tribute that once went to the courts of sorcerer-kings are now loaded onto the slow barges that float southwest with the Eastern Gyre's patient turn, to support and succor [[Reme]]. With this conquest, the synoecism of Ertran east-coast polities finalizes its turn from confederation to factual empire.
- Shaping enables superhuman qualities even in everyday people, but blunts progress in most tools other than alchemy; elite arms such as the belly-bow and the sarissa depend on shaped bodies and tuned frames.
- A staging harbour and accompanying fort are founded on the tip of Ertra, where the currents of the two Gyres meet, the *statia vala buia* or "stations between the currents" in the tongue of Reme, which will be the seed from which [[Principality of Balboa|Balboa]] geminates.
- In [[An-Mirda]], the Prophet [[Lur-Illuhan]] declares a spiritual renewal in the name of the Light (355 BR), having experienced a profound moment of connection and clarity; Judges arise in his wake as arbiters of nascent [[The Light|Lurism]], and soon come into conflict with the empire.
- First tribes consolidate in [[Stranu]]; proto-principalities arise, largely left unmolested by Reme but swiftly brought under Lurism by the Judges.
- c. 300 BR: the Light sets foot in Rimec-Suur -- Remean stations along the Suurian Gate and the Circle Sea littoral, long since too far-flung for the empire to support, are condemned and razed with little fight, while the inland tribes convert in uneven waves by choice or under pressure; local clergy begin blending Remean structural habit into Lurist rite, the first faint strands of what will become [[Concordianism|Concordian]] thought.
- Reme eventually rules the east and much of the southern coast of the West, its polyremes, legions, and prestige projecting the thalassocracy's law, architecture, and citizenship; local variations of elemental cults are tolerated if orderly.
- Svenish raiders begin delving past the [[Suurian Gates]] and well into the Gyres; first evidence of lasting Svenish settlements south of the [[Wolves' Teeth Mountains]], in what will eventually be [[Varngard]].
- The Long War: the martyrdom of Judge Saint Kharun (177 BR) turns a movement Reme had tolerated into a militant one, and the continued expansion of Lurism under a unified and increasingly theocratic polity catches the empire on the back foot; Reme falls back from its scattered western holdings toward Remea proper, until Balboa stands as its last door into the west and the crusaders muster to besiege the harbour as the opening move of a campaign into the heartland itself.
- The [[Eternal Dominion|Theocracy of Eternal Dominion]] follows the last of the [[Orthodox]] Judges and welds zeal to civic order, the investiture of Maverel the Undimmed (7 BR) closing the age of the Judges and opening what later historiography calls the First Hierophancy; dozenal notation rides with the crusaders to replace Reme's sexagesimal system everywhere they can reach -- though the western tribes, Lurist already and never crusade-conquered, keep the sexagesimal reckoning they had taken up on their own.
- Fall of Reme (0 AR): an attempted binding of [[Noble Element|Phlogistophor]], the Noble Element of Fire, unleashes a cataclysm that buries the Remean heartland and scrambles the Western Gyre, sweeping the Balboa-bound crusading fleet far south of its target so that the crusaders neither take the door nor witness the empire's end. The empire's arts are proscribed by the crusaders in the years that follow (though some knowledge survives by shedding the Remean connection), and the Circulean world turns toward a new age.
## The Age of Enlightenment (1st to 4th century AR)
 
- The Century of Crusades: denied the final confrontation with Reme, the crusaders begin pogroms against the [[Moonkin]] in the Wolves' Teeth Mountains, eventually driving them into diaspora.
- Elemental [[Invoking|invocations]] are disgraced under the harsh lens of dominant Antidiscordianist sentiment; the *damnatio memoriae* of Reme leaves only scattered and accidental (or else entirely hostile) accounts intact.
- In the east, the [[Eastern Rite|Kharunite]] Rite matures, incorporating older traditions of venerating the elements as acknowledging the Light in All.
- 143 AR: legates from An-Mirda collide with increasingly localist bishops in Stranu, the dispute foreshadowing a western insistence on liturgical self-governance.
- Across the 2nd century AR, provincial workshops quietly recover the retort-distillation of zinc lost at the Fall -- yielding purified zinc, and with it a true brass of even, dependable grade far beyond anything the older cementation could give -- restoring the precision-castable alloy on which all later Circulean tube-drawing, instrument-boring, and pressure-work will depend.
- 201 AR: the First Parliament binds nine nascent principalities to mutual aid and the arbitration of water law, convened at [[Santra]] by custom rather than any fixed calendar; it is reckoned ever after the Commonwealth's first Parliament, though a fitful one, kept alive by drought and flood more than by any lasting princely appetite for union.
- The Theocracy ossifies; partisans under the Thronebreaker sack An-Mirda (336 AR) and end it factually, though its name continues to be claimed by crusader polities for centuries to come. Later historiography draws its line between the First and Second Hierophancies here -- much as it marks the coming of the Third at [[Genovrast]]'s settlement of 998 AR -- the numbered Hierophancies being a retrospective scholarly convenience the Church never applied to itself.
- 337 AR: with the seat of An-Mirda broken, the Western clergy refuse to acknowledge a successor, and a synod at Santra articulates the Covenant of Concord, giving formal shape to the [[Concordianism|Church of the West]].
- The flight of An-Mirdan exarchs to [[Aramoor]], and the rankled response from the remaining Northern ecclesiarchy, set the stage for the problems of the Second Hierophancy.
- 367 AR: the Charter of Santra. Drawn chiefly by the Church's own business -- the Covenant of Concord needs a permanent seat -- principalities from across the peninsula gather at Santra, consecrating it the Synod's home and fixing its autocephaly within the West in law and custom; swept up in the occasion, the assembled princes also swear to labour "for the Common Weal of the Princes of the West," giving the [[Commonwealth of Principalities]] its lasting name centuries before it has any government to answer to it.
## The Dark Ages (4th to 10th century AR)
 
- Multiple claimants and braided obediences between the nascent patriarchates fragment the church. Anti-Hierophants become commonplace, Northern zeal opposing and obstructing any placatory candidate or shift in dogma.
- First appearance of [[Redemptionism]], a school of thought within Orthodoxy, in southern Lavion.
- The First Parliament, convened so proudly at Santra in 201 AR, meets ever less often as the generations pass; within a few generations of the Charter of 367 it has lapsed into memory and little else, kept alive in name only by the Church that still gathers in the same city.
- No singular polity can rise to claim the widespread control the Remeans or the Theocracy once held; warlordism and proto-feudalist structures predominate around the Circle Sea, even in the nascent Commonwealth, which has yet to promulgate the "land peace" that will end feuding in that realm.
- 448 AR: persistent raiding -- internal, and from emergent Lavienese warlords -- spurs first principalities to ally for coordinated defence, levy quotas, and finance pooled through merchant leagues; for the first time cooperation rests on principle rather than personal entourage. The alliance does not outlive the raiding that prompted it, and the principalities drift back to their own quarrels once the threat recedes.
- Balboa's "Five Generations of Expansion" make the principality a power in its own right in the Dark Ages, but come to a close with devastating losses against Gaviatolis, a local rival state on the peninsula; the subsequent turn inward leads to the formation of the fabled Balboan mercenary companies that will continue to fill the city's coffers for the following centuries.
- "The Hundred Years' Vacancy" (late 9th to late 10th century) -- the Interregnum, in ecclesiastical usage -- leaves the seat of the Hierophant entirely vacant; parish and guild routines endure, while Concordian practice stabilises the west and feels justified in not following a single voice. Against the spectacle of a vacant Hierophancy, the Synod of Santra's own unbroken, conciliar continuity becomes something closer to a political argument: a body that plainly never needed a crowned head to keep functioning.
- Settlement of the [[Varsa]] (998 AR): Genovrast "the Great" [[de Lavion]] forges the kingdom that will bear his name by the time he is 28 years old, and brokers the modern, conciliatory Third Hierophancy to have a church that can underwrite his rule; crusading bands are finally tamed and consolidated into the budgeted Church Militant. The same year closes the Hundred Years' Vacancy at Aramoor, and the coincidence is not lost on the Commonwealth's own princes: a century of meeting only at Synod, and the sudden sight of a unified crown at their border, together turn a long habit of talking into the first stirrings of a revived secular Parliament.
## The Age of Corsairs (10th to 13th century AR)
 
- Beyond the Suurian Gate, expeditions in the 10th century falter, then bloom with seamanship after the discovery of viable routes and deep-sea keels, fuelling three centuries of blue-water daring; even inside the Circle Sea, corsairs, convoys, and convoy-hunters define war and profit.
- The first breath-arms appear from noble duelling culture rather than military need: organ-builders already turning pliant-sealed brass pressure vessels for their instruments are commissioned to make a hand-cranked air-piece fit for affairs of honour -- a slow, finely bored arm, minutes to charge, that quietly founds the whole later air-gun tradition.
- Aetheric cannons (sometimes named "arcannons") enter service in the 12th century and transform ship and wall; torsion pistols and crossbows dominate alleys and rigging; alchemical grenades flourish. Cheap, unreliable military airguns spread as a poor man's alternative to arcannon and crossbow -- a coarse mass adaptation of the noble breath-piece rather than a new invention.
- The Commonwealth's sexagesimal [[Guilder]] becomes the de-facto standard of commerce -- its base-sixty reckoning adopted independently of Reme through the older diffusion of ideas and preserved in lands already Lurist before the Long War, where the crusaders' dozenal never took hold; Balboa on Ertra and [[Beniotti]] on the [[Keystone Isles]] rise as entrepôts even as [[Kingdom of Lavion|Lavion]] moves to consolidate.
- War of the Three Kings (1117-1121) between Lavion, [[Cendrelac]], and Balboa; Lavion brings Balboa to terms, granting it its privileged "Crown Vassal" status, then handily beats Cendrelac.
- Over the Age of Corsairs, pneumatics climb from novelty to civic backbone: what begins as organ wind-chests and workshop curiosities becomes first genuine tool -- pumps, bellows, and small machines driven off charged vessels -- and then, as pliant-sealed brass and drawn tube mature, piped municipal supply. By the early 13th century a mature Concordian city runs on graded pneumatic mains -- high pressure for forge and foundry, medium for craft workshops and public buildings, low for hearth-bellows, small tools, and tube-mail -- fed from early, crude open-tube thermoacoustic charging stations whose heat vaults warm whole districts, with portable brass-bottle accumulators rechargeable at public points.
- Formal close in the 1250s: King Lurvric centralises the maritime power of Lavion, the feudal monarchy of the southern Western Gyre now reaching all the way to Balboa; within the Circle Sea it dominates by leveraging Balboan expertise, while the confederated principalities of the Commonwealth in Stranu and the Svenish hold the waters beyond the Gate.
## The Aetheric Age (13th to 15th century AR)
 
- Aetheric siege doctrine matures; the arcannon becomes doctrinal; academy science reframes alchemy as aetheric engineering under the triad of Structure, Resonance, and Materiality.
- c. 1225: a scholar at the Grand Academy of the Broken Isles demonstrates the compression-expansion work cycle -- mechanical work and a cold outlet drawn from compressed air expanded through a pliant-membrane engine -- a boiler-less counterpart to the steam the Circle Sea disdains, slotting straight into the existing pneumatic mains and marking the true inflection from crude open-tube [[thermoacoustics]] toward disciplined [[Aetheric Engineering]].
- c. 1240: the Law of Harmonic Conversion is set down -- heat may always be turned into organised resonant or pneumatic motion, but never wholly, its yield bounded by the ratio of temperatures at a device's hot and cold ends -- the limit that will govern every choir engine after.
- Commonwealth finance and Lavienese prestige coinage knit a pan-Circulean economy.
- Mid-14th century: self-harmonising resonance design is formalised. Thermoacoustic "choir engines" become the heartbeat of industry.
- Late 14th to early 15th century: engines shrink to fit launches and riverboats; pontoon airships ("[[aeronaves]]") link major ports, the logic of the air following that of the sea; public mains regularise urban power.
- 1450s: valve-code beacons and pressure clocks point toward fluidic logic; prosthetic pneumatics enter workshops and wards.
- Late 15th century: the aetheric furnace, which had won [[alumen]] only in costly ounces since the 14th century, at last yields it at usable cost, a light metal that at last lightens the compressed-air bottle enough to carry real power to a soldier on foot, completing the breath-arm's long passage from duelling curiosity to infantry weapon.
## The Imperial Age (15th to 16th century AR)
 
- Lavion's sober modernity extends from its civic attitudes into its officer corps and drill; successive sovereigns professionalise the soldiery.
- Roburic II "Iron-Crown" (r. 1498-1524) finally weaponises that machine: protectorates on the Eastern Gyre littoral and the Southlands along the [[Swicca]], a self-crowned Emperor, and a rupture with Lurism that creates a loyal local Patriarchate and forces the Hierophant to flee into exile (never termed such) on the Keystone Isles.
- [[Aethermaton|Aethermata]] -- pneumatic constructs with fluidic logic for a brain, a choir engine for a heart, and braided pliants for tendons -- enter docks, yards, and policing; coffers fatten even as guilds and mutuals fight for deadman plates and safety slates.
- A breakdown in faith following the rupture at the heart of Orthodoxy leads to squalor and dystopian circumstances in many cities profiting from hitherto unheard-of growth from aetheric industry.
- The Undeclared War (1541-1561): two decades before any crown admits it, the Lavienese Companies Royal and the Commonwealth's United Chartered Companies fight a real war under company colours in the Rim of Fire, each empire disclaiming its own officers until an entire generation has already learned the trade its sovereigns have yet to declare.
- The Opening of the Great War: Lavion's formal declaration in 1561 opens with a single ruinous season, a belt of eastern Commonwealth princedoms falling before winter halts the advance more than any resistance does; out of that near-collapse the Commonwealth forges the rigged-soldier-and-brassback-wingman lance and the rotostat gunship, and by 1567 what began as a patchwork of princely levies has become, in all but charter, a standing army that holds the line where it once broke.
- The Balboan Question Answered: Vel Kiriya's push toward the Ertran coast exposes at last how much of Lavion's own navy the Crown Vassal's fleet and officers had always constituted; the Principe draws Balboan hulls home to guard the Rush, then recalls every company under foreign contract, declaring Balboa's arms bent only to its own defence -- and within a season the eastern war loses the naval reach that had let it be fought at that distance from home at all.
- The Desolation of Santra (1572): seeking to end the war with a single stroke against the Commonwealth's parliamentary and synodal seat, Lavion's suppressed lineage of natural philosophers unleashes a weapon beyond horror, untethering all six elements over the city at once; Santra burns, freezes, rots, and breaks apart together in an instant that kills the greater part of the Guard of the Common Weal and leaves a permanent aetheric wound the world has not been able to close. What Lavion meant to sever a head instead gives the Commonwealth its first undivided cause, "For Santra," and draws Vel Kiriya back into the war on righteous terms.
- The Peace of Balboa (1574): signed in the one city the war left with clean hands, the treaty restores every princedom lost in 1561 and raises Paltos, together with the Duchy of Rasselune, into a new Commonwealth Principality on Lavion's own soil; Vel Kiriya's satrapy administration of Lu-Pto and the Eastern Gyre littoral behind the Lupine passes is confirmed, Varngard and Karsova pass to independence, and Balboa's long-ambiguous Crown Vassal fealty is ended. At home, the Compact of Virell gives the Commons the right to sponsor its own legislation and binds war to the joint consent of Peerage and Commons alike; that, and Edramar II's forced abdication in favor of a lesser line of the family that quickly takes the name "Varsenne", close the Imperial Age that opened under Roburic II's crown and end the rule of Genovrast's dynasty.
