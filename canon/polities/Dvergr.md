# Dvergr

Though considerably more insular than other civilisations that crowd the Circle Sea, the Dvergr (singular: Dvergr) that inhabit the highlands and mesa of their homeland of Sarm do have a presence outside their ancestral holdings. Their trading posts dot the ports and markets of Lavion, An-Suur and as far south as ice-bound Svenu and Varngard beyond the Wolves' Teeth Mountains. True settlements -- called Holdfasts or Halls -- are rare outside Sarm, but exist intermittently throughout the Circulean, each a polity onto itself, answerable to no crown or council beyond its own Vizierate. No two Holds need agree on how deeply to entangle themselves with the surface world, and none do: a wealthy Hold sitting on a rich vein of ore may run its own fleet of factors and treat with princes as an equal, while its nearest neighbour, a bare day's tunnel away, may have gone a century without a single Dvergr crossing into the light on any errand but the digging of it.

Any compound that houses a larger group of Dvergr than a handful of traders is invariably guarded by a highly drilled detachment of Dvergr falangi, or "spearmen," whose tightly arrayed ranks present any opponent with an almost impenetrable wall of shields bristling with triangular points. Though their tactics are not known to be innovative, their efficiency and effectiveness is undebatable and especially vicious in tunnel warfare, where flanking is impossible. It is conceivable their word first gave the Circulean the concept of close-order spear formation, mastered in later centuries by the ancient Remeans.

But what the world sees as "dwarves" -- a people of squat, bearded, stoic males -- is a half-truth. In reality, Dvergr civilization is caste-bound, bears strong traces of eusociality, and is almost more akin to elementals than other mortals, since Dvergr are geode-born and feel a strong connection to the Noble Element of Stone, whom they deem their progenitor and name Grandmother Lode.

## Physiology

The Dvergr are shaped by subterranean darkness, stone, and the three-dimensionality of caverns. Their bodies skew stout and tendon-thick, with big dark-adapted eyes that hold a pistoned iris, feet that grip like a second pair of hands, and a pelt that runs from crown to heel. Facial hair that in a human would be extravagant ornament is an organ of extreme importance to a Dvergr: moustache, brow, and cheek carry dense vibrissae that read air the way fingers read embossed surfaces, letting a Dvergr feel pressure shifts, distant footfalls, and the faint wind of a breathing shaft.

They speak with the tongue, the "low speech" of everyday work, but move through the dark by "stone speech," a lattice of soft ultrasonic clicks providing a sonar sense so habitual that even their lullabies are such pulse-rhythms; this also gives rise to their "wall song," which uses the same clicks for subtle, far-reaching communicative purposes, one without an outsider audience.

No outsider has ever seen a female Dvergr, but this is not for some reason of taboo. It is so because Dvergr, as a people, do not procreate in the way that the many facets of humankind and indeed the Kin of the Alvai do. Neither are Dvergr male at all -- they merely express features that in other peoples are read as such. In fact, their expertise in mining and stonecraft is not, as often portrayed, based in a greedy hunger for gems and fine metals, but instead forms the basis of their continuance: for it is a fact not discussed nor disclosed to outsiders that Dvergr are born from geodes.

## Youth

In the depths, Dvergr scout seams for geodes to bring into the creche. There, each geode is primed by seedwrights with a mineral serum that encodes the Hold's lineage, and a seedstone, shaved from the calcified plate of a vizier, is set into the geode's heart. Under steady attention the lattice reorders around serum and seedstone until the shell parts and a pup slides free. A Dvergr's lineage owes nothing to dual parentage as in humans or Alvai; it is the quiet stamp of a vizier's seedstone in appearance and disposition, scrambled into unique individuality by the randomness of the natural geode.

The reason surface folk hold on to the superstition that all mountains are home to dwarves is that in some cases, geodes may quicken on their own. These wildborn seamlings are rare and precious to the Dvergr, the stuff of sagas. A Hold that finds one will bring it home and raise the child like any other, but the sign of the wild will follow them for life.

Grandmother Lode's gift is not given freely even in stone the Dvergr never touch: it is said, and every seedwright believes it, that the deepest and fairest geodes are hidden by her own hand from careless eyes, kept for those who have earned the finding of them. The Dvergr hold a doubled reverence for this hiding, since what it conceals is not mere wealth but the very stuff of the next generation.

## Adult Age

Licheners are caretakers of the milk-stone, the common feedstock for young and old alike. These pale felts of lichen come in many separate grades of "brew": ones for the pups to grow, ones for fine motor and tunnel work, for plate and tendon, for health, for relaxation and enjoyment. Mixed with specific broths, mash, and mineral licks, these brews guide the generations, for in Dvergr society it is not his clothes that maketh man, but his diet. By late youth -- measured in some decades by human reckoning -- the body forks by habit and diet to either remain compact and tireless, or to put on tendon and plate faster: younglings diverging into Workers or Warriors, depending on the Hold's need and circumstance. The transition is not heroic, merely practical, and when need calls, the change may be induced even late in life.

Growth never quite stops. Workers will eventually outgrow the mining tunnels before their second century, and thence move naturally into the caste of Porters, the load-bearing answer to stairs in darkness that no beast can climb, while Warriors age into Guardians: knuckle-walking hunchbacks laden with plates, strong enough to shoulder entire seams during quakes, hurl boulders from the back ranks as artillery, or anchor shield-walls. These, when seen outside the Hold, are what has given rise to folktales of stone trolls in the deep mountains. For both these plated protectors and the Warriors from which they spring, plate-carving is both an expression of the self and a medical art for pain relief; many will turn to Caretakers, a broad calling that spans both the Licheners tending the milk-stone and the bonewrights who file down calcified ridges, cap errant spikes, and keep nerve pinch at bay.

## Old Age and the Vizierate

Calcification comes for every Dvergr in the end, whatever caste they diverged into in adulthood. In late life -- the third or fourth century, by those who live so long -- metabolism slows, plates lay down like masonry, whiskers stiffen, and the voice drops to a cellar note a human would more feel than hear. Bone thickens, joints seize by slow degrees, and posture drops further until even walking becomes a procession, attended by slings, rails, and laughing apprentices, or by palanquin for the longest journeys. It is not appointment that makes a Vizier, but survival: those who resist the pull of Grandmother Lode the longest, whose inner chord still thrums after their agemates have gone still, are the Viziers by simple fact of having outlasted the calcification that claims everyone else, and are honored for that endurance rather than chosen for any office. In a Hold, its viziers arbitrate shifts and feuds, keep law and memory, and guide the younger generations, day by day less mobile, mind still sharp, until at last the thrum of their inner chord ceases to be heard and they become a funerary stele of their own making. It is a vizier's own calcified plate, shaved into a seedstone, that carries a Hold's lineage into the next geode, so that the ones who held out longest against becoming stone are the very stuff from which the Hold's future is grown. A Dvergr mausoleum is thus not a yard of graves but a hall of elders finally at rest, an archive of faces, ridges, and lineage. The result is a people whose birth, growth, and memorial are one continuous stonework: from seam to shelf to shift to stele, every Dvergr is set in the world by Stone and returns to it, adding one more pillar to the architecture of the Hold.

## The Dvergr Beard: Sensorium of Stone

Among the Dvergr, the beard is not ornamental, nor merely a marker of age or dignity. It is a primary sensory organ, a bundle of hyper-sensitive whiskers capable of detecting minute vibrations, shifts in pressure, and even subsonic resonance.

These whiskers are true vibrissae, hair set in thick follicles packed with nerves, arrayed at brow, moustache, cheek, and jaw. Not every hair on a Dvergr's face is a whisker, but the learned can identify the longer, thicker hairs from the ordinary ones. With age and plate growth the whiskers stiffen and lengthen, shifting sensitivity downward toward the low rumbles that Viziers read best. Loss is not merely cosmetic, but a sensory maiming on par with blinding or cutting the ear: the world goes dull, corridors lose their edges, and the hum of stone speech turns to mush.

Given the significance of these beards and the importance of keeping the whiskers in good care, combs are tools of calibration more than vanity, different cuts with gauges for a specific fan angle and tooth pitch for a given whisker bundle. Beard oils and waxes are as important. Beads, sleeves, and split-rings act as quick-change fittings: color tells caste and current duty at a glance; weight and placement tune a whisker's resonance the way a luthier weights a string.

In war, Dvergr tuck their beards behind specialized helmet plates and grilles, allowing sensation while protecting the sensitive filaments in combat. These protections have a limit, as whiskers do grow back, unlike eyes or ears, so the Dvergr armory makes some allowances in accounting for beard protection, but none that would go beyond the pale.

## Holdfasts and the Question of Sarm

Sarm is the Dvergr homeland, or should we say, the surface of it. In all their centuries of existing alongside the civilisations of Humankind and Alvai kin in the Circulean, the Dvergr of Sarm have opted to stay aloof. Well-known is their "capital" of Edomis, but even this is really nothing more than the oldest and most prestigious of their Holdfasts, whose viziers most other Holds align with by sheer weight of tradition rather than any authority Edomis could compel. No Hold answers to another by law, and a Hold's standing in the wider Dvergr world is a matter of age, endurance, and the accumulated memory of its Vizierate, not of rank.

That independence extends to how, and whether, a Hold deals with the surface at all. Some Holds sit on rich veins of copper and tin, tracing a trade older than reliable memory in the Circulean basin, or on the rarer prize of true ferrite ore, and lean hard into that wealth, maintaining enclaves in major cities and courting the favor of princes who would otherwise have no reason to notice a mountain. Others hold the opposite conviction entirely: that metal, being Stone's own substance already claimed and altered by Fire through their shared Tension, is a debasement of Grandmother Lode's gift when shaped into everyday tools, and such Holds refuse to trade their finds at all, building instead on a strong tradition of ceramics that keeps the work within Stone's own nature. Between these poles sit Holds that mine and trade freely, holding no special reverence for the metal they find and no quarrel with those who do, treating ore as simply one more thing the depths provide. No single Dvergr doctrine settles which reading is correct, and a Hold's neighbours may just as easily call its metal-taboo as admirably devout as they might call it parochial and impractical, depending on which Hold is doing the judging.

Many greedy rulers have considered going to war with a neighbouring Hold over its riches. Few have found the venture worth its cost. Dvergr warfare is built on the pike and shot, a doctrine the Dvergr are held to have originated long before it became the reckoning arm of any surface army, and their pike lines are said to be unbreakable. Moreover, the peculiar way in which Dvergr employ stone trolls as skirmishers makes engaging a major force in the field an exercise in futility: having to charge an unmoving pikewall under a shower of rocks and shrapnel thrown not by imprecise petraries such as onagers, trebuchets, or catapults, but by the cunning arms of these giant brutes, breaks the morale of even the most seasoned attacker, more so once they realize that unlike artillery crews, these trolls will happily enter melee once out of things to throw, and their half-stone skin is harder than the best cuirass. In the field, the trolls -- really veterans of the Warrior caste who have transitioned into Guardians as their growth began to calcify -- are most often led by officers drawn from the Warriors, who are still quick and can more readily assess a situation to make the right strategic decisions, where a Guardian would simply focus on the most obvious task: to smash the opposition, by rock and tree and horn and fist.

Beyond Sarm, some peaks of the Wolves' Teeth Mountains guard the entrance to a major Hold, trading copper and tin, silver and gold, and ferrite ore rare across the whole of the Circulean lands. Only one or two such Holds are believed to exist in that range, and their presence there, in country the Moonkin held long before the Scattering and the svartalfr still call home, is a history yet to be fully told.

## The Unrooted: Dvergr Beyond the Holdfast

Though Dvergr society is defined by cohesion, not all Dvergr remain embedded within the structure of their birth-vein. Throughout the history of Arlos, a small but consistent trickle of "Unrooted" Dvergr has found its way into the surface world, not as envoys or warrior detachments, but as individuals.

To humans, they appear little different from any other Dvergr, albeit perhaps more curious, more willing to speak, more at ease with the uncertain rhythms of the wide world. While the Dvergr language has no true word for "exile" or "wanderer," only circumlocutions that express detachment from home and harmony, and the idea that one can exist meaningfully on one's own in the outside world is deeply unsettling to the caste-bound cohorts, there exist several reasons a Dvergr might become solitary.

Some Workers, during missions abroad or long service at surface compounds, develop a personal dissonance, a yearning, inexplicable and unresolvable, that drives them to remain behind after their rotation ends. They often take up solitary trades or dabble in dangerous undertakings, and though they are rarely loquacious, they are often intensely perceptive.

In rare instances, a Dvergr may become cut off from their Holdfast through catastrophe, interference, or war. These individuals often suffer a deep spiritual vertigo, like a deafening silence. Some adapt, becoming cautious sages or mad tinkerers. Others never recover, and walk the world like stones cracked from the quarry, purposeless but still enduring. If there is talk of a savage troll in the high country, it will be one such maddened soul.

There is reason to believe that some Unrooted are not truly unrooted, but rather deep plants placed deliberately within human societies to observe or influence. If so, they maintain irregular contact with their home Halls if at all, and likely report to their viziers through means unknown.

## Open Threads

The precise relationship between the Wolves' Teeth Dvergr Hold or Holds and the Moonkin who held that range before the Scattering, and the svartalfr who remain there, is undecided: neighbours, rivals, something closer, or simply mutual strangers sharing a mountain by depth rather than by acquaintance.

Whether a Hold's choice to embrace or refuse the metal trade correlates with anything beyond its own Vizierate's reading of Grandmother Lode's nature, or is purely a matter of accumulated custom, is unresolved.

Whether a Worker or Porter line can produce a Vizier as readily as a Warrior or Guardian line, or whether caste bears on which Dvergr tend to outlast the calcification that claims everyone eventually, has not been settled.
