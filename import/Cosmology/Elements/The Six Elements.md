Arlos is defined by the interplay of its six elements: Fire, Air, Water, Ice, Stone (also called Ore), and Soil (also Loam, Wood, or Life). Each element characterizes a different inherent nature and certain qualities, and their interplay defines the material reality of the world.

## The Elemental Relations

No element exists without interaction with the other five. Their relations form a web of sympathetic and antagonistic relations, which can be influenced by aetheric modulation. Each element is thus a pole for five different relations: two with the elements deemed its neighbours, two with elements one step removed, and one with its polar opposite.

The relation between elements deemed "adjacent" in the sixfold circle is termed the "Strong" relation, for it is usually mutually reinforcing, a covalent bond that resists uncoupling. In addition, each element's three relations to non-neighbouring elements are termed "Weak": two are termed Abeyant, and one is Antagonistic. Unlike the stability of Adjacency, the Weak relations are characterized by the tension introduced by the interplay of the different natures of the elements: While Abeyant relations are usually in equilibrium, Antagonistic relations are in constant flux. Modern aetheric scholarship uses defined, commonly accepted terms for all elemental relations, drawing on older alchemical terms.

### The Strong Relations
Adjacent elements have long proved to be extremely resistant to influences; their natures are linked, and so it is exceedingly hard to introduce change (though they can be reinforced). For that reason they are best understood as the aspects forming the backbone of reality in a sixfold circle, and in the vocabulary of earlier alchemists, these relations are expressed simply as what action the elements most readily do.

- **Fire <> Air**: Rarefaction (Strong/Adjacent)
	Fire purifies, Air disperses
- **Air <> Water**: Flux (Strong/Adjacent)
	Air moves, Water flows
- **Water <> Ice**: Formation (Strong/Adjacent)
	Water shapes, Ice retains
- **Ice <> Stone**: Fortification (Strong/Adjacent)
	Ice hardens, Stone resists
- **Stone <> Soil**: Foundation (Strong/Adjacent)
	Stone anchors, Soil accumulates
- **Soil <> Fire**: Growth (Strong/Adjacent)
	Soil nurtures, Fire transforms

### The Weak Relations
Of the Weak relations, the ones deemed Abeyant are the stable ones, which in nature usually hold in equilibrium. However, the Abeyant relations will undergo change more readily than the Strong relations. In older alchemical parlance, Abeyant relations are expressed as what the element wishes to do, against the constraints placed on it by its opposite.

- **Fire <> Water**: Volatility (Weak/Abeyant)
	Fire wishes to excite, Water wishes to quench
- **Fire <> Stone**: Tension (Weak/Abeyant; also termed Magnetism)
	Fire wishes to act, Stone wishes to endure
- **Ice <> Soil**: Reactivity (Weak/Abeyant)
	Ice wishes to inhibit, Soil wishes to activate
- **Air <> Ice**: Fulminance (Weak/Abeyant)
	Air wishes to release, Ice wishes to retain ^eb262b
- **Air <> Soil**: Attraction (Weak/Abeyant; also termed Gravity)
	Air wishes to disperse, Soil wishes to clump
- **Water <> Stone**: Cohesion (Weak/Abeyant)
	Water wishes to diffuse, Stone wishes to structure

In contrast, the Antagonistic relations oscillate much more, and need but the slightest nudge to tip towards either pole. In alchemical terms, they are expressed as the elements' will -- what it yearns to do to abide its very nature. These relations are also the most readily experienced, as they form the basis of most everyday weather phenomena.

- **Water <> Soil**: Saturation (Weak/Antagonistic)
	Water will dilute, Soil will concentrate
- **Fire <> Ice**: Temperature (Weak/Antagonistic)
	Fire will melt, Ice will freeze
- **Air <> Stone**: Motility (Weak/Antagonistic)
	Air will move, Stone will repose

These elemental relations are foundational to all physical interactions in the absence of atoms or molecules.

## Types of Elementals

### Sprites
Sprites are the lowest order of elementals: animal-intellect manifestations, they are not the pure expression of a single element as their larger kin in the courts of the Noble Six. Rather, they are drawn out (or manifest) wherever the relations of the elements are in flux. There are an untold number of them: Some are no more than an ephemeral expression of potential that pass with the moment, some are steady companions, fed and loved like household pets or shooed away like persistent pests. Too many forms exist to name them all, but many are found often enough to have been named.

**Emberlings** rest on combustibles like cats on a warm mattress, **Sizzlewicks** splash in pans of fat and guzzle the tallow of candles. **Fluecombs** dance with smoke in the draft and pull guttering flames tall, while **Windshears** throw up dirt and dust as they race oncoming storms and tangle the laundry. **Dewlasses** and **Furrow-Folk** are a farmer's best friends when given room and respect, but can turn sound soil to soggy soup if slighted. **Cracklers** creep along iron like a thin blue edge, dancing on ships' masts in pale light and crackling in hair and wool, and join with **Raindancers** pitter-pattering in the gutters when a thunderstorm comes down. **Rimekin** draw frost-lace on windows and metals and relent with a sigh when given a little warmth, but **Snapsingers** delight in needling exposed skin in the winter, or drawing hairline fractures in hot ware that cools too suddenly. **Cobolts** tease miners with false promises of rich veins, their hungry licks leaving patina on bronze and iron. A happy **Slumper** in the foundations can make the difference between a wall that stands even in the worst of times and a disaster waiting to happen.

Folk traditions and fairytales are full of wise words about how to appease, assuage, coax or chide sprites in everyday life, and examples of what failure to respect them can result in. While modern societies turn to aetheric modulation as the "properly scientific" way to enact change in the elements, the older traditions -- of respect, of offerings, of the right words spoken at the right time -- persist particularly in rural areas, despite the stigma Lurism applies to the practice of invoking the elements.

### **The Courts**
#### The Flamelords & -ladies of Halefire Hall
Flamelords and -ladies, said to represent Phlogistophor's volcanic court, are often beings of radiant flames or scorching liquid lava, though some follow their "king's" example and manifest as sculpted, perfected humans of blueish black and fiery orange hues, like black-figure pottery brought to life, fire shining from their eyes and mouth.

They are notoriously volatile, their tempers rising and falling as swiftly as sparks in the wind. At one moment they may roar with the destructive rage of an erupting mountain, and at the next bask in self-admiration, strutting like monarchs of flame. Their vanity is legendary: they delight in offerings of precious metals, gemstones, or even poetry and song praising their beauty. To cross them, however, is to invite ruin, for their wrath is indiscriminate and all-consuming.

Yet their pride also makes them appeaseable. Those who flatter, honor, or humor them often escape unscathed, sometimes even rewarded with embers of fortune or fire that burns without consuming. Scholars and priests alike note that Flamelords crave reverence as much as they crave fuel, and that the clever may bend their vanity into a shield against their volatility. Dangerous though they are, they are not beyond the reach of diplomacy -- provided one never forgets that their favor is as fleeting as a flame in the wind.
#### Windwalkers, Riders of the Wild Hunt  
Windwalkers embody the restless and mercurial spirit of the air. Tall, lean, and translucent, their bodies flicker as though drawn in strokes of cloud and lightning. They never seem still: hair streaming backward as though in a gale, clothing fluttering even in still chambers. Their laughter is sharp as whistling winds, and their anger rolls like thunder.

They are the heralds of storms and companions to **Morwynt** on her never-ending Wild Hunt. Windwalkers are often encountered at the edges of mountains, among spires, in stormfronts, or following great migrations. They are tricksters, fleet and free, with a keen intellect and a cruel streak when crossed. Hunters swear that a Windwalker's whistle can drive prey into ambush or panic cavalry horses. Sailors say that to be favored by one is to find endless tailwinds; to be cursed is to tack forever against the gale.
#### Tidestewards, Emissaries of the Senate of the Sea  
Tidestewards are solemn, majestic figures of flowing form. Their limbs trail into foaming surf or strands of kelp; their eyes gleam like polished shells. They speak slowly, but with resonance, as if all the weight of the tides presses behind their words.

Sailors and coastal folk consider them emissaries of **Maregina’s Senate**, enforcers of the tidal covenants between land and sea that are so much older than mankind. They dwell in grottoes, tidepools, or offshore reefs, emerging where water is deepest or currents converge. They are patrons of natural harbors, of the balance between giving and taking from the sea, and invoked in many fisherfolk oaths. To the Tidestewards, the ocean is both court and temple, and they despise waste or arrogance. A captain who sacrifices fairly and honors the sea may see their ship borne by favoring currents; one who cheats the sea may find their keel fouled forever in kelp and barnacles.
#### Rimeswains of the Heartless Harem  
Rimeswains are statuesque and coldly beautiful, their skin white as hoarfrost, lips blue as glaciers, eyes crystalline with a faint inner glow. They adorn themselves with veils of snowflakes or jewels of frozen dew. Unlike the warmth and life of the Tidestewards, they are seduction without compassion, company without comfort.

Legends call them the paramours of **Anapagonia**, forever alluring but never faithful, always drawing mortals into the dance of winter and leaving only frozen corpses in their wake. They move with grace like drifting snow, their voices sweet and brittle. In settlements where winter rules long, people whisper of Rimeswains walking among them, coaxing lovers away from hearths into the frost. To scholars, they are a paradox: elementals of preservation that also bring death, beings that embody the unyielding cruelty of ice.
#### Oreads of the Crystal Hearth
Oreads are come in as many shapes and shades as there are gemstones in rock. Their movements are slow but deliberate, and their voices reverberate as though spoken through caverns, or ring like bells. They are custodians, keepers of hidden wealth and the secrets of the deep underground.

Outwardly, they are described as the wardens of **Piatros’ crystal hearth**, nurturing gems, sowing ores, and ensuring the veins of the world run deep. To petition an Oread is to risk binding yourself to oaths as heavy as stone, but to befriend one is to hold access to hidden veins of treasure and enduring strength.

It is said that the Dvergr of Sarm revere the Oreads as distant forebears.
#### Wildwardens of the Worldroot  
Wildwardens embody the fecundity and patience of soil. Broad-shouldered, rooted in stance, their skin is bark-like or loamy, their hair matted with moss or vines. Their movements -- when they do move, for many are sedentary -- are deliberate, never hurried, and their speech comes in deep, rolling tones like earth shifting beneath plow or hoof.

They are regarded as tenders of the Worldroot that connects all life to the earth. Where they dwell, forests grow thick, fields yield abundantly, and even weeds thrive with ferocity. Yet they are not gentle gardeners: they despise exploitation without balance. A farmer who tills with care and respects the seasons may find his crops blessed beyond measure, but one who exhausts the soil may wake to find their fields choked by thorns overnight.

Wildwardens are often venerated in rustic shrines, and there are accounts of them adopting entire villages under their protection, demanding festivals of fertility and labor in return. They remind mortals that soil is not just ground to be used, but a living, breathing fundament that renews or consumes, and of all the courts the Worldroot is the one that rural clergy must grudgingly tolerate.
