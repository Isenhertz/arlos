## Situation and Orientation

Lu-Pto forms the furthest eastern shoreline of the [[Circle Sea|Circulean]]. It lies north of the Lonely Plains of [[Lu-An]] and south of the Merhal floodplains of [[Riv-î-Tep]]. The coast is a narrow ribbon of dry woodland steppe, pressed between the sea and an interior that sags into salt basins before lifting again toward the basalt isles that anchor the Sifting Fields. The powers and people of the Circulean treat Lu-Pto as the last familiar margin of the Circle Sea before the world thins into wind and rumor.

Along this coast, black iron sands lie heavy in the swash -- fed by the shield volcanoes of Ertra into the Eastern Gyre, then sifted and laid down along the shores of Lu-An and Lu-Pto. With the Wolves' Teeth and much of the Circulean interior sparse in iron ore, these strandlines have become the premier source of iron; they account for most of Lu-Pto's exported wealth and for most outsiders' interest in so inhospitable a shore.

The land arranges itself in belts running roughly parallel to the coast, each with its own logic of travel and survival. The littoral is a thin band of thorn and dry woodland on poor soils. Small headland ports cling to sea caves and notches in the cliffs, taking fish and salt and sending out reed goods, glazes, and news. Where saline depressions crowd the shore, the liveable strip narrows to a thread.

Inland the ground breaks into saline depressions and evaporite flats, chotts that fill shallowly with winter rain and groundwater to leave behind glare and crust, and supratidal sabkhas near the coast, dry on the skin and treacherous beneath. The hardy vegetation here doesn't mind salt or drought -- tamarisk and saltbush hedges, glasswort mats, reeds and rushes. The hard, alkaline temperament of these soils has spared them wide exploitation and preserved long corridors of wilderness.

Faults and old flows bring water to the surface in sparse but dependable oases for what little precarious agriculture can be done wherever salinity can be managed. Communities wall their cisterns, police water strictly, and travel by night. The region's famous basalt ridges rise like islands from these flats, breaking wind and offering the only honest foundations for heavy work. Communities hug the largest of these black outcroppings, harvesting dew and nursing gardens in slivers of soil, while wayhouses and shrines perch on more lonely heights.

Offshore, the Eastern Gyre runs fast and clean northward in its counter-clockwise path along the coast, but the inshore counters are choppy, full of reef tongues under the water and silica-laden squalls above it. Winter brings fog and steep swell; summer lifts furnace winds along the cliffs. Airship travel is possible but never easy, and the further inland a journey ventures, the more common are disturbances, both aetheric and atmospheric ones; either can force landings exactly where landing is least advised.

### The Sifted Fields

Beyond the oases begins the feared desert of fines where some manner of elemental imbalance makes sand, silt, and salt de-pack and flow like a slurry. Folk vocabulary divides the Fields by behaviour. The Softs are powder dunes on the very edge of the Fields that liquefy under specific conditions but are otherwise stable enough to traverse with spread shoes and lattice sleds. The Wakes form as crusted ripple pans that remain reliable only while conditions holds steady, liable to collapse. The Hush are treacherous flats that come undone if agitated; here one must communicate by hand signs and soften every footfall. Finally, the Sift itself is the vast expanse of bubbling, shifting fines, too stirred up to ever come to a rest. Whosoever sets foot here vanishes noiselessly into the warm powdery sands, without so much as a splash.

## Ecology

The coastal strip hosts drought-hardy shrubs and thorn trees, small antelope analogues, foxes, and migratory gryphoids that surf the cliff winds. Chotts and sabkhas ring with reeds and rushes when wet, then pull back to lichens and crusts when dry; seasonal brine blooms bring rich pulses of life. Oases manage palms, gourds, and grains in patient rotations with sacrificial fallows to salinity. On the Fields, lichened basalt and fog nets shelter hardy shrubs in wind shadows; movement by night spares water and keeps Hush from noticing.

## History

In the high days of the Sorcerer-Kings, Lu-Pto was subdued outright. Roads and resonant pylons pinned basins, terraces held water where none should have been, and the coast paid tribute to their northern masters. Their lesser successors quarreled over scraps and let the maintenance lapse. When Reme came later, the works of the First-and-Only were already broken ruins, howling with the winds and less savoury things.

The Remeans stitched the littoral into a workable polity of pilotage, standardized dues, shrines, and storehouses, but never pushed inland past the chott rims. With the Fall of Reme, the coastal league fractured, though shrine-law and wayhouse custom persisted where it served consensus.

## Peoples and Polities

Lu-Pto is not empty. It is fractal, seasonal, and stubborn. Headland polities balance on rock and the waterline. Chott-rim towns harvest salt and reeds for caravans that leave when the basins allow. Oasis and basalt communities live by dewdrops and an etiquette of inviolate hospitality. The only constant institutions are the shrines, the wayhouses, and the guilds of Waykeepers who time Sift crossings, mark safe spots, and prize their knowledge like gold. Every settlement tries to tax them; none controls them for long. Headland councils guard iron-sand strandlines like granaries, issuing seasonal leases to dredgers and winnowers and enforcing closed days when storms must rebuild the bars.

Travel here succeeds by timing, not force. Inland caravans stage on the basalt islands and step off on the bell the waykeepers name. Loads here are spread wide, like they are in the south when ice must be traversed -- paddle shoes and sailed sleds predominate over animal labor. Chotts are skirted on the high side, and every crust is assumed to be lying. Given the temperatures, night marches are preferred whenever the waykeepers deem them possible.

### Economy and Exchanges

Foremost among exports are the black iron sands -- graded on the beach, bagged on the headlands, and shipped to foundries across the Circle Sea. Control of a productive strand, bar, or seasonal lease can bankroll a headland league or topple a rival, and foreign factors court councils for concessions with a fervor that borders on worship. Smelting is often done abroad to spare local water and fuel, though small bloom hearths operate where basalt gives good draft.

After iron sands come salt in many grades, natron and other evaporites, cured fish, reed cordage and mats, resilient pottery glazes, bitter resins, and sometimes the rare trinket from lands far to the east. Local crafts prize dust-shedding finishes, tolerant mechanisms, and glazes that withstand abrasion.

### Routes East and Their Costs

South through Lu-An would be the easier grade, but it comes with harsher politics -- you cannot bypass the Hetmanate of Ulul-Azek, who will sometimes welcome you with parley, but more often with the point of their spears. North from Lu-Pto you could attempt to cross past the Merhal floodplains, but while the riverways run well east, the jungle eventually eats progress altogether. That means the Sifted Fields are the cleanest line -- at least in theory, if one turns a blind eye to the inherent dangers of the region. It is true that a correctly timed traverse could achieve contact with the far east while avoiding conflicts and vines both, and this idea alone is tempting enough to make princes, pilots, and smugglers attempt the folly of crossing the Fields again and again.
