## Geography and Climate 
Svenu stretches across a vast swath of southwestern Rimec-Suur, from the temperate valleys of its northern borderlands near Stranu to the taiga, tundra, and snowfields that edge into the southernmost reaches of the continent. Glaciers cling to the distant peaks of the Inner Ranges, and long fjords gouge the western coast, where the land meets the roaring Open Ocean. With no natural access to the Circle Sea, Svenu’s position isolates it from the great thalassocracies of the north, but grants it unchallenged dominion over the open waters, and its hardy sailors are the undisputed pioneers of deep-sea navigation.

The Wolves' Teeth Mountains, jagged and storm-battered, serve as a natural wall dividing Svenu from Lavion, enforcing a millennia-long cultural and political distance. Only a few treacherous passes link the two lands.

## People and Society
The Svenic people are tall, broad-shouldered, and commonly fair of hair and eye. Their endurance in harsh climates is matched by an inner stoicism and cultural pride, stemming from millennia of resistance to foreign incursion, from Anish and Remean colonial efforts to ill-fated crusades. Though often viewed as insular, Svenu's communities are tightly knit, shaped by the belief that survival depends on collective effort and clear generational bonds.

Svenic society is strongly matriarchal, not merely in political leadership, but in the deepest structures of law and identity. Children inherit names, property, and clan affiliation through their mothers. Lineage is recorded through long memory-chants sung in ritual halls, passed from grandmothers to daughters to granddaughters. Men are not excluded from public life, but rarely hold decision-making roles outside of their households, and may be fostered out in early adolescence to other clans or taken to sea for long years of service before marriage.

Naming Conventions thus follow a matronymic pattern: a woman named _Aestra, daughter of Elin_, would be _Aestra Elinsdottir_, while her son _Kael_ would be _Kael Aestrasson_ -- though his children would take the name of their mother again, as among Svenic folk, sons' names hold no legal standing for inheritance unless sanctioned by the matron of their hearth.

## Religion and Worldview
Svenu retains an ancient, animist folk religion untouched by the flames of Lurist zealotry, despite centuries of attempted missionary campaigns. Here, the Noble Elements are not abstract forces, but living presences woven into every facet of life: the sea is both bounty-giver and soul-keeper; the storm is destroyer and awakener; the cold is silent endurance and death. The names, locally, of the six Noble Elements are couched in terms of familial relations, recorded as **Sjarmodr** (Sea-Mother), **Daudisamma** (Granny Ice-Death), **Styrisysdr** (Sister Storm), **Arnvördr** (Hearthkeeper), **Sprotafadr** (Sproutfather), and **Jarnibrodr** (Iron Brother).

Temples, if one can call them that, are rarely more than shrines, located in places of interest to the elements -- a promontory over the surf, ringed megaliths, an ice-carved cave on a remote cliff. Yet their keepers hold quiet sway in local politics, and their authority is rarely challenged, even by nobles.

## Political and Economic Role
Though peripheral in Circle Sea politics, Svenu holds power in two key areas: deep-sea trade and naval exploration. Its northwestern coastal towns -- Jorrhalm, Skarnafirt, Rejaviik -- are home to expert shipbuilders who construct vessels not for elegance, but for resilience. Svenu-built high-runners, with their broad keels and flexible ribbing, are prized across Arlos for long-haul journeys through rough waters. Indeed Svenic captains led the earliest expeditions of the Open Ocean, long before the Circulean powers began to look past the Suurian Gate. 

Trade with the Circulean is modest, mostly overland through the Lupines or along the coast through Stranic ports, and often limited to rare timbers, ores from subarctic mines, smoked fish, and hard liquor.

### Relations with the Alvai  
Unlike other realms where Moonkin live as tolerated outsiders, Svenu is home to the proud and ancient Svartalfr, a distinct cultural kin-group. They are stockier than their cousins, often darker of skin and eye, and tend toward somber, meticulous expression. Most speak both Svenic and a dialect of Engelemair, the shared language of the Kin, though their oral histories claim a linguistic root even older.

According to their lore, the Svartalfr were never scattered -- they were set to guard the land by the will of the Wolf Mother, and never fell to the crusaders. Their homes are deep within the glacier-rifts and blackstone caves, and they keep rites no outsider has witnessed in full. Their tradition of axe-brides -- sworn warrior-women who renounce clan and comfort for a life of wandering and battle -- is both feared and revered, and known (often in romanticized versions) well beyond the ranges they call home.

Despite past tensions, relations between the Svenic and the Svartalfr are now characterized by complex mutual respect. Many noble houses pride themselves on having a Svartalfr retainer, bodyguard, or teacher.
