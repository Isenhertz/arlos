Profanity in Lavion follows a spectrum of severity tied to Lurist cosmology and the elemental worldview. Everyday speech contains mild outbursts that echo prayer or ritual, but harsher tongues stray into language that hints at absence, disorder, or even open inversion of sacred ideals. Thus is shaped what one may safely cry out in the kitchen or marketplace versus what can only be spat in a tavern brawl or shouted in angry rebellion against Light and Harmony.

---

#### 1. Exhorting Lurism or the Elements (light oaths, everyday)

These expressions are sharp but socially safe, akin to prayers blurted in frustration. They are common among maids, dockhands, and even polite company. They draw on the imagery of flame, ash, root, and creed without any true blasphemy.

- **"Lur’s Light!"**  
    Soft oath, mild surprise, often on the lips of servants and gentlefolk alike.
    
- **"Blight!"**  
    Clipped from _By the Light_, this exclamation is sharper and more exasperated than the simple exhortation, but only the strictest governess would rap a child's knuckles for it.
    
- **"By the Six!"**  
    An archaic invocation of the six elements, considered improper in pious settings, but respectable in everyday use.
    
- **"Untrisix!"**  
    Clipped from the creed _One is Three is Six is All_, often said with wonder or exasperation. More pious than _By the Six_, but still an oath.
    
- **"Roots and rot!"**  
    An earthy rustic curse, emphatic without being scandalous.
    
- **"Ashes!"**  
    A quick household cry, used to avoid a stronger oath, such as when accidentally hurting oneself.
    

Together these serve as the safe register of oaths, their sting softened by constant repetition in kitchens, market stalls, and farmsteads.

---

#### 2. Invoking the Absence of Light or Harmony (true profanity)

Here speech turns sharper, referencing extinguished light, broken resonance, or loss of sacred balance. Such words carry a sting and are common in taverns, marketplaces, or barracks. They still avoid direct blasphemy, but they are recognised as profane rather than harmless.

- **"Doused!"**  
    From _doused light_, a barked curse that can also be used as verb or intensifier.
    
- **"Liss!"**  
    Clipped from _lightless_, usually a curt and bitter exclamation. Stronger than _Ashes_.
    
- **"Cord!"**  
    Another clipped oath, short and cutting, from _discord_.
    
- **"Nance!"**  
    From _dissonance_, used as a dismissive insult, implying someone is out of place or outmatched. Its adjective form _nancy_ describes something or someone shoddy or gaudy, ill-suited to the context.
    
- **"Dim fool!"**  
    Implying a weak Light in someone’s soul is a strong curse, but not yet blasphemous. "Dim", generally speaking, holds the place "damn" holds in other faiths.
    

This level of profanity is the lifeblood of ordinary anger: sharp enough to sting, but not so outrageous as to bring censure from clergy or magistrates.

---

#### 3. Invoking the Inverse of Lurism (shocking blasphemy)

At this level sacred ideals are inverted or dragged through the gutter, often invoking darkness, the void, or the shattering of harmony. These are true blasphemies, shocking in polite speech and not uttered before clergy.

- **"Cack!"**
    
    From _cacophony_, guttural and ugly, equivalent to "shit" and used as intensifier.
    
- **"Darkness take you!"**
    
    A damning curse, outright banishment from the Light.
    
- **"Void!"**
    
    A short, annihilating oath, from the idea that the absence of Light is a bottomless emptiness; also in _voidborn_, akin to "hellspawn."
    
- **"Chorus oust you!"**
    
    A harsh curse condemning a soul to eternal disharmony, the severing of resonance itself. In harsher, clipped form this becomes _caust you_.
    

These words are the verbal equivalent of rebellion, reserved for moments of rage or despair, and they draw shocked silence if uttered in the hearing of the pious.

---

#### Everyday Elemental Appeals (rustic invocations)

Outside of profanity, rustic Lavienese speech is rich with appeals to the Noble Elements, half‑prayers and half‑exclamations. Though clergy frown on such invocations, they remain deeply rooted in common speech, especially in the countryside and at sea.

Examples:

- **"Piatros grant me strength!"**
    
    Common among laborers and farmers, and figuratively used by any beleaguered or disgruntled individual.
    
- **"Empress preserve us!"**
    
    A sailor’s cry of despair, calling on the Empress of Fathoms for mercy in storm or wreck.
    
- **"Heart of Ice!"**
    
    An invocation of Anapagonia, asking for cold resolve in grief or hardship.
    
- **"Send a gale!"**
    
    A bid for swiftness. Also invoked in bidding someone farewell, manifesting a sailor's hope for favorable wind.
    
- **"Under the Worldroot's shelter."**
    
    A rustic blessing and promise of hospitality.
    
- **"Pyre spare this hearth!"**
    
    A desperate prayer that the Prince of Pyres avert his wrath, often wailed when fires already rage.
    

These appeals reveal how ordinary folk live with the Elements as neighbors and dangers both, and they mark rustic or seafaring speech as distinct from the carefully orthodox oaths of city and court.
