### Overview

In the so-called Rivian Epoch -- approximately the thousand years before Reme's rise -- a succession of sorcerer-kings dominated the lowlands of Riv-î-Tep, centered on the Merhal Floodplain along the eastern Circle Sea littoral. Their polities were anchored in temple-cities engineered as resonance machines, inaugurating with the First-and-Only and continuing through his Successors to the later Lesser Kings. Across centuries these regimes weaponized hydrology, acoustics, and coerced bodies -- notably the _Hatekin_ drawn from the Alvai Kin of the Night Jaguar -- to extract labor and impose order. Later fragments tell of an end by absorption rather than apocalypse: after -1024 AR, the rising Remean power advanced eastward along the Gyre, domesticating or dismantling resonant infrastructures, taxing temple economies, scattering the _Hatekin_, and converting miracle into maintenance.

### The First-and-Only

**Emôn-Dju-Bâ-Mhâhat-Nmu First-and-Only** rose in the shadow of prehistory, but his reign marks the beginning of what scholars now term Antiquity. Made powerful and immortal by means unknown (likely lost arts of massed harmonic choirs of blood-bound slaves, effigial sacrifices and architectural resonance of a grand scale not attempted since), he ruled for centuries until he was usurped and toppled by Set-Ahotep-Imekh Breaks-The-Crown in -1410 AR. More sorcerer-kings followed, often competing and displacing one another, until the rise of Remean society in -1024 AR.

The First-and-Only ruled from a city known only from fragments of surviving vassal states as **Pta-Amon-Mhâhat Riv-î-Tep Adjeh**, believed to now lie under the silts of the Mer-Hal delta. His court gave the entire region its toponym -- Riv-î-Tep -- that remains in use even today, but all surviving accounts agree that it was a theatre of terror: ritual combats, blood harmonics, and forced rites of binding shaped the population into instruments of his power. The **Alvai Kin of the Night Jaguar** were enslaved and corrupted under his reign, becoming the feared _Hatekin_ -- a lesson in the brutal utility of shaping not just the elements, but the very souls of beings.

Many surviving cities in the Merhal plains owe their founding mythos to the First-and-Only, or one of his many successors, all of whom attempted and failed to surpass his grandeur.

### The Two Successors

**Set-Ahotep-Imekh Breaks-The-Crown** (r. -1410 to -1270 AR). Lover, lieutenant, and executioner of the First-and-Only, he shattered his inviolable rule in a rite that cracked the sky. The usurper expanded the grand choirs of blood with machinic architectures of resonance: mile-deep shafts sunk into the ground and latticed with copper-plated bones, terraced step-fields where irrigation pulsed by harmonic cadence, and causeways of fused basalt binding vassal cities to his court. He kept the _Hatekin_ as his terror-guard, but collared them in electrum masks engraved with nulling sigils. In the end he was undone in the intimacy of his own sanctum, toppled by the one he had raised closest.

**Fa-Hê-Rhet-Bâ-Estu She-With-Skin-of-Silver** (r. -1270 to -1219 AR). The silver was not metaphor but artifice: a skin of quick-forged electrum impressed with water-lines and moon-seals, a body made mirror. She called herself Sorcerer-King in the manner of the age and tolerated no lesser styling. Her rites prized reflection and recursion -- mirror-galleries that sang back a single note in thousandfold, canals tiled in polished obsidian, and cohorts of eunuch adjudicators whose faces were plates of bright metal. In her time, the court drifted between river-capitals whose names the present only half remembers -- Tla-ê-Amah, Il-î-Amah, and Sip-ê-Apah among them -- each tuned to her itinerant pageant. In her fifty-first regnal year she was overthrown by three of her sworn lesser lords, who parceled her silver into temple weights and divided her domains.

### The Lesser Kings

**The Triumvirate of Ash-Reeds** (collective rule, -1219 to -1186 AR). Their names are given variously in broken stelae and clay tallies: **Djeser-Khet-Emun Taker-of-Names**, **Ras-Pta-Kelm Drinks-the-River**, and **Nehes-Amon-Dju Carries-the-Scales**. Functionaries of Fa-Hê-Rhet-Bâ-Estu's Wandering Court, they coveted the realm they governed in her name, and swore a pact to overthrow her and succeeded through subterfuge and perniciousness. Each then sought to crown himself First-and-Only-Again; each bled the others by famine and war. Their concord finally shattered outright in a night of masked killings; when dawn came the river-barques bore three gilded corpses and a hundred claimants.

From the ruins of their compact began the long dusk of the **Lesser Kings** -- sorcerer-kings in name if not in magnitude -- whose reigns overlapped, collided, and wore the people thin. What follows is the accepted chronology of the principal lines; dozens more flared and guttered in the backwaters.

**In-Khun-Emet Kills-With-a-Glance** (Agd-ê-Ynem; r. c. -1186 to -1151 AR). A cult of the eye: opal lenses grown in brine, watch-towers sheathed in glassy stone, and the **Hall of Unseen Judgments** where defendants were tried behind a curtain of shimmering heat. His gaze-stones ate names from ledgers; rebellion came blindfolded and victorious.

**Anh-Met-Ren Who-Walks-in-Fire** (Tla-î-Xol; r. c. -1168 to -1132 AR, overlaps with In-Khun-Emet). He paved the salt-flats with vitrified tongues of lava and drove heat through reed-kilns to fire a city of red brick. The **Basalt Causeways of Xol** still vein the wastes; his end came when a drought called his fires hollow.

**Set-Ukhet-Ra Feeds-the-Serpents** (Sar-ê-Adat; r. c. -1149 to -1110 AR). He engineered serpent-canals whose silt-baskets moved by living coils, binding Water to Soil in a harness of Cohesion. Flood and famine were meted in baskets and bites; a spring flood unseated him and drowned his priesthood.

**Ba-Dju-Nemu Sings-to-the-Ribs-of-Earth** (Omt-î-Thes; r. c. -1130 to -1096 AR). He hollowed amphitheatres into the bedrock and tuned them to the planet's deep hum, raising grain-yields by song. Earth-tremors answered his choir with contempt, cracking his vaults and swallowing his singers.

**Pat-Es-Kheru Keeper-of-Empty Thrones** (Tla-ê-Ysih; r. c. -1122 to -1084 AR). Master of effigial sovereignty, he ruled through wax-kings moved by copper threads and spoke law through a chorus of reed-dolls. When fire took the puppet-house, the people found the strings led nowhere and the empty dais awaited a beating heart.

**Ashet-Namu Hewer-of-Masks** (Il-î-Amah; r. c. -1090 to -1065 AR). She -- styled Sorcerer-King nonetheless -- carved persona-masks from river-bone that lent borrowed virtues to wearers. A plague of mirrored fevers ran the streets; the infected saw ten faces in every pool. Her masks were heaped in pits and brined to silence.

**Xet-Ophet-Rem Weighs-the-Flood** (Sip-ê-Apah; r. c. -1079 to -1044 AR). An accountant of waters, he raised the **Gates of Cohesion**, titanic weirs counter-weighted by stone-lungs that inhaled and exhaled the river. Under him, bread was plenty and borders hard. Age unmade him; his heirs fought over keys and counterweights.

**Khem-Bâ-Res Splits-the-Day-from-Night** (Tla-ê-Amah; r. c. -1052 to -1011 AR). He yoked twin cults of sun and dark to a single calendar of labor, cutting the year into bright and shadow spans. His **Bifacial Pyramid** cast noon-shade like an eclipse; assassins came at the hinge-day when rites were most taut.

**Mhar-Tep-Iyen Eats-the-Names** (Agd-ê-Ynem; r. c. -1030 to -1005 AR). A unifier by ledger and doom, he consumed ancestral lists in blue fire and issued new rolls bound to his person. Border wars with Xol and Ysih bled his granaries; when western banners rose from Ertra, his scribes could not write victories fast enough to make them true.

**Shen-Uru-Atum Binds-the-Reeds** (Mer-Hal Delta; r. c. -1008 to -992 AR). Last of note among the Lesser Kings, he tried to knit the lowlands with living dykes of woven reed and clay, seeded with sprites of Water and Soil to self-heal cuts. Reme's legions -- new-forged in Ertra after -1024 AR -- marched with engineers and iron discipline. They cut the living dykes with stone saws tuned to Tension, burned the reed-sprites with dry salt, and took the delta city by city. Shen-Uru's barge was found beached and empty below a sky of black birds that were not birds.

By the turn of the 11th century before Reme's count, the Merhal breadbasket lay under Remean assessment. The Hatekin were scattered into the jungles of the north and east, the temple-cities paid tithe in grain and labor, and the grand architectures of resonance were repurposed into mundane works: weirs made mills, obelisks became milestones, and choir-pits echoed with market shouts. Thus ended the Rivian Epoch -- not in a single fall, but in a long unmaking where empire proved more patient than sorcery.
