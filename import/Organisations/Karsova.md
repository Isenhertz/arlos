East of Varngard the river takes over the horizon. Here the Swicca is the road, the granary, and the law. Karsova rose from settled steppe hosts who wintered on levee crests and summered their herds on piedmont grass, then learned patience and put plough to silt. Early rulers were riding-lords with felt courts; later they were canal-builders who yoked distributaries with cutoffs and pounded marsh into paddies. From the Wolves' Teeth to the Deserted Sea, Karsova hugs the river's back like a rider who knows his mount can throw him at any bend.

The capital, Karsograd, stands where the old main arm divides for the delta -- a levee city of timbered longhouses grown into clay-brick courts, tied together by canal and ferry-chain. Upstream cities like Novolut and Dravopol sit on older river belts that still run close enough to feed their mills in good years. Downstream, Zarech on the reed edge keeps an eye on the Deserted Sea and the salt caravans that circle its shrinking shores. Settlements beyond the levees are earth-and-timber forts.

Karsova's state is at once a march and a host. Power rests with the Zadrugas, an assembly of household elders and captains who elect a Woyod for twelve-year terms and a Hetman of the Riding for war. The country is divided into districts of a gross houses called upon to raise cavalry and scullers, grouped into dozens for muster. When the Eternal Dominion marched upriver generations ago, Karsova rode and rafted, trading ground for time until the winter river froze their foes to a standstill; the victorious "Charge across the Swicca" that turned the tide and drove the crusaders out is a household tale, eternally retold. The banners here carry gryphoid emblems and the old riding-knots of the steppe, now stitched in river-blue.

Faith is stubborn and many-voiced. Lurism does hold the main churches, but the riding shrines still pour libations for Maregina in her aspect as the River-Mother, and leave wreaths for the Lord of Loam in poplar groves along the old belts. Moonkin Alvai enclaves lie upriver at Novolut and in the Glassers' Lane in Karsograd; they gave the march its best lens-grinders and levee surveyors and keep their own night-watches on the flood stairs. Karsova still bears scars from Lu-An riders and from crusader burnings; in answer the zadrugas keep proof-plates and charters in their household vaults rather than a central archive, and each district records its births and deaths in a common book.

Economy rides the flood. In high years the Swicca lays a skin of silt that feeds flax, rye, and millet; in low ones Karsova sells salt, fish, and reed to make up the lack. When the river jumps, Karsova throws men and wattles at the breach, then sues for peace with the water if it will not be moved. Trade with Varngard is a necessity and a rivalry -- Karsova hates to pay portage tolls, Varngard hates to see Karsova saddle up.

Karsovan names tilt Slavic with a Gothic root: men called Dragor, Velesovo, Radokar, Barsomir; women called Mavala, Radokara, Vesinya, Havryna. Place-names run heavy and sibilant: Karsograd, Novolut, Dravopol, Zarech, Hristanya.
