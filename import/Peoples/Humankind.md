### **Anish**

**Homeland:** An-Suur, Coastal Sarm,
**Traits:** Olive-brown skin, dark eyes, dark curly or wavy hair  
**Language Family:** Aniric branch of Suurese (itself a descendant of Ancient Aniric)  
**Religion:** Strong pre-Lurist elemental cults, later subsumed into Lurism  
**Legacy:** Shipwrights, merchants, chroniclers

The Anish were the first great seafarers of the Circle Sea, long before the rise of Reme or Lavion. They carved their city-states into the jungle coast of An-Suur and the wind-polished shores of  Sarm, and traded everything from goldleaf papyrus to smoked lizardmeat across the known world. Their influence on agriculture and sea-faring traditions is profound, and they are often credited with refining the precursor to the modern alphabet.

Physically, Anish people tend toward warm brown complexions, dark hair, and sharp, fine features. Centuries of trade along the Gyres have left them with a spectrum from very dark to almost olive-fair, but the "classic" Anish look, as the sort of person who writes travelogues imagines it, is tawny skin with black hair worn in careful braids or coils, and dark eyes that do not miss much.

Culturally they are the archetypal coastal middlemen. Even before Lur or Reme, the Anish had an old tradition of mercantile city-states -- walled, argumentative, religiously serious, and utterly shameless about profit -- stringing harbors along every tolerable anchorage, swapping grain, dyes, glassware, and gossip. When the Theocracy of the Eternal Dominion rose in An-Mirda, it rose largely on Anish shoulders and in Anish tongue.

Though much of their ancient sovereignty was eventually subsumed into the early Lurist theocracies, Anish culture persisted through coastal customs, salt-dyed textiles, and lyrical mourning rites. Their descendants in modern An-Suur still prize sharp minds and sharper tongues, especially in the realms of poetry, law, and long-term debt.

Outside peoples like to describe them as grasping, legalistic, and slippery. The Anish describe themselves as prudent, pious, and prepared, which is a polite way of saying "better at this than you." They are the template Circuleans reach for when they picture a trader with an eye for currents both literal and political.

### **Rivian**

**Homeland:** Merhal, Lu-Pto, Kiriyan periphery
**Traits:** Merese: very dark-skinned (deep brown to black), hair tightly curled, often worn short or in intricate, practical braids; Halic: skin a rich brown rather than dark; features sharper and narrower; hair varied (curled to looser coils)
**Language Family:** Tepic (isolate)  
**Religion:** Once shaped by Elemental Invocation, then sorcerous god-kings; now fractured under Lurist rule  
**Legacy:** Monumental resonant architecture, sanguinary modulation, priest-kings

Rivian is not itself an ethnic group, but a regional grouping, for it contains the two ethnicities that have defined the Merhal floodplains since prehistory: the Merese, and the Halic.

Merese people are typically very dark-skinned, with a range from deep brown to almost black; hair tends to be tightly curled, often worn short or in intricate, practical braids suited to a humid climate. Bodies seem built for work in water: strong legs, broad shoulders, a certain habitual ease moving through mud, boats, and flooded streets.

Halic people share much ancestry with the Merese but have a subtly different look. Skin is usually a rich brown rather than very dark; features trend sharper and narrower; hair is varied, from tightly curled to looser coils, and is more often worn in elaborate styles signifying household and craft. 

Historically, Rivian polities were the backbone of the ancient Sorcerer-Kings' domains, and later crucial subjects of Remean logistics. That legacy shows in a strong tradition of hydraulic engineering and communal labour, a reputation for bureaucracy, scribal learning, and a slightly smug sense that if civilisation were a race, they had the longest head start by far.

Religiously, Rivians are devout Lurists who nevertheless behave as if Maregina is always listening. River rites are slightly more elaborate than the Orthodoxy officially requires: processions on boats, lanterns set adrift, offerings at confluence-shrines that are "strictly symbolic, of course." Their calendar quietly bends Lurist feasts to the Hal's reliable flood pulse rather than the other way around.

By outsiders -- the majority of which consider themselves maritime -- the Rivians are considered "river folk": patient, communal, endlessly fussy about flood calendars, and inclined to treat anyone who cannot read a tide as basically a child.

The Rivian peoples built the stepped temples and towering ziggurats under the Sorcerer-Kings and their twisted priesthoods. Their artisans sculpted stone to sing, and their scribes etched memory into gold, but after the rise of Lurism, much of the ancient knowledge was destroyed or declared heretical. Nonetheless, their descendants retain a dignified pride and speak in a melodic language isolate called Tepic. Today, the majority live as their ancestors did, following the rhythms of the floodplain confluence of the mighty Mer and Hal rivers, but many live as minorities anywhere the water touches.

### **Remean**

**Homeland:** Ancient Reme and its colonial periphery  
**Traits:** Pale to coppery skin, dark or auburn hair, sharp profiles, universally gynoid
**Language Family:** Old Remean (extinct)
**Religion:** Civic polytheism that matured into a codified formalism of Shaping -- later condemned as blasphemy by Lurism  
**Legacy:** Maritime empire, law codes, philosophy, metrology, calendar reform, canons of aetheric Shaping

Reme began as a constellation of matriarchal tribes turned city-states on the volcanic eastern coast of Ertra, then knit itself into a thalassocracy built on bonded harbors, lighthouse chains, and treaty ports. Its influence spread not only by trireme and tribute, but by the prestige of its tools -- law, rhetoric, and the aetheric Shaping of the body into pleasing gynoid proportions with near superhuman capabilities.

The Remeans were unmatched at sea, and the greatest rivals of the rising Lurist dominion of An-Suur, though they disregarded the western gyre as a backwater until the crusader fleets were already at their doorstep. In the empire's final crisis, desperate magistrates attempted to bind the Noble Element of Fire; the Father of Flame answered with cataclysm, entombing the capital beneath ash and obsidian. Lurist chroniclers took this as proof of hubris; modern scholars treat it as the hinge of Circulean history.

In the aftermath, their society declared idolatrous and corrupting, Remean refugees faced persecution, flight, and uneasy absorption into the new developing order. How the Remeans are remembered varies by shore. In Lavion they are a cautionary tale with useful footnotes; in An-Suur, a sermon on the inevitability of the Light's triumph; in Ertra and among certain scholars of the Grand Academy, a brilliant, dangerous ancestry whose mathematics and structural resonance still repay study -- provided one keeps well clear of the proscribed ideas of bodily transformation.

### **Ukili'ata**

^4b8a91

**Homeland:** Rim of Fire, now dispersed; migratory; certain islands
**Traits:** Olive or copper-toned skin, light brown, blond, or silvery hair, wide-set almond eyes  
**Language Family:** Ukili'akima (isolate)  
**Religion:** Moon-reverence and ancestral invocation; later absorbed some Lurist elements  
**Legacy:** Calendar innovation, oral epics, navigational mastery, secrets of the hydrofoil

The Ukili'ata are older than the Remeans and the Anish -- or so their songs claim. Migratory in nature, they came to the Circulean from across the Open Ocean, guided by the rhythms of the moon Belana and the stars they alone named. As a ship-bound migratory people, their influence is seen in the beliefs and bloodlines of coastal peoples and the names of landmarks all around the Circle Sea.

Despite centuries of assimilation (to the degree that some lineages are nobles of respectable pedigree), they retain their oral traditions and ritually hold true to the _Belanar Calendar_ in spite of Lurist evangelism, making them a distinct people. The Ukili'ata are sometimes seen as uncanny, even eerie -- their pale hair and eyes giving them a ghostly air that contrasts with the often deeply tanned color of their skin.

### **Svenish**

**Homeland:** Svenu, Varngard, Southlands beyond the Wolves' Teeth Mountains
**Traits:** Fair to ruddy skin, pale or coppery hair, light eyes, strong builds, wide jaws  
**Language Family:** Svenish branch of Western Suurese  
**Religion:** Matriarchal animism; minimal Lurist presence  
**Legacy:** Matrilineal clan structure, ironworking, shipcraft, layered storytelling

The Svenish peoples are rugged survivors, shaped by fjord, forest, and snow: tall, broad-shouldered, and often fair of hair and eye. Pale skin is common, but weather darkens it quickly; dark hair and eyes are not rare, thanks to centuries of quiet intermarriage with stray northerners who made the unfortunate choice to fall in love with a blonde powerhouse.

Their society is thoroughly matriarchal. Names, property, and clan pass through women; the legal spine of a household is the matron, as well as her mother and sisters. Men do a great deal of the visible dangerous work -- fishing, raiding, sailing the long voyages -- and then hand their earnings back to the women who actually decide what happens with them.

Svenish folk are as likely to be seen on a merchant ship as in a dueling ring, and they have a reputation for hospitality and bluntness in equal measure. While some consider them provincial, the educated know the Svenish clans maintain extensive oral archives and have developed some of the finest steelcraft, learned from the Svartalfr that live in the mountains. In the Circulean imagination, Svenish are loud but reliable, startlingly good shipwrights, and as jovial as they are terrifying. Their opinion of everyone else is that northerners whine a lot about weather and cannot tie a proper knot.

### **Rimecian**

**Homeland:** Lavion and its dominions, Varngard, Karsova
**Traits:** Mixed -- often tawny skin, dark hair, hazel or green eyes, sharp but rounded features  
**Language Family:** Virellic (which the Learned called Middle Suurese)
**Religion:** Mainstream Lurism
**Legacy:** Feudalism, courtly traditions, centralised statecraft, early aetheric codification

"Rimecian" is not a tribal name so much as a historian's shrug. It covers the descendants of old Anish littoral colonies, Remean provincial towns and army camps, and waves of Svenish migration over the passes and along the coasts. The citizens of Lavion and a dozen lesser polities all sit inside this broad ethnic envelope, with local distinctions of dialect, law, and snobbery.

Physically, Rimecians are extremely variable. Even in Lavion proper you get everything from pale, light-eyed types reflecting Svenish and highland ancestry, to darker, Anish-leaning faces in old port quarters; common to all is their generally medium height, and a tendency to freckles in the west and darker complexions closer to the Merhal trade routes.

Rimecian nobles are often obsessed with proving bloodlines, yet rarely agree on what constitutes a pure one. The common folk speak a flowing dialect of the Suurese family full of borrowed words with roots in Old Remean.

### **Stranic**

**Homeland:** Stranu, the west of Lavion  
**Traits:** High variability -- typically freckled tan skin, wavy dark-blond hair, gray or pale green eyes  
**Language Family:** Stranic branch of Western Suurese
**Religion:** Lurist influence on top of older spirit practices  
**Legacy:** Republican thought, charter towns, egalitarian streak

Stranic folk are what happens when you leave Anish merchants and Svenish adventurers around the same harbors for long enough and fail to chaperone them properly. Physically they have a very wide range of olive-to-ruddy complexions, hair in all shades of brown, but a high incidence of light eyes thanks to the Svenish contribution.

Culturally, Stranu has been a kind of perpetual hinge: Rim-wise seamanship, winter-rain agriculture, hills terrace-farmed and coasts busy with lagoons, vines, and olives. The Stranic identity coalesced around shared maritime habits and legal norms rather than any one ancestry. Their city councils are chatty, quarrelsome, and eerily efficient; guilds and small republics are common.

During the Age of Corsairs, Stranic merchant interests were instrumental in financing the exploration of the high seas beyond the Suurian Gate. Their proud heritage makes them unusually resistant to orthodoxy, and many quietly practice reverence to Maregina and Morwynt in the manner of the Concordian rites that characterize Western Lurism.

### **Ertrese**

**Homeland:** The Ertran triangle between Fiurisci, Balboa, and the Gulf of Lu-An
**Traits:** Often copper-skinned with blue or green eyes, dark curly hair, aquiline noses  
**Language Family:** Balboan (Eastern Suurese in the parlance of academics)
**Religion:** Highly syncretic; Harmonism is the predominant school of Lurism here, and eclectic personal worship persists in practice  
**Legacy:** Seafaring, political cunning, cultural innovation, queer openness

Ertrese ancestry braids three major threads: Anish coastal colonies, Halic and Merese migrants following wealth and grain routes across the Rush to Ertra's shores, and what remained of the settlers and garrisons of Reme. Later Rimecian or Ukili'ata influences sit on top of this, but ethnically the base mix is those three.

The result is a very typical Circulean look: medium to dark complexions, hair from tight curls to straight, a wide range of eye colours, and a general air of having seen too much sea and too many tax assessors.

Culturally, the Ertrese are coastal pastoralists whose marriage to the sea was less the necessity of the isolated Anish communities than technical and mercantile ambition. Ertrese clothing favors opulence, speech favors double meaning, and politics favors the knife behind the fan. Yet theirs is also a land where the faith and sciences intertwine in Harmonist teachings, where sapphic love may be honored in ballads rather than be smothered by scandal (a fact disgruntled detractors claim is a forbidden echo of the Remeans' scrambling of the Triune Family), and where a commoner will rise through wit and service, not the lineage of his name.

### **Azek**

**Homeland:** Korvosa, Lu-An
**Traits:** Tawny skin, almond eyes, shockingly frequent red shades of hair  
**Language Family:** Azeki
**Religion:** Nominally under Lurist guidance; practically elemental invocation, sky burials, and heathen ancestry worship
**Legacy:** Horsemanship, husbandry, and cavalry tactics

Azeki have the build of people who live in the saddle: compact, tough, with strong legs and lower backs, against a background of great internal variation. Steppe genetics are democratic; you get broad faces and epicanthic folds, straight or hooked noses, light eyes, dark eyes, skin from wheat-gold to deep brown, depending on which caravan or raid your ancestors got swept up in. Notable as distinctively Azekian is the prevalence of red hair among them, a trait not even the Svenic exhibit this often.

Socially they are organised around clans and war-bands, structured by oath circles and seasonal courts. The old raider habit is to value mobility over masonry -- power is what rides with the Hetman, not what sits in his city -- though by the 15th century AR plenty of Azek elites have settled into towns, married into the river folk of Karsova, and discovered the subtle joys of owning other people's granaries.

Their historical fame is the Azek momentum of the centuries in which their confederacies dominated Lu-An's interior, alternately raiding and escorting caravans, and supplying mercenary cavalry up and down the Eastern Gyre.

Religion among the Azekians is officially Lurist via the Autonomous Church of the Eastern Gyre, but steppe piety likes saints who approve of horse theft and long marches. Sky rites and ancestor fires sit comfortably next to the creed.

The Circulean shorthand for them is predictably "horse barbarians." The Azekian shorthand for everyone else is "people who sleep indoors because they fear the sky."

### **Kiriyan**

**Homeland:** Vel Kiriya, Merhal
**Traits:** Skin bronze to brown, strong cheekbones, narrow eyes, barrel chests; hair dark, thick, wavy
**Language Family:** Kiriyan
**Religion:** Kharunite Lurism
**Legacy:** Imperial infrastructure, centralized messenger services, religious tolerance

Kiriyans carry the look of people carved by altitude and sun. Skin tones range from rich bronze to deep brown; faces often have strong cheekbones and narrow eyes accustomed to glare off rock and cloud. In the highest districts, generations of high-altitude living show in barrel chests and the easy way even children hop up steps that leave lowlanders wheezing. Hair is usually dark, thick, and straight or wavy, worn in plaits or coiled under layered headcloths.

Their world is vertical. Fields are terraces rung up and down the mountainsides, stitched with stone walls and cunning little channels that carry meltwater or diverted streams with almost obsessive precision. Roads cling to slopes, cross gorges on dizzying bridges, and zigzag up escarpments in a style politely described as "economical." The same instinct that forces grain out of cliffs also builds multi-tiered cities on spurs and ridges, with civic plazas perched like eagles' nests above cloud and chasm.

Politically, Kiriyan identity is tied to Vel Kiriya's imperial habit. People think of themselves first by family, clan, terrace or town, then satrapy, and only then by the wider name of Kiriyan. The King of Kings in Praxhar Vel rules through satraps who are part governor, part tax farmer, part hereditary rulers. Beneath them, an intricate mesh of clan elders, terrace councils, priestly colleges, and professional guilds administer daily life. It all works because the Kiriyans have trained for millennia in the noble art of keeping meticulous records and then arguing about them.

Their empire is not coastal but riverine and vertical, from the shallow-draft fleets of the upper Mer to the caravans grind up and down the plateau roads, trading jungle produce and lowland grain for highland wool, metals, and the sort of precision-engineered tools that makes foreign envoys quietly revise their invasion plans. Long barred from beasts of burden larger (or quicker) than a mule, their long-distance runners and relay messengers are a point of pride: a properly trained courier can carry a waxed packet across three valleys and two ridges in the time it takes a Lavienese clerk to find the correct form.

Socially, Kiriyan culture expects discipline. Terraces must be maintained, channels cleared, trails kept open, and if you neglect any of these things the mountain will kill you in assorted creative ways. Labour is communal and scheduled to the last stone; festivals are lavish but timed around planting, flooding, and harvest as strictly as any Merhal levy calendar. Lineage is reckoned through both blood and terrace-rights: a family is as much the land it has the duty to keep from sliding downhill as it is its grandparents' names.

Religiously, Vel Kiriya is formally Lurist, but in its Kharunite flavor. Lur is honoured as the "Light in All", the principle that holds the cosmos in order; the elements, noble and common alike, are venerated as readily as the many saints of the Church. Some of the hymns sound suspiciously like older invocations.

To the Circulean lowlands, the Kiriyans are polite, inscrutable, excellent engineers, unnervingly calm about heights, and entirely too relaxed about the idea of marching armies over passes that coastal generals call impassable. Kiriyans, for their part, tend to think of the Circle Sea peoples as clever but slightly soggy, very excitable about ships, and distressingly unconcerned with the basic question of what happens when your hillsides fall into your rivers.

### **Ptolean**

**Homeland:** Lu-Pto
**Traits:** Medium to dark brown, wiry, high cheekbones and hawk noses, hair often tightly curled or wavy
**Language Family:** Ptolean
**Religion:** Lurism, with a strong local mystical bent
**Legacy:** Gliders, oasis agriculture, pathways across the Sifted Fields into the far east

Ptoleans are, by necessity, an oasis people. Settlements cling to seeps, wells, and palm-gardens; wider "tribal territories" are really patterns of remembered routes between water sources. Some Ptoleans are settled farmers and gardeners, coaxing crops out of grudging soils with careful irrigation; others are semi-nomadic herders, moving flocks between seasonal pastures and using oases as anchor points.

Ptolean culture prizes endurance, memory, and tact. Routes, hospitality customs, and feud histories are held in long recitations and taught as carefully as prayers. Caravan trade to Riv-î-Tep and the Circulean ports has been their lifeblood for centuries, so they live in a state of uneasy mutual dependence with both riverine and coastal powers.

Religiously, Ptoleans sit under the Autonomous Church of the Eastern Gyre and the Merhal's wider influence, so they are notionally orthodox Lurists. In practice, their rites lean mystical and practical: invocations for safe wells, breath-songs in the old style, and a certain reverence for the spirits of basins and dust storms that the Ordinate of the Flame pretends not to notice.

Stereotypically, Circuleans see Ptoleans as taciturn, honour-bound, and alarmingly good at spotting a mirage where outsiders see a lake.
